# Editorial policy

Version 1.1, 28 October 2026. This policy governs the grammar-led course and its presentation revisions.

## Audience and presentation

Assume no Old English; assume familiarity with standard grammatical terminology. The user's own experience does not count as a prerequisite. A terminology appendix may be added later.

Use compact rules, full paradigms, short examples, vocabulary, and substantial practice. Follow the economy of Thackston and the practical course organization of Lambdin's Gothic book. Omit study coaching, motivational framing, and repeated elementary explanations. Put source citations and editorial commentary in section footnotes or endnotes.

Use consistent paradigm tables: person and number down the left for verbs; Nominative, Genitive, Dative, Accusative down the left for declensions. Put tense or number across the top as appropriate, with Masculine, Neuter, Feminine in that order. Combine identical verbal plural rows as **1–3 pl.** while preserving their complete logical coverage. Keep distinct forms, scope restrictions, pronouns and numbered exercise responses separate. Tables use Cambria 10.5-point type, bold headings, upright forms and horizontal rules without vertical rules; split wide tables into readable panels.

## Grammar units

Choose chapter boundaries by coherent grammatical systems. A chapter may combine several Cook exercises and span several study sessions. Once introduced, a declension should be presented in all its singular and plural cases, with characteristic alternations and exceptions. Define the complete verbal paradigm covered by each verb unit. Do not divide a declension merely to match Cook's exercise boundaries.

Complete paradigms must remain graded: distinguish the forms and ordinary uses taught now from later constructions. Teach and practise the basic uses of newly presented cases. Add original tasks when Cook does not exercise the full paradigm; label these as new material. The first noun unit will need accusative/dative practice in addition to Cook's nominative/genitive phrases.

Use the grammar to plan the exercise sequence, retaining original source IDs and prompts. Group complete sets where feasible; redistribute mixed material only with an explicit crosswalk and rationale. Account for every audited item through assignment, deferral, or exclusion with reasons. No numerical vocabulary ceiling governs the new course.

## Sources, spelling, and corrections

Use Cook's 1895 *Exercises in Old English* and the 1895 second edition of *A First Book in Old English*, with the registry's selected scans. Cook's edited prose spelling is the starting baseline; his preface describes Early West Saxon normalization and excepts poetry. Do not silently convert the text to another dialect or spelling convention.

Preserve faithful source transcriptions. Check letters, macrons, references, and uncertain readings against images. Use UTF-8/NFC. Any normalization or adaptation must be reversible and documented. Historical explanations may clarify a pattern but are not a separate required historical-grammar course.

Evidence-backed corrections are authorized. Preserve the original, state the replacement or corrected analysis, give the evidence and exact affected items, and distinguish an error from a valid variant. Carry forward correction C01, **wǣstm → wæstm**, where relevant; its checked evidence is indexed in `sources/CORRECTIONS.md`.

Lambdin supplies a structural model, not Old English linguistic authority. The checked contents and publisher description are linked in `docs/DECISIONS.md`; the full book has not been supplied or audited here. Write original exposition.

## Vocabulary and cumulative knowledge

The unit determines vocabulary size. Larger lists are acceptable. Give verified headwords, gender or verb class, specific meanings, and the diagnostic forms needed to use the full paradigms. Keep assigned vocabulary distinct from supplied reading help.

Track lexical senses and inflected forms separately, with recognition, production, and supplied-exposure milestones. Track constructions precisely. Reuse checked source evidence where applicable, checking any new sense, form, or construction. The active contracts establish teaching milestones.

Independent exercises require previously taught or newly taught productive knowledge. Reading glosses can support harder material but do not silently promote it to learned status. A familiar lemma does not establish every inflection or meaning.

## Readings

Aim for connected passages with a coherent beginning and end. Select length by the actual grammatical and lexical demands, not a fixed word quota. Introduce enough verbal and syntactic groundwork early for useful reading; fuller noun tables alone do not solve finite verbs, passives, wishes, or subordinate clauses.

Prefer checked original extracts with explicit local support. A controlled adaptation is permissible when its source and changes are recorded. Label fragments accurately and new compositions plainly. Supply the actual difficult forms and constructions, not just dictionary headwords. Prepare comprehension, selective analysis, and translation tasks before the full key.

## Keys, review, and typesetting

Keep the key separate. Use compact answers and shared explanations, with target construction, essential meaning, valid alternatives, and relevant errors. Do not reject a grammatical translation merely for differing from a model string. Prepare independent translations from the edition's wording.

Use independent source and curriculum analysis before drafting, then independent solving and review. Mark no chapter reviewed while material errors remain. Keep uncertainties and evidence limits explicit.

Markdown remains the editable master. LuaLaTeX produces complete learner and answer-key PDFs, including every vocabulary row, exercise, reading support, and source note. Use the shared Cambria layout and 165 × 241 mm trim size. Inspect every new rendered page, including special characters and tables. Keep completed chapters as local Git revisions; publishing is a separate decision.
