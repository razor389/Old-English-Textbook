# Correction evidence

## C01: wǣstm → wæstm

The course corrects Cook's long-vowel **wǣstm** to short-vowel **wæstm**, and consequently uses **wæstmas**. Preserve the source spelling in transcriptions. Apply the correction in learner text where this lemma occurs, citing it in a section note and recording the affected items.

Evidence: [Baker's glossary, wæstm](https://glossary.oldenglishaerobics.net/oeaginfo.php?objid=xwaestm&objtype=gentry), and [Stenroos (2024), p.118, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.17811/selim.29.2024.109-25), whose [page image](corrections/c01/stenroos-2024-p118.png) explicitly places **wæstm** among lexemes with Old English short /æ/. The [correction record](corrections/c01/README.md) identifies the retained primary witnesses and the limits of the change.

The active contracts establish teaching milestones. Valid gender or inflectional alternatives should not be called corrections merely because another form is selected for the course. New corrections need their own source record and evidence.

## C02: the objective genitive in Cook §153.d

Cook's *First Book*, selected 1895 second edition, printed p89 / one-based PDF105, gives **in Frēan ęgesan** and then says **“ęgesan is an obj. gen.”** The objective genitive is **Frēan**, the Lord. It depends on the noun **ęgesan**, fear. Cook's own following explanation confirms this: when the noun *fear* is replaced by the verb *fear*, the Lord becomes its object.

This corrects the grammatical analysis, not the transmitted phrase. Preserve both the phrase and the erroneous statement in source evidence; teach **Frēan** as the objective genitive. The coordinator independently inspected the [page image](audits/unit09-coordinator/cook-fb-p89.png). Unit10's grammar audit independently checks the same primary page. The correction affects Unit10's discussion of dependent genitives and its source note; it does not change any of Cook III.13–15's English prompts or the selected Judgment reading.

## C03: the reference after forceorfan in Cook VII.23.7

Cook's *Exercises*, printed p. 12 / one-based PDF 20, prints **“forceorfan, 134”** in VII.23.7. In the selected 1895 second edition of the *First Book*, §134, printed p. 78 / PDF 94, concerns **munan**, not **forceorfan**. The reference is therefore inapplicable to the selected grammar and is treated as an apparent miscitation. The evidence does not establish how the mismatch arose or whether another edition explains it.

Preserve **134** in the original prompt. Give the verified strong III paradigm and lexical evidence separately, as recorded in the [Unit 13 grammar audit](audits/unit13-grammar/REPORT.md). The coordinator inspected both the [exercise page](audits/unit13-source/cook-ex-002.png) and the [grammar page](audits/unit11-grammar/cook_fb_1895_2-p78.png). This correction concerns a reference; it does not replace the verb, alter the English prompt, or assign the unrelated munan system.

## C04: the noun reference after wund in Cook VIII.27.4

Cook's *Exercises*, printed p. 14 / one-based PDF 22, prints **“sores (wund, 43)”**. In the selected *First Book*, §43, printed p. 28 / PDF 44, describes strong masculine nouns. The noun **wund**, a wound or sore, is feminine: Bosworth–Toller, main volume p. 1281a, gives **wund, e; f.**, explicitly including a sore caused by disease. Its Supplement, p. 570b under **hund**, quotes the relevant Luke 16.21 wording **Hundas cōmon and his wunda liccodon**. The selected grammar's vocabulary on p. 323 lists a distinct adjective **wund**, “wounded, sore,” under §58.

Preserve **43** in the original prompt and explain the mismatch separately. Teach the noun through the complete strong feminine pattern in Cook §51.b, printed p. 35 / PDF 51, with the selected accusative plural **wunda**. Do not infer masculine **wundas** from the erroneous reference or confuse the noun with the adjective. The evidence establishes the mismatch, not its historical cause.

The source editor and coordinator independently inspected the [exercise page](audits/unit14-source/cook-ex-002.png), [§43](audits/unit14-source/cook-fb-p28.png), [§51.b](audits/unit14-source/cook-fb-p35.png), [vocabulary page](audits/unit14-source/cook-fb-p323.png), [dictionary main page](audits/unit14-source/bt_b1281.jpg) and [Supplement page](audits/unit14-source/bt_d0570.jpg). This correction affects Unit 14's vocabulary, declension and VIII.27.4 support; it does not alter the original English prompt.

## C05: the reference attached to ōðre in the Dangers reading

In the selected *First Book*, printed p. 157 / one-based PDF 173, note 1 is attached to **ōðre** in **ofer ðā ōðre eorðan** and reads **“See p. 156, n. 12.”** The cited note 12 explains **torrum** as probably “crag.” The relevant parallel for **ōðre** is instead note 11 on p. 156 / PDF 172, which explains **se ōðer wudu** as “the rest of the forest.”

Preserve the printed pointer in the original apparatus. Give the reading **“above the rest of the earth”** in separate learner help and identify note 11 as the relevant parallel. Treat the original pointer as an apparent mismatch; the evidence does not establish its cause or the editor's intention. The source editor and coordinator independently inspected both [p. 156](audits/unit14-source/dangers-001.png) and [p. 157](audits/unit14-source/dangers-002.png). No Old English wording changes.
