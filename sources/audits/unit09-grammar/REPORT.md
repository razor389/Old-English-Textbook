## Central conclusions

Finite ðæt clauses, wh questions and hwæðer questions are new constructions despite prior exposure to their words. Cook §§194–199 and Fulk §§44–45,58 distinguish factual indicative content from reported or hypothetical subjunctive content. Tasks must state a particular reporting stance when they require a particular mood; neither reporting nor a past main verb triggers a mechanical change in every subordinate verb. Person/reference changes depend on the actual context. The selected simultaneous present/past examples do not assign a full tense-sequence system, perfect or pluperfect, or other advanced Unit 12 material.

Hātan command + accusative actor + plain infinitive and gesēon perception + accusative actor + plain infinitive are separate lexical frames. An infinitive can retain its own object. Prior hātan naming takes two accusatives and was taught in Unit 7; older supplied statements in retained lexical history do not erase that later milestone. Gewyrcean object + predicate adjective is directly illustrated in Cook §173. The new predicate must agree with the object. BT Main855b independently supports Saga mē with a wh clause and sęcgan with hwæðer; Supplement575a–b supports witan with dependent wh. The new conjunction hwæðer is kept distinct from Unit 8's inflected which-of-two pronoun.

Cook II.11.5–6 require the explicit bounded headless ðæt relative, illustrated by Cook p133's be-what-you-are and what-one-ought-to-be constructions. Unit 8 ordinary antecedent relatives alone do not establish it. The preference in II.9.14 is grounded in Cook p132: dative experiencer + lēofre is + plain wician, with parallel mid+dative phrases. Source geðūht constructions on p131 stay locally supplied; Cook note14 calls furðra nominative, so the audit does not flatten every source infinitive expression into accusative-plus-infinitive syntax.

The smaller exercise dependencies are explicit: temporal on+accusative day; mid+dative accompaniment; dative recipient with speaking; dative beneficiary with fręmman; hwilc ēower and some-of-them partitives; causal for-ðām; a present unreal wish in the preterite subjunctive; and volitional refusal with ne plus the prior willan forms. Only the personal genitive ēower needed by that partitive frame is promoted. II.9.6 combines masculine ęle and neuter wīn: Fulk p47 states the usual neuter agreement for mixed-gender groups, supporting selected neuterplural gōd. This is a stated normal pattern, not an exceptionless claim.

II.9.11 can retain slēan without an expressed object. BT Main883a, s.v. sleán B.I, explicitly lists strike/make a stroke and gives Hé yrringa slóh; B.Ia concerns a smith striking. This is a new bounded sense/frame, not an inference from the old accusative-object frame. Cook p132's bēatendra slęcgea corroborates the hammer-as-striker situation and supports bēatan as a defensible alternative. The teaching sentence is a new composition, not an exact quotation.

## Paradigm choices and limits

The new systems are cīepan, rōwan, āfēdan, bycgan, blāwan, sēcan, wician, begān, gestillan, ðolian, forlǣtan, āstīgan, bēatan, fręmman, gegaderian, ālīehtan and rǣdan. Full matrices expand source plural rows into three persons. Derived cells are identified as grammatical expansions, not separately attested tokens.

Selected diagnostics include bygest/bygeð and singular command byge, verified in BT Main137; cīepte, āfēdde, gestilde, fręmede and the irregular bohte/sōhte; rēwst/rēwð, blǣwst/blǣwð, bīetst/bīett; and āstāg/āstige/āstigon with āstāh a defensible alternative. Rǣdan is explicitly weak113 in Cook's vocabulary and “usually weak” in §110, so the selected past is rǣdde/rǣddon; the strong rēd/rēdon alternative is valid language but unselected recall material. Ālīehtan selects uncontracted present ālīehtest/ālīehteð, licensed by Wright §529; its present/past homographs are acknowledged rather than repaired with invented length marks.

All twelve indicative cells of every verb are productive. Commands are added only for rōwan, bycgan, sēcan, begān, gestillan and rǣdan. Sēcan alone adds a complete new subjunctive system. The source's exact sēceað remains a recognition variant beside selected sēcað. New infinitive citations remain supplied except wician, which is productive in the preference frame. No other nonfinite production follows from a vocabulary heading. Postposed-pronoun plural -e remains unassigned reference evidence, as it was in Unit 6; selected commands omit or prepose their subject.

The source agent owns nominal/adjectival paradigms and local supports. Its 365-days phrase is a declared rule adaptation, not a quoted attestation: ðrēo agrees with neuter hund (BT Main566a) and daga is the genitive count under Cook §§78–79. It must remain supplied unless a future contract deliberately teaches the larger numeral system. Exact fēt, geðūht, gerǣd, tōgēiecte and the living-creatures participial phrase remain bounded source supports, not excuses to assign additional full systems.
