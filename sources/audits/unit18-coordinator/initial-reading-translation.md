Now, when the king's daughter saw that Apollonius was so well trained in all good accomplishments, her heart fell in love with him. Then, after the banquet had ended, the girl said to the king: “Dear father, a little while ago you allowed me to give Apollonius whatever I wished from your treasury.” King Arcestrates said to her: “Give him whatever you wish.”

Then she went out very happily and said: “Master Apollonius, with my father's permission I give you two hundred pounds of gold, four hundred pounds by weight of silver, the greater part of the precious clothing, and twenty enslaved men.” Then she spoke thus to the enslaved men: “Carry these things with you, which I promised to Apollonius, my teacher, and put them inside the chamber before my friends.” This was then done as the princess commanded, and all the men who saw them praised her gifts.

Then the banquet ended, and all the men rose, greeted the king and the princess, wished them well, and returned home. Apollonius also said: “You, good king and benefactor of the poor, and you, princess and lover of learning, may you be well.” He also looked toward the enslaved men whom the girl had given him and said to them: “Take with you these things which the princess gave me, and let us go and find our lodging, so that we may rest.”

## Initial analysis notes

- *Hiere mōd* is the grammatical subject of *gefēoll*; the natural English rendering above compresses the literal heart/mind expression. *His lufe* is love directed toward him in this context.
- *Līefdest … ðæt ic mōste* is permission expressed with a finite clause, naturally translated with an English infinitive. Do not import an unexpressed Old English infinitive.
- The gift list preserves the two quantities, weight, clothing and twenty human beings. The exact numerical expressions need their own local support and evidence; this translation does not establish an unrestricted higher-numeral system.
- The later relative *ðe* refers to *męnn*, as original note 176.13 explicitly states; the intervening plural *hīe* is interpreted as the gifts seen. A learner with the note is not independently discovering that antecedent.
- *Bǣdon hīe gesunde bēon* reports the guests' farewell; *bēon gē gesunde* directly addresses the king and princess together. The latter is a plural wish, not a silently regularized *bēoð* command.
- The final enslaved-men relative identifies people already given to Apollonius, while the final things-relative identifies the portable gifts. Keep both referents distinct.
- The requested action is to seek the guesthouse so that the speakers can rest. The next printed teaching episode is outside this witness.
