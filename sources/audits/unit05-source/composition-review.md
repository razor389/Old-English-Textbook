The account uses already productive nominal senses: food, man, bird, fish, water and dwelling from Unit 1, two from Unit 3, and companion and shore from Unit 4. **Hiera** and **his** are invariant third-person possessors. **Twēgen męnn** has masculine nominative plural agreement. The giving clauses have an accusative thing and a dative recipient; **ðǣm fuglum** is therefore correctly dative plural. Using birds as recipients is an ordinary application of the recipient frame, not an assertion that the composed sentence is attested. The mass noun **męte** denotes food, including the man's share; the text does not specify its kind or amount.

The account requires **etan** without an expressed object in three clauses. This use is independently supported by Bosworth–Toller, Supplement p.195a, *etan* I(1), “to take food, take a meal,” and Main p.259b, *etan*, which also includes birds as eaters. Both facsimiles were inspected (`unit05-grammar/bt_d0195.jpg` and `bt_b0259.jpg`). The grammar audit's `U05-EAT-FRAME` already includes this absolute use as well as food in the accusative. No understood object needs to be supplied as a new grammatical construction.

The ordinary intransitive senses of **sittan**, **licgan**, **standan** and **feallan** are supported in the grammar audit's frames and independently checked against Main pp.879b, 636b–637a, 910b and 271b. The physical carry and take senses were checked on pp.88a and 721a–b; giving on p.474b. The source audit's independently checked ordinary seeing, winged flying and living-creature swimming frames cover the remaining verbs. The phrases **on ðǣm hofe**, **on ðǣm strande** and **on ðǣm wætere** all express location with the dative, including swimming within the water. None denotes a goal. The negation **ne ǣt** is the proposed simple indicative negation; no negative indefinite or negative-concord construction is involved.

There is one interpretive boundary for the key. In **and hīe ǣton**, the intended eaters are the companions, but plural **hīe** does not uniquely select them over the men. **Ān mann** introduces a man without explicitly saying whether he belongs to the first pair or the companions. The story has a coherent food-sharing interpretation, and no wording change is necessary. A question or key must not demand a unique antecedent or membership that the text does not express. If unique reference becomes an assessment target, the author should instead repeat the appropriate noun explicitly.
