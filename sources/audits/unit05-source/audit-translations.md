# Independent source-audit translations

Prepared from the exact Old English before any Unit5 learner key. These are editorial audit translations; they do not assign tasks or certify a future key. Local support and structural limits are recorded separately in `audit.json`.

## First day: explicit revisit

In the beginning God created heaven and earth. Now the earth was empty and void; and darkness was over the surface of the deep; and God's spirit was borne over the waters. God then said, “Let light come to be”; and light was made. God then saw that it was good; and he separated the light from the darkness. And he called the light Day, and the darkness Night. Then evening and morning had come to be—one day.

**Heaven** represents source weak-feminine singular **heofonan**, not the exercise's masculine plural “heavens.” **Was borne/carried** preserves the passive **wæs gefęred**; “moved over” is a natural contextual English rendering. **Now** renders connective **sōðlice** under Cook's *autem* note. The last formula may be rendered “there came to be evening and morning—one day”; it does not literally contain the ordinal “first.” The two naming accusatives are a supported construction.

## Second day: complete new extension

God then said again, “Let a firmament come to be in the midst of the waters, and let it separate the waters from the waters.” And God made the firmament, and separated the waters which were under the firmament from those which were above the firmament; it was then done so. And God called the firmament Heaven. And evening and morning had come to be—a second day.

The subject of both quoted optatives is **fæstnes**, the firmament. **Those** supplies the substantive use of **ðǣm**, whose understood referent is waters. Both relative **ðe** tokens are subjects within their clauses. **Under** and **above** render the two stated locations, with no directional meaning added. **It was then done so** keeps the actual **gedōn** participle and fulfillment formula distinct from the first-day **wearð geworht** and the closing **wæs geworden**. Cook's note for **ęft** gives *quoque*, so “God then also said” is defensible alongside “said again.”
