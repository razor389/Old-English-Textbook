## Allocation implications

1. **Exercises 1–3 are a coherent first nominal unit:** 46 items, with 16 + 12 + 18 preserved original prompts. Their predominant nominative/genitive work needs new accusative/dative practice. The exact Exercise 1–2 transcriptions supply the original prompts; this unit establishes the complete nominal teaching scope.
2. **Exercise 4 is mixed.** Its 18 items combine strong/weak adjective use, comparison, ordinals, the past copula, and a final passive command: “Increase (sing.) and be multiplied.” If passive commands are not taught in the adjective unit, defer **I.4.18 explicitly**. Teaching only the imperative is insufficient for that item. The source note reads §55; OCR §65 is wrong.
3. **Exercises 5–8 need genuine verbal and clause preparation.** Exercise 5 uses present and preterite forms across verb classes. Exercise 6 adds an optative command, a recipient dative, and naming; Exercise 7 adds passive and further commands/wishes. Exercise 8 requires pronouns and a final relative clause. Retaining complete exercises after the relevant verbal systems is defensible; earlier units can supply new focused practice.
4. **Exercises 9 onward are not isolated conjugation drills.** Exercise 9 already includes hortatory subjunctive, modal refusal, and an impersonal comparative preference. Exercise 10 ends in an irrealis wish; Exercise 12 combines comparatives, commands, relative clauses, an object complement, partitives, and numerals. Allocate later complete sets as cumulative practice after adequate morphology and basic subordinate syntax, then let each contract audit additional dependencies.
5. **Later groups support grammatical consolidation and connected composition.** Case government, perception constructions, passive, relatives, indirect statement/question, purpose/result, temporal and conditional clauses recur across sets. Whole-set allocations can follow a planned progression through those systems without pretending the source sequence itself consists of pure grammar units.
6. **Preserve connected sequences.** Exercises 28–29, 38–39, and 44–48 contain substantial sequential material. In particular, keeping 44–48 together provides a coherent final composition block and avoids splitting the Joseph episode merely to equalize unit size. A course unit may span several sessions.

The proposed grammar-led 18-unit allocation received from the curriculum analyst is consistent with this structural inspection, with the specific requirement that I.4.18 reach the passive-command unit unless its full construction is taught earlier.
