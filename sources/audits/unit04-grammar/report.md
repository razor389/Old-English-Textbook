## Morphological decisions

1. **Four stems and past second singular.** Cook §§92–95 distinguish the present, past singular, past plural and participial stems. Past second singular is the plural stem plus **-e**, not the past singular plus an ending: **drāf / drife / drifon**, **cēas / cure / curon**, **band / bunde / bundon**. The instructional contrast must be explicit.
2. **Present singular changes.** The selected endings show the actual surface combinations: **rītst/rītt**, **wrītst/wrītt**, **rīst/rīst**, **snīst/snīð**, **bīetst/bīett**, **cīest/cīest**, **sīest/sīeð**, **bintst/bint**, **gieltst/gielt**, **bierst/bierst**, **wierst/wierð**, **winst/winð**. Cook's **īe** is long in class II; the **ie** of class III **bierst/wierst/wierpst** is short. These distinctions are image-checked, not inferred from OCR.
3. **Quantity and consonants.** Class I normally has **ī : ā : i : i**; class II **ēo or ū : ēa : u : o**. The selected ordinary class III family vowels are short. Explain d/t + ð, d + st, s and ð combinations, geminate reduction, and the stated g/h environment together with the tables. **Snīðan** and **sēoðan** change ð to d in later stems; **cēosan** changes s to r; **weorðan** changes ð to d. These are lexical stem relationships, not free substitutions.
4. **Contracts.** **Wrēon** and class II **tēon** have present **wrēo/wrēoð** and **tēo/tēoð**, not forms made by adding the ordinary endings to the contracted spelling. Their present singular has **wrīhst/wrīhð** and **tīehst/tīehð**. Class I **tēon** ‘censure’ and weak **tēon** ‘arrange’ are different lexemes and not assigned. Cook §101 and Wright §492 also acknowledge class II analogical pasts of class I contracts; the selected **wrēon** row is the stated class I system, with no silent mixture.
5. **Findan.** Cook §104, p.62 / PDF78, marks third-singular **funde** usual. Selected first singular is **fand**, second singular **funde**, third singular **funde**, plural **fundon**. Wright §498, p.258 / PDF278, explicitly also permits first-singular **funde**. Thus first **funde** and third **fand** are valid unselected alternatives; a selected-target instruction may distinguish them without calling them impossible Old English.
6. **Bregdan.** Selected recognition reference: **bregde, brītst, brītt, bregdað; brægd, brugde, brægd, brugdon**. Cook prints exceptional **brītt**, with long ī. The second singular is transparently derived using the checked g-loss/lengthening, singular vowel and dental rules. Clark Hall's digital **brīst/brītst/brīt** is corroboration only because no original Hall scan was checked. Wright's **brǣd/brūdon/brōden** g-loss family is an explicit alternative; do not silently substitute it for all selected cells.
7. **Fēolan.** The full recognition reference is deliberately composite: **fēole, filhst, filhð, fēolað; fealh, fulge, fealh, fulgon**. Cook supplies **fēolan, fealh, fulgon (fǣlon), folen**. BT main p.275a, entry **felgan**, explicitly prints short-i **filhst/filhþ**; Supplement p.211b corrects **feolan** to **feólan** and transfers the **felgan** passages. The transfer concerns passages, not attestation of every generated cell. The adopted present forms preserve h and declare **þ → ð**. Cook's short-o **folen** remains the citation; Wright §501 has distinct **folgen/fōlen** alternatives. This synthetic table is recognition only and must not be described as a verbatim Cook paradigm.
8. **Postposed subject pronoun.** Cook §95 footnote gives present plural **binde wē**. This is a word-order-conditioned -e, not permission to write -e for every plural verb. The final design may leave this as an editorial boundary without a learner milestone; ordinary production can keep subject-before-verb order. Full-ending and dialectal variants need explicit evidence; the audit does not mechanically enumerate every imaginable -est/-eð spelling.
