## Teaching recommendation

The finite verbs **tōbærst** and **forwurdon** supply two useful Class III targets: third-person singular of **tōberstan**, “break apart,” and third-person plural of **forweorðan**, “perish.” Their source uses are intransitive. The grammar auditor has agreed to audit both complete prefixed indicative systems separately; no unrestricted prefix rule or passive use is inferred. The agreed target IDs are `toberstan-pret-3-sg` and `forweorthan-pret-3-pl`, with senses `toberstan-1` and `forweorthan-1`.

The source contains no Class I or II finite form. The controlled narrative and other tasks must provide that practice. Extending this excerpt merely to collect additional classes would add avoidable subordinate clauses, infinitives and other systems.

Four nouns can become productive under patterns already taught. The dictionary identifies each class, while Cook §§43, 47 and 53 supply the complete patterns. The 32 cells in `new-nouns.json` are pattern-derived, not misrepresented as four printed individual paradigms.

| **wind**, masculine: wind | wind, windes, winde, wind | windas, winda, windum, windas |

## Local support and limits

Ten support groups explicitly cover the harder material. Four of the six finite verbs remain supplied: **cōmon**, **stōd**, **becōm**, **ūpēode**. The last keeps both printed macrons. Other exact readings include **sūðwesterna**, **ęgeslican**, and **Cyreniscan** without an added length mark.

The source can test target-verb agreement and basic narrative meaning with this help. It remains a guided reading: 23 of 44 token forms and 24 of 44 token senses are supplied. Seven token forms and seven token senses represent the proposed productive nouns and verbs; two tokens, **eall/ealle**, retain prior recognition status. Supplied material must not be tested for independent recall.

- **him ongēan stōd** is supplied as “stood against/opposed them,” with the preceding northeast winds as the guiding antecedent. This is contextual inference: **him** alone permits dative singular or plural. A unique antecedent is not an unsupported grading requirement.
- Postposed **eall/ealle** mean “the whole ship” and “all the companions.” Their prior recognition milestones do not grant productive quantifier syntax.
- The first **Apollonius** is genitive, explicitly identified by Cook p. 166 n. 1. The later occurrence is the nominative subject. No regular foreign-name declension is invented.
- **āna**, “alone,” is weak masculine nominative singular under §79; prior cardinal **ān**, “one,” does not cover this sense or construction.
- **on ðisse ęgeslican hrēohnesse**, “in this terrible storm,” uses a supplied circumstantial sense of **on**. **tō dēaðe**, “to death,” is likewise a supplied endpoint idiom.
- **mid sunde**, “by swimming,” is a noun in the dative and a supplied expression of means. It does not introduce an infinitive system.
- **tō Pentapolim ðǣm Cyreniscan lande** is a supplied destination and apposition. The native apposition is dative neuter singular; the foreign spelling **-im** does not establish an Old English accusative rule after **tō**.
- **ðǣr ūpēode on ðǣm strande** has Apollonius as the continuing subject. The source explicitly has masculine dative singular **strande**. Idiomatic “came ashore” does not authorize changing that source case.

Cook p. 165 n. 7 quotes a Latin verse about opposing winds; it does not resolve the Old English pronoun’s antecedent by itself. The remaining notes are p. 166 n. 1 on the foreign genitive, n. 2 referring **forwurdon** to **forweorðan**, and n. 3 referring **āna** to §79. All four are retained separately in the audit.
