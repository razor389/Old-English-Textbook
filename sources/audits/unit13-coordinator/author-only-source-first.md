## Context read from the edition

The preceding account identifies the man as Owini. He had served Æthelthryth, renounced worldly possessions and come to the monastery carrying an axe and an adze. He worked with his hands; while the others studied indoors, he worked outside as needed. This information may be supplied in English before the extract. The text distinguishes the church to which the companions have gone from the bishop's local oratory: see 152 note 5. The continuation beginning «Ðā hīe ðā tō him cōmon» is outside this assignment.

## Independent translation

When, one day, he was doing something of this sort outside, and his companions had gone to the town, to church, as they often did, and the bishop was alone in the oratory, occupied either in reading or in prayer, he suddenly heard—as he said afterwards—the sweetest and most beautiful sound of people singing and rejoicing descending from heaven to earth. He said that he first heard that sound and that song from the south-eastern part of the sky, that is, from the height of the winter sunrise; and from there they approached him little by little, until it reached the roof of the oratory in which the bishop was, and, entering, filled the whole place and surrounded it on all sides. He then directed his attention earnestly to what he heard. Then, after about half an hour, he heard the same song of joy rise from the roof of that same oratory and return upwards to heaven with indescribable sweetness, by the same way by which it had come before.

He remained there for some time, wondering and marvelling; with an attentive mind he thought and considered what those things might mean. Then the bishop opened the window of the oratory and made a sign with his hand, as was his custom if anyone was outside, that he should come in to him. He immediately went in to him. He—the bishop—said to him: “Go quickly to the church and tell our seven brothers to come here to me; and you too be present with us.”

## Analyses to check during reconciliation

- The opening «Ðā … ðā» frames an extended temporal setting and the principal hearing event. Coordinated clauses and parenthetical reporting interrupt a simple subject–verb–object sequence.
- «Ðā stefne and ðone sang» are accusatives governed by the embedded «gehīerde», not subjects or objects of «cwæð»: source note 8 explicitly supplies that relation.
- The plural «nēalǣcton» follows the coordinated sound and song. «hē becōm» refers to masculine «sang»; note 9a explicitly says «Se sang». The matching «ðe hē ǣr cōm» likewise refers to the song, whereas «And hē ðā … his mōd» returns to the listener. English pronoun gender must follow the intended referent rather than mechanically reproduce masculine «he».
- The relative particle in «ðe se bisceop in wæs» is governed by the following «in», according to note 11. Its printed shape does not identify a noun case ending.
- «eall» is accusative singular according to note 12; the translation above supplies “place” to make the reference explicit. «ingangende», «gefylde» and «ymbsealde» continue the movement and action of the sound. Confirm the exact selected senses and support in the final inventory.
- «swā-swā healfre tīde fæce» and «ðȳ ilcan wege» need their own source-supported time and route analyses. Do not treat them as ordinary accusative duration.
- «hwæt ðā ðing bēon sceolden» asks the significance of the event. The English wording in this witness is a contextual rendering, not a newly generalized rule of obligatory backshift.
- «Cwæð hē, se bisceop, him tō» uses an apposition to disambiguate the speaker; «tō» follows its pronoun complement. «mid wes» is a command to be present, not a newly inferred prefixed verb.
- The last words are a coherent summons, not Chad's complete death narrative. The translation's “with us” makes the command's implied company explicit; compare alternative “you too be there”.
