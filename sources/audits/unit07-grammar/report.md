## Agreement and passive

Cook §§59–62,179, Wright §§429–442 and Fulk §§54–56 distinguish three matters:

1. An attributive participle is an adjective for gender, number, case and strong/weak choice. Present **-ende** follows the **grēne** pattern: source neuter plural **-endu** is not an error. An **-end** agent noun is a different formation.
2. Past participles have different lexical stem patterns. The tables explicitly distinguish **bunden**, **hīered**, **lufod**, and **seald**. Heavy disyllabic stems can show syncope, for example **bundnes/bundnum** and **hīerdes/hīerdum**; West Saxon also restores vowels. Full vowels are the consistent canonical choice in these edited models, with checked syncopated variants listed. Before consonantal **-ne/-re/-ra**, preserve the stem; **bundenne** is legitimate. Short-root **fręmed/nęred/lufod** retain their medial vowel in the selected patterns. Do not extrapolate every syncope variant indiscriminately to every -od participle.
3. **Predicate participles do not require the same agreement rule as attributive adjectives.** Cook §61 calls present participles in nominative/accusative predicates generally uninflected. Section62 calls the predicate past participle generally indeclinable **or ending like the strong masculine**; §179 also permits uninflected predicates. Thus **ðā dēor sindon bunden** and **ðā dēor sindon bundene** are both defensible verbal passives. The selected attributive strong neuter plural is **bundenu**, with accepted **bundene/bunden** variants. These surface spellings can therefore occur in either construction. The distinction is the syntactic agreement rule, not an exclusive spelling contrast: neither adjective variant becomes invalid merely because it also occurs in a verbal predicate.

Rare -u cells in the full tables are expressly derived from the checked paradigms and lexical stem, as earlier rare adjective cells were; they are not falsely labeled separately text-attested. The learner's productive case scope remains NGDA. Reference instrumentals do not acquire new productive syntactic uses.

For assigned accusative-object verbs, past participle plus a finite form of **wesan/bēon** or **weorðan** gives the ordinary passive. The auxiliary marks person, number, tense and mood. It is useful to contrast a resulting state with an event, but **wesan = state / weorðan = event is not an exceptionless law**. Nor does an Old English auxiliary tense fix one modern English perfect/progressive translation. Productive transformations can omit the agent; no new agentive **fram** construction is needed. A genitive complement of **gīeman**, or dative complement of **helpan**, is not automatically promoted to a nominative subject by this elementary accusative-passive rule.

The fifth-day **wæs geworden** is an intransitive become/come-to-pass resultative formula, not a transitive passive. Earlier supplied status remains appropriate. General perfects, progressives, absolutes and substantival participles remain outside independent production.

## Ordinary mood uses

The productive syntax can be bounded to independent wishes/exhortations and basic **gif** conditions. Cook §193a explicitly supplies first-plural **gān wē** and a third-person wish; the retained plural n is therefore acceptable even though other postposed-pronoun variants exist. English *let* in these renderings does not introduce a new causative **lǣtan** construction.

Cook §196d permits indicative as well as subjunctive conditions. Fulk §45e is more emphatic: **gif does not itself require subjunctive**. Teach an open indicative condition and a contextually tentative request with present subjunctive; avoid a mechanical mood-conversion rule. Fulk §44 explicitly permits nonpast interpretation of preterite subjunctive under unreal/unfulfilled conditions. A fully contextualized, supplied illustration is appropriate; a large autonomous counterfactual production system is not required here.

The proposed new illustration **Gif hē cōme, ic him hulpe.**, with the explicit setting *he is not coming* and supplied nonpast interpretation, is grammatical: **cōme** and **hulpe** are preterite subjunctives, and **helpan** retains its taught dative object. Its interpretation depends on the setting, not the spellings alone. It is new composition based on the audited rules, not an invented quotation from Cook/Fulk.

Ordinary **mæg** capability, **mōt** permission, and **wille/wolde** volition remain distinct from subjunctive wishes. Fulk §48 explicitly warns against treating **willan** as a universal modern future auxiliary. Homographic **wille/wolde/lufode/bēo** forms require clause context; tasks should not demand a unique mood from an isolated spelling.
