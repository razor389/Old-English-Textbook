## Decisions that matter for authoring

Purpose and factual result are separate senses of ðæt. Purpose normally uses the subjunctive; an asserted result normally uses the indicative. Cook §196g gives a curse or wish and does not establish a universal subjunctive result rule. Fulk §§44–45 supplies the necessary contrast. Concessive subjunctives may admit facts. Gif does not automatically select either mood. The new contrary-present conditional is limited to a setting explicitly false now; full tense sequence and past counterfactual systems remain outside this unit.

Temporal ðā/ðonne conjunctions have distinct records from then-adverbs. Supplied histories of ðā, then-ðonne, purpose-ðæt and for-ðon-ðe are retained in promotions. Already productive invariant surfaces remain previously taught where only a sense is new. Mid-ðȳ and ðā-hwīle-ðe, anticipated ǣr/oð-ðæt, negative purpose and the temporal-relative day-when frame are explicit. Fulk p.158 independently attests a day antecedent followed by þe with temporal role. Main/dependent order patterns are parsing tendencies, not obligatory transformation rules.
