# Independent Unit 2 paradigm crosscheck

22 October 2026. Source editor, independently checking `sources/audits/unit02-grammar/paradigms.json` before the chapter contract. Exclusive review file; the grammar auditor retains ownership of the original audit. No learner prose, keys, registers, or contracts changed.

## Result

The **23 selected verb rows** and **40 selected personal-pronoun cells** are defensible from the checked Cook evidence and the targeted Wright corroboration below. Each verb has all twelve present/preterite indicative person/number cells. Twenty-two verb paradigms are proposed for production; the contracted negative companion **nabban** is recognition only. The pronoun table has 34 productive cells and six recognition-only first/second-person genitives; it does not silently teach the agreeing possessive adjectives.

One material **evidence defect in optional variants**, GC01, was found and communicated immediately. The grammar auditor removed the affected mechanically inferred variant claims. The selected paradigms were unaffected. The later file inspection confirmed the removal; no source-book error or new course correction is asserted.

This is a focused crosscheck of selected forms, derivation conditions, and declared alternatives, not a second lexical or syntactic audit of every proposed task.

## Findings

### GC01 — Optional full endings with simplified inherited consonants

**Material; repaired by removing unsupported optional variants.** The initial audit listed **cysest/cyseð** and **gefylest/gefyleð**, inferred from general endings or the printed **sęllan** paradigm. That evidence did not justify transferring its single-consonant pattern to every verb with a doubled consonant.

Wright §305, printed **p.151 / PDF171**, explicitly prints **cyst** “from older **cysseþ**.” This is an actual printed form with double **ss**, not an inferred **cyseð**. Wright §528, **p.269 / PDF289**, separately identifies the inherited double consonant in **fyllan**, comparing Gothic **fulljan**; its simplification before the preterite dental does not establish simplification before every vowel-initial present ending. His §530, **p.272 / PDF292**, lists **fyllan** and **cyssan** together in that inherited long-stem division. Contrast the originally short-stem explanation and **settan/fręmman** patterns in §§522–524, **pp.266–267**.

The bounded editorial conclusion is **not** that every single-consonant historical spelling is impossible. It is that the initial optional forms were not source-certified by the cited rule. Removing those claims is appropriate. No replacement full-ending variant needs to become a new teaching target.

The auditor also removed other mechanically generated uncontracted alternatives unless directly printed or separately verified. Printed **sęlest/sęleð**, the **tt/t** options, and the specifically documented class-II/III alternatives remain distinguished from selected targets.

### GC02 — gierwan has direct paradigm corroboration

**No error; stronger evidence supplied.** Cook §113, **pp.68–69 / PDF84–85**, gives **gierede**, **(ge)gier(w)ed**, and says that present forms may retain or omit **w**. Wright §529, **p.270 / PDF290**, directly prints:

| Present indicative cell | Wright's spelling |
|---|---|
| 1 singular | gierwe |
| 2 singular | gierest |
| 3 singular | giereþ |
| all plural | gierwaþ |

Thus the audit's selected **gierwe / gierest / giereð / gierwað** is an evidenced paradigm, not merely an arbitrary mixture of alternatives. Wright §533, **p.273 / PDF293**, explains the regular loss of **w** in **gierest, giereþ, gierede** and later generalization of forms with or without **w**. Cook's quantity/spelling baseline remains the course's baseline; Wright's **þ** is shown faithfully above.

### GC03 — geręstan and the st contraction

**Selected row acceptable; acknowledge the additional contracted alternative when discussing the rule.** The selected **geręste / geręstest / geręst / geręstað** uses a full ending in the second singular and contraction in the third. The evidence basis should say this explicitly, as the revised audit now does. Its preterite **geręste / geręstest / geręston** follows the consonant-plus-**t** type and agrees with the actual reader's **geręste**.

Wright §476, **p.248 / PDF268**, explicitly includes **st + -st, -þ → -st** and illustrates the corresponding contracted **birst** beside full-ending **birstest/birsteþ**. Alongside Cook's lexical placement of **geręstan** in §113 and his dental simplification rules, this supports contracted second-singular **geręst** as a by-pattern alternative. It should not be rejected as ungrammatical if a learner supplies it. This is not a demand to change the selected **geręstest** target or to add another production milestone.

No erroneous **geręstte** should be formed in the past. Cook's reader note expressly asks why the actual past **geręste** has one **t**; the class's consonant-plus-dental simplification provides the answer.

### GC04 — gewyrcean, dental contractions, and copied principal parts

**No material error found.** Cook §114, **pp.69–70 / PDF85–86**, supplies **wyrc(e)an / worhte / (ge)worht**. The selected prefixed finite forms **gewyrce, gewyrcst, gewyrcð, gewyrceað**, with preterite **geworhte, geworhtest, geworhton**, follow that stem and the class endings. For **gewyrceað** and alternative **gewyrcað**, cite Cook **§18 p.14 / PDF30** explicitly: its rule concerns **e** after **c/g** before another vowel in or before an ending. §114's brief wording alone mentions the infinitive.

Cook §§33–35, **pp.24–25 / PDF40–41**, support the selected dental forms, including **sęntst/sęnt**, **lǣtst/lǣtt**, **mētst/mētt**, and simplification of final **tt** to **t**. Wright §524, **p.267 / PDF287**, independently prints **sette, setst, set(t), settaþ**; the following page confirms **sette, settes(t), setton**. These corroborate the selected **sęttan** forms and the recorded **sętt/sęt** alternative, without requiring an unverified full-ending present variant.

Cook's exact **hīeran, sęllan, fręmman, nęrian** tables (p.72), **lufian** (pp.73–74), and four class-III verbs plus **nabban** (pp.74–76) were inspected against all selected surface rows. Expansion of an all-plural row to three person-labelled cells is legitimate; the past second singular in tables abbreviated with “etc.” follows the explicit weak **-dest** ending. Supplied infinitive and participle citations remain separate from finite teaching and do not imply mastery of passive, perfect, or infinitival syntax.

## Pronouns

All 40 selected cells match Cook §81, **pp.48–49 / PDF64–65**, including the dual, quantities, optional **e** spellings, **hēo**, **hīe**, short **hiere/hiera**, and the personal-genitive forms. The less common variants that remain in the audit are supported by §81.1. Cook §§82–83 distinguish ordinary personal forms used reflexively and the indeclinable third-person genitive possessors from the declining possessive adjectives.

The table correctly avoids third-person dual forms and invented dual verb endings. Recognizing first/second-person genitive cells does not authorize using **mīn/ðīn/ūre/ēower/uncer/incer** as independently productive, inflecting possessive adjectives before their grammar is taught.
