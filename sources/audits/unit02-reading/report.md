22 October 2026. Source editor. Proposed source record **FB-I-CREATION-SABBATH-01**. This is source evidence for reconciliation, not an assignment or learning milestone. No learner lesson or task key has been drafted here.

## Recommendation

Use the complete closing paragraph of Cook's **Reader I, The Creation of the World**, printed **p.128 / one-based PDF p.144**, after the independently graded original reading planned for Unit 2. Its 61 words form a coherent sequence: completion, rest, and blessing of the seventh day. All six noun lemmas and their actual forms are already taught. The paragraph has six weak indicative verbs, genitive possessors, and a useful contrast between reflexive **hine** referring to God and ordinary object **hine** referring to the day.

The paragraph is still a supported source reading. It contains four class-I and two class-II finite verbs, all third-person singular preterites; it contains no class-III finite verb, present tense, dual form, or first/second-person verb. The controlled reading and other tasks must provide those independently. Do not claim that this source tests the whole Unit 2 system.

The coordinator chose the whole paragraph over its shorter first action sentence. The latter would remove some support but would lose the coherent blessing and final explanation. Trades and Occupations, pp.129–133, was screened through OCR and the structural inventory: questions, modals, infinitives, adjectives, and new trade vocabulary give it a heavier support burden here. That screening is **not** an exact source audit of Trades.

## Independent source translation

Prepared directly from the checked Old English before consulting a modern biblical translation or any task key; Cook's notes were used to check the difficult local constructions:

> Accordingly, then, the heavens and the earth and all their array were completed. And God then completed on the seventh day his work which he made, and he rested himself on the seventh day from all the work which he performed. And God blessed the seventh day and hallowed it, because he on that day ceased from his work which he created to make.

A smoother rendering may use **thus**, omit English reflexive **himself** after *rested*, use **had made / had performed** for the relative past sequence, and render **hallowed** as **made holy**. These changes do not create extra Old English forms or constructions. The last words **created to make** remain an awkward but deliberate source relation; they should not be silently replaced by familiar modern biblical wording.

Cook's p.128 note 12 explicitly gives **gescēop tō wyrceanne = creavit ut faceret**, cites the marginal rendering “created to make,” and refers to §200. His §200, p.106 / PDF122, explains the inflected infinitive, called *gerund* in his terminology. The whole ending must be supplied: **ðe hē gescēop tō wyrceanne**, “which he created to make.” The understood maker remains God, not the work. Do not demand a unique smooth paraphrase or treat the phrase as “created and made,” “finished creating,” or an independent finite **wyrceanne**.

Temporal **ðā** remains supplied in this audit, as it was in Unit 1; the coordinator may explicitly teach it in the contract. It must never be marked prior solely because the learner saw it before.

Required local help:

1. **Eornostlice** means “accordingly, thus”; **ðā** means “then” here. The latter is not an article. Vocabulary p.285 / PDF301 and §84.1 p.50 / PDF66 verify the meanings and distinction.
2. **wǣron fullfręmede** means “were completed/finished.” Supply the passive whole. **fullfręmede** is a plural participle here, not another finite preterite merely because it ends in **-ede**. See p.128 note 2 and §187 p.101 / PDF117.
3. Supply the actual adjective forms: **eall hiera frætwung**, “all their array”; **eallum** agrees with neuter dative singular **weorce**; **seofoðan** means “seventh,” weak masculine accusative singular. No full adjective or numeral system is thereby taught.
4. **on ðone seofoðan dæg** and **on ðone dæg** are temporal accusative phrases, “on the seventh day / on that day.” Cook expressly flags the case at p.128 note 5 and repeats the example under §172.1, p.97 / PDF113. The learner must not replace **dæg** with **dæge** by extending Unit 1's locative rule, or infer motion from the accusative.
5. The three **ðe** clauses mean “which he made,” “which he performed,” and “which he created to make.” Supply their boundaries and relation to **weorc / weorce / weorces**. Bare **ðe** represents the direct object inside each clause; the antecedents' external cases differ. The particle is not second-person **ðē**. See §87d p.52 / PDF68.
6. Teach **hiera/his** as third-person genitive possessors and the ordinary personal-pronoun forms. They do not inflect to agree with the possessed noun. **hē geręste hine** is Cook's exact reflexive example in §184b, p.101 / PDF117. The later **hine** instead refers to masculine **dæg**. This is suitable reference analysis after the pronoun lesson.
7. Supply the actual contextual frame **geręste hine ... fram eallum ðǣm weorce**, “rested ... from all the work.” Unit 1 taught **fram** with dative in an explicitly supplied separating frame. This audit does not silently expand its independent production scope to every resting/cessation expression.
8. **for-ðon-ðe** introduces “because”; supply the actual causal span. Its vocabulary entry is on p.288 / PDF304.
9. **geswāc his weorces** means “ceased from his work.” **geswāc** is a supplied strong preterite; **weorces** is genitive governed by that verb. Cook gives this exact example under cessation, §156k p.92 / PDF108, and explains it again in p.128 note 11. Known noun-genitive forms do not imply known verbal genitive government.
10. Supply **gescēop** and the whole final **tō wyrceanne** relation as discussed above. **gescēop** remains unlearned despite its earlier supplied exposure; a familiar finite **wyrcean**, if taught elsewhere in Unit 2, would not teach this inflected infinitive or its use.

The first-day source in Unit 1 contained 12 finite tokens: five learned copula forms and seven supplied non-copulas. This paragraph contains nine finite tokens: one learned copula, six newly taught weak indicatives, and two supplied strong preterites. Thus verb-form support falls meaningfully. This numerical comparison does **not** erase the passive, temporal, relative, causal, adjective, or infinitive support listed above.
