**FB-XII-APOLLONIUS-FISHERMAN-GIFT-01**

Then, as soon as the fisherman saw that the young man was lying at his feet, he compassionately lifted him up and led him with him to his house, and set before him the provisions that he had to offer him. He still wanted, according to his ability, to show him greater kindness; he then tore his cloak in two and gave Apollonius one half, saying: “Take what I have to give you, and go into the town.”

“With compassion” is a closer rendering of mid mildheortnesse; “compassionately” is natural English. The context identifies the fisherman’s feet/house and Apollonius as the beneficiary. “He wanted to show him still greater kindness, as far as he could” is a defensible less literal English rearrangement, but still is printed with the temporal/volitional sequence and māran supplies greater. “Food,” “provisions,” or “a meal” can convey ēstas if the learner understands its source accusative plural. “The half part” is literal; “one half” is natural. Nothing in the cut says that the later direction has already been followed.

**NEW-U06-TEXT-A, the68-word controlled account**

The man said, “I want to travel. My ship is ready. Come and help me.” Two companions came to help. The man said, “Take the food and carry it. Give the birds the water.” The companion said, “I can carry the food. We must perform the work.” The companions carried the food and gave the birds the water. The man said, “Sit and eat.” The companions ate. They travelled.

All commands in the account address more than one person. Hine most naturally refers to the masculine food, but context contributes to its identification. Eating the mentioned food is a reasonable inference; the final sentence with ǣton supplies no explicit object. The final “they” can refer to the companions or contextually include the whole party. The text asserts readiness of the ship and later travel, without explicitly asserting boarding, sailing, or a destination.

**Teaching examples01–10**

1. I know how to swim. He can carry the water.
2. We must perform the work.
3. He wanted to travel.
4. Take the food: first to one person, then to several.
5. Give me the water: to one person. Help me: to several.
6. Do not drink the water: to one person.
7. He came to help.
8. Go. Do the work well: to one person.
9. The ship will be ready, in the explicitly supplied expected-later context. The form bið does not by itself make every use of this series future.
10. I know those things. Here the things are previously mentioned facts or matters. This is an explicitly new bounded sense of the familiar noun, not an assumption that the earlier creation-object use had already taught it. The corresponding M13 second sentence, Hē wiste ðā ðing, means “He knew those things.”

**X05 optional four-sentence model in the design token seed**

Se mann cōm tō helpanne twǣm gefērum. Ðā gefēran willað ðone męte beran. Berað ðone męte. Hīe bǣron ðone męte.

The man came to help two companions. The companions want to carry the food. Carry the food. They carried the food.

The purpose subject is the arriving man, the two companions are dative recipients of helping, the desire belongs to the companions, and the command addresses them in the plural. This is one possible connected answer; other valid sequences satisfying the stated lexical and construction targets are not errors merely for differing from it.
