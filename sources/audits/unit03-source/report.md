## Exercise 4

Cook Group I, Exercise 4 occupies printed p. 2/PDF 10. All items `EX-I-04-01` through `EX-I-04-18` are preserved. The note reads: “[NOTE. — For adjectives preceded by a demonstrative, see 55.]” The reference is **55**, confirmed against Cook §55, p. 38/PDF 54. Item 12 retains the literal reference “(80)”.

Items 1–17 are proposed for Unit 3. Item 18, “Increase (sing.) and be multiplied.”, remains explicitly deferred to Unit 7. Its passive wording requires the later imperative/passive system; it must not be shortened to an active command to make it fit this unit.

The exercise requires the following substantive additions or promotions:

- A complete heavy-neuter paradigm for **ðing**, “thing”: singular **ðing, ðinges, ðinge, ðing**; plural **ðing, ðinga, ðingum, ðing**. The lexical class is printed in the vocabulary, p. 318/PDF 334; the complete model is Cook §47, p. 32/PDF 48. This is a new lexical entry using a previously taught pattern.
- A count sense of **lēoht**, “a light; luminary”, and explicit production promotion of its four plural cells. Its prior sense is mass “light”; its prior plural cells have recognition milestones only. The two lights and their greater/lesser pairing in the reader establish the count use. Item 15 and the later light/darkness contrast retain the mass sense.
- **æppelbǣre**, “fruit-bearing”, from the compound lexical entry, rather than an unexplained substitute **bǣre**. The printed glossary compound hyphen is recorded separately; joining it in a teaching headword is declared normalization.
- **styriendlic**, “moving”, as an attested adjective, without silently adding participial syntax.
- Production teaching for **īdel**, **ǣmtig**, **gōd**, and **seofoða**, which were previously supplied rather than learned. Both empty/void adjectives are attested for either English sense; their order is not intrinsically a linguistic distinction.
- Complete strong/weak, comparative, and ordinal systems under the morphology audit, including predicate agreement, adjective agreement inside genitives, and the exceptions to a simple “article means weak” rule.

The independently reconciled target cells include weak **lȳtla**, comparative neuter **māre/lǣsse**, and weak genitive plural **styriendlicra**. The selected feminine nominative of **ōðer** is **ōðru**, with endingless **ōðer** accepted under Cook §57.3. It remains strong after an article. Other ordinals are weak even without an article. **ǣresta** is selected for “first”; no uncertain quantity in an unselected synonym is adopted.

The form requirements in the audit are not a learner answer key. Noun-phrase heads use nominative citation targets; the final contract should declare any case-specific assessment, so valid language in another syntactic setting is not confused with compliance with a stated target.

## Prior teaching and local support

Each of the 109 tokens has separate form and sense histories from `lexicon/entries.json`, plus its proposed current disposition. There are 66 occurrences with prior form/sense targets, 33 locally supplied occurrences, and 10 newly taught target occurrences. These token counts do not certify the syntax of their containing clauses as taught.

The sixteen finite-verb tokens break down as follows:

| Finite forms | Occurrences | Required scope |
|---|---:|---|
| **wæs**, **geworhte** | 5 | Prior productive forms. Two **wæs** occurrences still belong to supplied resultative constructions. |
| **Bēon/bēon, tōdǣlen, scīnen, ālīehten, scinen, gīemden, tōdǣlden** | 8 | Actual plural optative/subjunctive forms, supplied with their complete wish/purpose relations. |
| **cwæð, gesętte, geseah** | 3 | Actual indicative forms supplied. Prior appearance of **cwæð/geseah** did not teach their strong systems; **gesęttan** is a new prefixed lemma. |

The two occurrences of **geworden** also remain supplied. No independent mood, strong-preterite, participial, perfect, or productive-prefix rule follows from their appearance.

The construction inventory specifies the following local help:

- **on ðǣre heofonan fæstnesse**: the phrase head **fæstnesse** is dative; **ðǣre heofonan** is embedded genitive feminine singular. Cook §53.3 explicitly distinguishes source weak feminine **heofone** from the previously selected strong masculine **heofon**.
- **on ðǣre heofonan** after **gesętte**: here **heofonan** is dative, despite the identical spelling. The article agrees directly with it.
- **tō** with the actual dative function/purpose phrases: signs, seasons, days, years, and illumination. The earlier supplied inflected-infinitive phrase did not teach this use. The day/night genitive is inside the dative illumination phrase.
- **And gesętte hīe**: “and he set them”; source note 29 refers to Cook §189's omitted-subject note. **hīe** is the accusative object here, contrasting with the nominative subject of the following purpose clause.
- **ðæt hīe scinen ... gīemden ... tōdǣlden**: the complete purpose construction and its three actual past optatives. The purpose sense of **ðæt** is distinct from supplied content “that”.
- **gīemden ðæs dæges and ðǣre niht**: “might rule over the day and the night”; Cook §156.f prints this exact government. The noun/article genitive forms are prior, but verbal genitive government is supplied.
- **ofer eorðan**: supply “over the earth” as a whole. The syncretic singular oblique **eorðan** does not support a uniquely graded accusative-versus-dative parse here.
- The fulfillment, perception-content, and closing resultative clauses: explain each complete relation. New adjective **gōd** or ordinal **fēorða** can be observed within a supplied clause without making all its syntax independently productive.

The source passage is therefore a useful supported reading after the new morphology and controlled reading, with precise grammatical limits. It is not evidence that its supplied verbs or clause systems have become learned material.
