# Supplied giving frame for Unit 1

Coordinator image check, 22 October 2026. These are new teaching sentences, not quotations from the Creation narrative.

Cook, *A First Book in Old English*, 1895 second edition, §117, printed p.72 / PDF page 88, gives **sęllan, give** and the preterite indicative paradigm. Its third-person singular is **sealde**, without a length mark. See [page image](cook-fb-p72.png). The lesson supplies this exact form with “gave”; no productive knowledge of the verb or its conjugation is claimed.

The recipient dative is supported by §160, printed p.93 / PDF109, and §164(a), printed p.94 / PDF110: the former defines the indirect object and giving relationship; the latter explicitly lists verbs of giving or imparting. See [p.93](cook-dative-01.png) and [p.94](cook-dative-02.png). The transferred thing is the direct object, within the separately checked accusative scope.

The proposed sentences **God sealde ðǣm męnn męte** (“God gave the man food”) and **Se mann sealde ðǣm fugle sǣd** (“The man gave the bird seed”) are new composition from the checked noun paradigms. Their word order is a teaching model, not a uniquely obligatory order. Learners select the recipient dative and direct-object accusative; *sealde* remains printed support.

For comparison only, the inspected Creation page 127 / PDF143 contains **ic forgeaf** with source note 11 referring to *for-giefan* ([image](cook-fb-p127.png)). That is first-person singular speech and is not assigned or substituted for the directly verified third-person *sealde* frame. No further Creation passage is assigned by this inspection.
