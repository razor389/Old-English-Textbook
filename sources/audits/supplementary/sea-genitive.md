# The genitive plural of sǣ

Coordinator check, 22 October 2026. This supplements the opening source audit; it does not establish a teaching milestone.

Cook's glossary, *First Book*, printed p.324 / PDF340, gives the singular forms and nominative/accusative and dative plurals of **sǣ**, but no genitive plural. The missing cell must not be filled merely by extending the masculine **-as** pattern.

Joseph Wright and Elizabeth Mary Wright, *Old English Grammar*, second edition (1914), §388, printed p.187 / selected PDF207, explicitly prints **\*sǣwa** as genitive plural. The asterisk is part of the source: this is a reconstructed form, not an attested form certified by this audit. The macron, æ, w, and asterisk were checked against [the rendered page](wright-p187.png). The local PDF is `sources/cache/wright_oe_1914.pdf`; its SHA-256 is recorded in the source registry. PDF pagination in this scan is printed page + 20 here.

The source also gives masculine singular genitive **sǣs**, dative **sǣ**, plural nominative/accusative **sǣs**, and dative **sǣm**, beside **sǣwum**. Cook's selected dative plural **sǣum** is retained; the supplementary source does not authorize a silent dialect or spelling change.

Editorial resolution: retain the complete case/number table, mark the genitive-plural cell **\*sǣwa — reconstructed; not tested**, and assign it supplied/reference status with null recognition and production milestones. Every assigned Cook prompt can be completed without this cell. This is an explicit evidence limit, not a core exercise dependency left unresolved.

Discovery checks: [Bosworth–Toller, sǽ](https://bosworthtoller.com/26140) supplies other case evidence and links Wright §388; [Clark Hall's online dictionary transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/31543/old/files/dict_os.html), printed p.246b, lists an unstarred genitive plural. That transcription was not verified against its scan and does not override Wright's explicit qualification here.
