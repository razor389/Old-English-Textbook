## Findings that govern drafting

- **Breaking:** Cook §21 first says a→ea, then expressly says the immediate input is really **æ**. Its aggregate r+C/l+C/h rule must be narrowed by Fulk: the conditions differ by input vowel. Do not write a universal “all front vowels break before l+C” rule.
- **Umlaut:** §16 identifies following i/j, often later lost or reduced to e. §17 gives exact **dōm/dēman, brūcan/brȳcð, eald/ieldu, weorpan/wierpð**. It expressly says **helpan/hilpð e→i** is historically different even though convenient to group with mutation. Fulk should control the historical distinctions.
- **Ablaut:** §22 explicitly separates it from umlaut. **helpan, healp, hulpon, holpen** are directly verified in §104, p.61/PDF77; §104, p.62/PDF78 repeats the qualification about present raising. A worked comparison with **hilpð** can distinguish inherited grades, later breaking and older raising without inventing an unattested derivation chain.
- **Nouns:** **fōt/fēt** is exact at §46, pp.31–32/PDF47–48; fēt also serves as the selected dative singular. **dæg/dagas** is exact at §43.2, p.28/PDF44, but its historical fronting/restoration explanation must be sourced to Fulk. **hof/hofu** versus **word/word** at §47, p.32/PDF48, shows why syllable weight must be kept distinct from vowel length.
- **Other causes of diphthongs:** §18 gives palatal effects on following vowels; §20 gives back mutation and explicitly says it is not invariable. Neither should be collapsed into breaking. §19 warns that ie/y/i spellings cannot alone determine the original vowel.
- **Lost sounds:** **hēafod/hēafdes** (§23), **feoh/fēos** (§29; §47.5), **sęllan** and **sęcgan** (§36), and **cēosan/coren** (§37) are useful bounded examples. The evidence retains their exact spellings and the conditions or limitations attached to each.
- **Comparative evidence:** Appendix V explicitly warns that Gothic is not the ancestor of Old English. Its **Gothic dōmjan / OE dēman** comparison can show a surviving j without asserting a direct parent-to-child derivation.

## Scope and fidelity

Cook’s compact historical input forms often lack reconstruction stars. They are preserved in the source ledger but should not become unmarked new Old English vocabulary. A selected earlier form must be labelled by language and evidential status. Ordinary Old English outcomes and familiar paradigm comparisons will usually explain enough.

The appendix should not reproduce Cook’s broad pronunciation rules or his Shakespearean drawl analogy as the course reading convention. Smoothing, the chronology of fronting/restoration and high-vowel loss, precise back-mutation conditions, and a Verner’s Law account need Fulk’s separately checked evidence. These are evidence boundaries, not unresolved transcription errors.
