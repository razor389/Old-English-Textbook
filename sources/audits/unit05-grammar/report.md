The four principal-part positions are not four interchangeable tense/person endings. Preterite second singular takes the plural stem plus **-e**. All three preterite plural persons take **-on**. Present second/third singular require the selected lexical and sound changes; a principal-parts list alone would leave those cells unexplained. Eight surface columns compact twelve labelled cells without losing any person/number distinction.

IV has ordinary **e : æ : ǣ : o**, but Cook expressly gives **niman nōm nōmon numen** and **cuman c(w)ōm c(w)ōmon cumen (cymen)**. The selection is nōm/nōmon and cōm/cōmon, with present **cymst, cymð**. Wright's nam/nāmon is a documented alternative, not a reason to silently replace Cook's long-ō row. **birst/birð** is selected; the checked ie spelling is retained as a variant note.

V normally has **e : æ : ǣ : e**. In the palatal row **giefan geaf gēafon giefen**, ie and ea in the present/singular are short, whereas ēa in the plural-based past is long. **cweðan** has **cwist/cwið** and plural-based **cwǣd-**, including second singular cwǣde; its participle is cweden. **biddan, licgan, sittan** retain their different j-present consonants and dental combinations. Cook's licgan **ligst/ligð** is selected; **līst/līð** is documented separately. **etan** selects the expressly documented long **ǣt**; short æt remains valid. **sēon/gesēon** have long ēo in the contracted present1sg/pl, but **short ie** in siehst/siehð and gesiehst/gesiehð. Their selected preterites use **seah/sāwon**, not a newly invented single-vowel past stem.

VI normally has **a : ō : ō : a**, with several non-interchangeable lexical systems. **standan** retains n in present and participle but loses it in **stōd/stōdon**; its selected singular presents are **stęntst/stęnt**. **slēan** has short ie in **sliehst/sliehð**, g in **slōg/slōgon**, and supplied participle **slægen** beside checked slegen. **hębban** has **hęfst/hęfð** and **hōf/hōfon/hafen**. **scieppan/gescieppan** select the printed **scēop/scēopon** alternative to scōp/scōpon, matching the reader's gescēop; the nonfinite citations are sceapen/gesceapen. **swęrian** explicitly has **swęrie, swęrest, swęreð, swęriað**, followed by strong **swōr/swōre/swōr/swōron**, supplied sworen. It is not a weak preterite system.

Cook calls VII “reduplicating”; the course may use VII with that source label recorded. Both preterite stems have long **ēo** or **ē**. Present/participle vowels normally match, with explicit exceptions: **wēpan** has **wōpen**; **fōn** has **fangen**. The full fōn present is **fō/fēhst/fēhð/fōð**. **hēawan** has long **īe** in hīewst/hīewð, whereas feallan/healdan/weaxan have short ie. **weaxan second and third singular are both wiext**, not an analogically invented *wiexð. **lǣtan/hātan** retain selected dental **lǣtt/hǣtt**, with single-t spellings documented as alternatives. Active hātan morphology does not teach the separate hātte system or the reading's naming construction.

The supplied **weaxen** participle is a declared rule-derived citation. Cook §109 p66 explicitly places weaxan “like feallan,” whose printed participle is feallen, following §108's present/participle-vowel rule. Wright §516 p264 likewise places weaxan with fealdan/fealden. Neither page prints an isolated whole-form weaxen, so the machine inventory does not label it individually attested there. These rules support the selected ea spelling. This audit has not established a competing **waxen** citation from the selected page images and does not list or condemn it as a checked variant.

The exercise-dependent **geswīcan, scīnan, flēogan, swimman** are complete new lexical systems applying the prior I–III patterns. Previous supplied geswāc or scīnen/scinen does not establish independent control. Cook Exercises explicitly assigns each lemma to its class. **flēogan** selects **flīehst/flīehð** with long īe and g/h alternation, and has **flēag/fluge/flēag/flugon**. **swimman** selects the nasal III **swamm/swumme/swamm/swummon**, supplied swummen, transparently expanded from Cook's class rule. BT Main957a actually prints an alternative **swom/swommon**; that is recorded, not misquoted as evidence for swamm.

Cook's allowance for full -est/-eð spellings and the historically variable syncopated spellings is not a license to mark all other documented language wrong. The selected rows are finite recall targets. A defensible documented alternative should be distinguished from failure to meet an expressly stated target. The audit does not generate every conceivable full-ending, regional or late analogical form.

## Attestation, normalization and register boundaries

The twelve-cell records deliberately identify their origin as **new teaching material generated from cited grammatical evidence**. A principal-parts spelling printed in Cook is not automatically an independently attested first-person or third-person token. Exact printed rows are separately transcribed; explicit source tokens and lexical claims are separately located. This conservative distinction also covers prefixed finite expansions and analogous participles such as weaxen.

The current display **gesēon** is supported by Cook's unambiguous vocabulary **ge-sēon**, p294/PDF310. The declared presentation change removes the dictionary compound hyphen and supplies the checked quantity to the formerly unmarked active headword display `geseon`. The lemma ID remains `geseon`; historical supplied display and short **geseah** remain documented. This is not a silent global spelling normalization. **gescieppan** likewise preserves Cook's short ie and independently documented lexical identity.

Existing finite IDs are retained for gescēop, cwæð, geseah, hēt and geswāc. **cuman-pret-3-pl-source** and **standan-pret-3-sg-source** remain the actual cell IDs; unsuffixed canonical aliases are recorded for integration. Ordinary standing receives the new sense ID **standan-ordinary**, distinct from the existing **standan-stand** supplied opposition sense with ongēan. Displaying a new ordinary standing paradigm does not independently teach that older complement frame.

## Ordinary use and exercise/reading scope

`lexical-frames.json` provides image-supported carry/take + accusative; give + accusative thing and dative recipient; eat + food accusative or eat without an expressed object; sit/lie/stand and fall without an object; strike and hold + accusative. Eating food and giving food to a bird or person are declared ordinary lexical applications, not pretended quotations of new classroom sentences. Prior location syntax may accompany the posture verbs. Selected slēan means **strike/smite**; killing is not entailed by every occurrence of this frame. Ordinary drinking retains the previously established drincan frame and needs no new milestone here.

The source audit supplies the independent prefixed create/see identities and ordinary seeing + accusative; its BT ge-sēon entry additionally establishes absolute use. Intransitive literal flying, shining and swimming have separate primary dictionary evidence, with bird, star and fish/person uses bounded explicitly. **geswīcan + genitive** is new production: Cook §156k p92/PDF108 prints geswāc his weorces. No English “from” preposition should be inserted by mechanical translation of that frame. The remaining verbs may be practised through labelled morphology without introducing unverified complements.

The complete I.5 set requires these new systems alongside already taught weak verbs and nominal material. It need not be broken apart to avoid the difficult items. **eall ðā ðing** remains an exact supplied phrase; no unrestricted eall production follows. First-day Creation finite promotions and the second-day reading reuse cwæð/hēt, while clause complements, creation commands, naming double accusatives and other supplied syntax remain separately scaffolded. This audit did not inspect task answer witnesses, the draft key, or source-audit reading translations.
