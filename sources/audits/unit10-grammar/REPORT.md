## Source findings requiring visible treatment

1. **Cook §153d, printed 89 / PDF 105, correction C02.** Cook calls ęgesan the objective genitive in Frēan ęgesan. The dependent objective genitive is Frēan: the Lord is the object of fear. Retain the original printing and correct the analysis. Fulk’s inspected p17 independently explains the corresponding objective relation in metodes eġe. The correction is an analytical correction, not a change to the Old English quotation.

2. **Unnan has a checked-source gap.** Cook p77 §129, Wright p284 §542, and Fulk pp89–90 §123 do not supply a present indicative second singular. The display therefore has a nonlexical dash and no recall target for that cell. This is a statement about the checked sources, not universal nonattestation. Cook explicitly labels unne “Imper.”; Fulk explains subjunctive substitution. Both descriptions are preserved. Direct singular “Grant” binds the second-person subjunctive ID, not the third-person same-surface cell. No anst, unnest, or unnað is invented.

3. **The right-hand expression is not explained solely by motion.** Cook p134 has on his swīðran hand both with placement and, in the next unassigned sentence, with standað. The selected bounded on … hand idiom uses accusative even in the static context. The bare hand supports that analysis; weak swīðran alone does not distinguish cases. Cook §172.1 explicitly cautions that the usual rest/motion distinction is not invariant. Ordinary on plus dative location remains a separate construction.

4. **Genēosian takes an accusative person in the chosen frame.** Cook’s mē/ðē examples alone are ambiguous between dative and accusative. The inspected BT main p421 has two unambiguous hine examples. This establishes the prefixed visit frame without invalidating genitive government of nēosian. Cook’s marked genēosian spelling is retained; BT’s eó notation is not silently substituted.

5. **The two experiencer systems must remain distinct.** Hine hyngrede has an accusative experiencer and no nominative subject. Mē ðyncð ðis gōd has a dative experiencer and an overt nominative stimulus. The dictionaries also document other uses, which are outside these selected two-cell systems. The earlier Unit 9 geðūht judgment/passive source expression remains supplied; its scope and corrected nominative token are not generalized or reverted.

6. **The remaining exercise frames are explicit.** Hīeran plus accusative actor plus plain infinitive is a new lexical extension, independently supported by Cook §169. Perceived-object adjective agreement is separately supported by §173. Tōgelǣdan explicitly promotes the prior supplied bring/recipient frame and adds its complete indicative. Wyrcean gains the ordinary work/perform sense and both commands. Scrȳdan’s productive scope is accusative person; the dictionary garment complement is reference evidence only. Dōn plus accusative deed and dative beneficiary, on … naman, destination tō plus dative, future reference of present indicative, and bounded coordinated-predicate ellipsis are individually recorded.

7. **Harder source syntax remains supported.** Cook III.14.11 receives local sęllan plus dative person plus plain drincan help. III.15.15 receives the whole swā lange swā correlation, including both swā senses and lange. Cook p135 note17 explicitly observes “inasmuch as” force; duration must not be imposed. The selected Judgment passage’s two ðonne tokens are sequencing adverbs, not temporal subordinators. Source-only forms, comparison, passive, and quality-genitive help remain in the source editor’s bounded records.
