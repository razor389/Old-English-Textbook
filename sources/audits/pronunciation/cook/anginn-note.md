# Anginn: bounded follow-up

**No noun-specific consonant value is certified by this inspection.** The learner draft’s hard **g** claim lacked sufficient evidence. The primary stress of **anginn** also cannot be settled by transferring the marked stress of **onginn** without examining the prefix variant. [Full locators, images and source identities](anginn-evidence.json) preserve the findings.

Cook’s Reader I, printed 123 / PDF 139, has **On anginne gescēop God heofonan and eorðan.** His vocabulary, printed 276 / PDF 292, gives **an-ginn (ongin)** and the sense “beginning”. Neither location marks the consonant quality or primary stress. Cook’s §23, printed 18–19, contains no anginn stress example.

Wright and Wright’s *Old English Grammar*, second edition (1914), §11, printed 16–17 / PDF 36–37, explicitly has **ongínn** with root stress and relates it directly to **ongínnan**. But §568, printed 299 / PDF 319, explicitly calls **an-** the stressed form of the preposition **on** and includes **anginn** as an example. The two passages require care about the an-/on- variants; the former does not alone certify primary root stress for the latter.

Henry Sweet’s *The Student’s Dictionary of Anglo-Saxon* (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1897), printed 11 / PDF 33, third column, has **an-ginn** with no later-stress marker. His notation key, [xii]–xiii / PDF 18–19, says that unmarked hyphenated words generally have first-syllable stress and explicitly marks later stress, as in **be·cuman**. Applying that convention supports first-syllable stress in his entry, consistent with Wright §568. Sweet’s undotted **g** does not settle its pronunciation: his entries do not supply a usable dotted/undotted contrast here.

The earlier Unit 1 wording about the “stressed stem -ginn” is not independent evidence and does not itself distinguish main from secondary stress. The related verbs marked in Fulk may support further analysis, but their consonants have not been silently substituted for a noun-specific witness. Any n+y or affricate interpretation would need to be identified as an inference and independently reviewed.

An indexed example in Melinda Mix’s 2007 MA thesis was investigated. Its university metadata was verified, but the actual PDF page could not be retrieved: the official endpoint returned an access challenge, and the CiteSeer mirror led to an unavailable archived URL. No claim from its search excerpt has been promoted to checked evidence. The Wiktionary pronunciation was likewise used only as a finding aid.

The coordinator has chosen to remove **anginn/anginne** from the guide’s pronunciation targets and use the declared adaptation **God gescēop heofonan and eorðan.** This deletes initial **On anginne** and moves **God** before **gescēop**; Cook’s original is preserved above. The root agent owns the contract amendment, replacement D3 task, learner/key edits and final review. This audit makes no change to the existing Unit 1 contract or registers.
