For long strong feminines, N/A plural **-e** is a source-listed alternative to selected **-a**. The selected -nes forms double s in the plural too. The tables do not imply every listed mass-noun plural or special sense should be tested independently.

The inherited record's classification of **wyrt** under general §51.b is inadequate for its newly taught accusative: the newly inspected p324 lexical cross-reference and p125 reading establish endingless **wyrt**. This is an expansion of evidence, not a change to the exact old nominative.

The **sǣ** genitive plural deserves explicit treatment. Cook's p324 note supplies the singular and N/A/D plural forms but no G plural. The coordinator located and the source editor independently inspected [Wright & Wright (1914), p.187/PDF207, §388](../supplementary/wright-p187.png): it prints **\*sǣwa**, with an actual star. Preserve this evidential status. A full display may show “\*sǣwa (reconstructed; not tested)” or explicitly mark the cell as unattested in the checked evidence. No Exercise1–3 item requires it. Its production and recognition milestones must remain null; do not silently publish **sǣa** or unstarred **sǣwa** as a verified learner form. Cook and [Bosworth–Toller sǽ](https://bosworthtoller.com/26140) corroborate the other sea forms and valid gender variation.

**Niht** does not acquire an invented vowel mark or arbitrary *nieht* target. Its genitive **niht** is printed in the selected reader and explained by note28; the datsg and N/A pl use the no-ending root pattern, with **nihta/nihtum** in G/D plural. [Bosworth–Toller niht](https://bosworthtoller.com/23718) additionally corroborates **nihte** in genitive/dative contexts and **nihta/nihtum**. These are valid variants, not a requirement to discard Cook's selected form.

## Basic uses and the guided reading

Fresh nominal forms still need practice and syntax evidence. FB §40 p27/PDF43 verifies direct object accusative and indirect object dative in general. Inherited checked images verify subject/predicate nominatives at §149–150 p88/PDF104. For more precise constructions the analyst should use §153 pp89–90/PDF105–106 (genitives), §166 p95/PDF111 (prepositional dative), §168 p96/PDF112 (transitive accusative), §172 p96–97/PDF112–113 (prepositions), §173 p97/PDF113 (two accusatives), and §189 p102/PDF118 (agreement and understood subject). These locators were found in OCR; unless a matching image is already checked elsewhere, they are not newly image-certified by this paragraph. The reading's own checked notes independently identify several of these uses.

Do not reduce **ofer** to “always dative.” In the first-day source it governs dative **brādnesse**, identified by n10 → §166, and accusative plural **wæteru**, overt in the noun ending. A guided reading must explain each actual phrase. **On anginne** and **fram ðǣm ðīestrum** are useful dative examples. If new accusative practice uses supplied **gescēop** or **geseah**, label these finite forms as supplied frames and test the taught case choice, not an unexplained verb conjugation.
