## Explicit source notes and audit consequences

| Source note | Checked content or reference | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| p123 n1 | gescieppan and §18 | **gescēop**, past third singular, requires exact supplied help unless that verb system is taught. |
| p123 n2 | Order probably determined by Latin *creavit Deus* | The opening has verb–subject order after a preposed phrase. Do not impose English SVO parsing. |
| p123 n3 | §53.3 | **heofonan** is weak feminine singular accusative from **heofone**, not a plural of masculine **heofon**. |
| p123 n5 | Latin *autem* | **sōðlice** is connective “now/for its part” here. |
| p123 n7 | Plural, like Latin *tenebrae* | **ðīestru** is plural and agrees with **wǣron**. |
| p123 n8 | Governs **brādnesse** | **ofer** governs the head “surface”; the deep is a genitive dependent. |
| p123 n9 | Genitive dependent on **brādnesse**; §153.i | The phrase contains nested genitive **ðǣre nēowolnesse**. The article belongs to the genitive dependent. |
| p123 n10 | §166 | **brādnesse** is dative singular after spatial **ofer**. |
| p123 n11 | **wæs gefęred** = Latin *ferebatur*; gefęrian | Supply passive idiom “was borne/carried,” with movement over the waters. “Moved” is a natural contextual rendering; do not turn it into an independent active verb paradigm. |
| p123 n12 | wæter, §§47.1,6 | **wæteru** is N/A plural; here governed by **ofer**, accusative by form. This differs from preceding **ofer + dative**. |
| p123 n14 | geweorðan; §193.a | **Geweorðe** is a third-singular present optative/subjunctive wish/command, “let … come to be.” |
| p123 n16 | **wearð geworht** = Latin *facta est*; gewyrcean | Supply whole passive verbal phrase “was made/came into being.” |
| p123 n17–18 | geseon; hē | **geseah** “saw”; **hit** refers to neuter light. |
| p124 n2 | gedǣlan | **gedǣlde**, past third singular “divided/separated.” |
| p124 n5 | hātan; §189 note | **hēt**, past third singular “called/named”; subject **God/hē** is understood from the prior clause. Two accusatives name an object. |
| p124 n6 | **wæs geworden** = Latin *factum est*; geweorðan | Supply whole resultative/perfect phrase “had come to be/there came to be.” Do not equate it mechanically with the earlier passive. |
| p124 n7 | Latin *dies unus* | **ān dæg** is “one day” in the first-day closing formula. Do not silently rewrite it as the ordinal “first.” |
