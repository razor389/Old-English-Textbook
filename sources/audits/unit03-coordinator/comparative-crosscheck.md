## Evidence inspected

- Cook, *A First Book in Old English*, second edition (1895), §§35, 55, 60 and 63–66: printed pp.24–25, 38, 40–43; one-based PDF pp.40–41, 54, 56–59. Images: [p.25](../unit02-grammar/cook-fb-p25.png), [p.38](../unit03-grammar/cook-fb-p38.png), [p.40](../unit03-grammar/cook-fb-p40.png), [p.41](../unit03-grammar/cook-fb-p41.png), [p.42](../unit03-grammar/cook-fb-p42.png), [p.43](../unit03-grammar/cook-fb-p43.png).
- Wright, *Old English Grammar* (1914), §259.2–4, printed p.130 / one-based PDF150: [page image](wright-p130-pdf150.png). The heading continues §259; the printed §260 begins lower on this page.

Cook prescribes weak comparative inflection. His ordinary weak genitive plural has **-ra**, with **-ena** an expressly permitted less usual ending. His **gearu → gearura → gearra** example illustrates reduction when compounding comparative and genitive endings would create excessive repeated consonants; it does not authorize printing every comparative stem followed mechanically by **-ra**.

Wright §259.2 describes simplification of doubled consonants before another consonant; §259.3 also describes simplification after another consonant. These are distinct environments. §259.4 documents later simplification in unstressed syllables, including **ōðerra → ōðera**. Cook §35 independently prints **ōðerra**, **ōðera** and **ōðra**. None of these variants is evidence that the selected baseline must silently lose every double consonant.

For **lǣssa**, the mechanically assembled **lǣssra** raises a separate before-consonant simplification question. Historical **sr → ss** cannot be applied recursively as a universal spelling algorithm. No exact attestation of its **-ra** genitive plural was established by this crosscheck. Cook's expressly permitted **-ena** ending licenses rule-derived **lǣssena** without that extra assumption; selection belongs to the reconciled source audit and contract. A derived cell must be labelled as derived, even when its underlying rules are explicit.
