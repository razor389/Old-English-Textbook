## Complete adjective system and presentation

The selected positive lemmas are **gōd** good, **glæd** glad, **micel** great/large, **īdel** empty/void, **ǣmtig** empty/void, **æppelbǣre** fruit-bearing, **lȳtel** little/small, **styriendlic** moving, **lēof** dear, **eald** old, **yfel** bad/evil, **gearu** ready, and **hēah** high. The glosses of the pattern examples are printed in Cook's grammatical examples or Wright's paradigm headings, not inferred from their morphology. Cook's vocabulary independently checks the less transparent exercise words. The dictionary's **æppel-bǣre** is normalized only by removing its lexical compound hyphen. **Styriendlic** is a lexical adjective, not an assignment in participle formation.

Present complete singular/plural nominative, genitive, dative and accusative systems for all three genders. A full **gōd** table, **glæd** table, **æppelbǣre** table and a syncopating disyllable table may establish the shared endings, provided the vocabulary/stem crosswalk supplies every differing form of the other assigned adjectives. The complete weak paradigm may likewise be shared, with explicit weak stems and genitive-plural selections. Do not replace the whole system with just nominative examples.

The important alternations belong with those tables:

| Pattern | Selected diagnostic forms | Basis and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Short stem with æ/a | **glæd; glades; gladu; glædne; glædre; glædra** | Cook §57 full paradigm. The vowel changes before the appropriate vowel endings, not everywhere. Weak **glada/gladan**, but Gpl **glædra**. |
| Long stem | **gōd**, feminine Nsg and neuter N/Apl **gōd** | Cook §58 removes the short-stem **-u**. Its other cells follow the expressly shared pattern. |
| Final **-e** | **æppelbǣre; æppelbǣru; æppelbǣrne; æppelbǣrre; æppelbǣrra** | Cook §59. Retain headword **-e** only where another ending is absent; do not infer a headword without **-e** from its accusative. |
| Disyllabic syncope | **micel/micles/miclum/micelne/micelre/micelra; miclu** | Cook §57.3; Wright §§223, 429–431. The reader directly attests accusative neuter plural **miclu**. Syncope before vowel endings does not authorize deleting the medial vowel before every consonant ending. |
| Other disyllables | **īdel/īdles/īdelne; lȳtel/lȳtles/lȳtelne; lȳtlu** | Complete selected rows are in JSON. **īdel** and **ǣmtig** feminine predicates are exact first-day forms; they remain valid despite the usual **-u** pattern. Selected **ǣmtig-** retains medial **i** in the oblique forms. |
| Final **-u/-w** | **gearu; gearwes; gearwum; gearone; gearore; gearora; gearwa** | Wright §435 supplies the complete strong table; §440 gives weak **gearwa/gearwe**. The **o** before consonants is part of the selected checked pattern. |
| **h**-loss and contraction | **hēah; hēas; hēaum; hēanne; hēa; hēarre; hēarra** | Cook §58.1; Wright §427 complete table. Weak **hēa/hēan**, not mechanically **hēaha** or **hēaa**. Simpler **hēam/hēane/hēare/hēara** are recorded valid variants. |
| Doubled consonants | **eall/ealles**, but **ealne/ealre/ealra** | Cook §58.2; Wright §259. **Eall** is a new-recognition morphology reference, strong-only, with no independently taught “all” construction. The same rule prevents mechanical triple consonants in derived cells. |

The source allows variants, including retained medial vowels and optional endingless feminine/neuter-plural forms in disyllables. The inventory chooses one recoverable baseline and lists relevant alternatives. It does not assert that every possible patterned variant has been individually found in a text. In particular, selected rare **-u** possessive cells below are openly rule-derived.

Strong adjectives are used in ordinary predication and in unmarked attributive use. An ordinary adjective following a demonstrative/article is weak. Comparatives are weak even in predication; ordinals are weak except **ōðer**; attributive superlatives are usually weak. Wright §421 explicitly supports strong positive and superlative predication. Ordinary agreement is in gender, number and case. The special mixed-gender animate predicate convention, substantive adjectives and vocatives are not independently assigned here.

**Possessives do not impose one universal rule on the following ordinary adjective.** Cook §55 says weak is used *sometimes* after a possessive. Fulk §56 and Wright §421 use a broader summary associating possessives/definiteness with weak forms. They are not used to erase Cook's stated alternative. The lesson should permit the checked strong/weak choice or state an explicit requested target. For a nested practice phrase, the article-explicit structure **on ðǣm miclan hofe ðæs gōdan mannes** avoids making an unsupported general rule about limiting genitives.

Instrumental masculine/neuter singular forms are visible reference/recognition cells. No instrumental means, manner, measure-of-comparison or prepositional construction is thereby taught. Weak instrumental forms coincide with the corresponding dative forms; no separate productive instrumental task is licensed.

The selected complete degree series are:

| Positive | Comparative | Strong superlative citation | Weak masculine Nsg |

| lēof | lēofra | lēofost | lēofosta |
| eald | ieldra | ieldest | ieldesta |
| gōd | bętera | bętst | bętsta |
| yfel | wiersa | wierrest | wierresta |
| micel | māra | mǣst | mǣsta |
| lȳtel | lǣssa | lǣst | lǣsta |

Cook §§63–66 supplies the series. **Ieldra/ieldest** have no added macron. A full comparative weak table plus the selected comparative stems is sufficient if its genitive plurals are explicit: **lēofra, ieldra, bęterra, wiersena, mārra, lǣssena**. The first two reduce doubled **r** after another consonant under Wright §259.3; **bęterra/mārra** retain intervocalic **rr**. For **wiersa/lǣssa**, the selected **-ena** is Cook's expressly admitted weak alternative. It avoids claiming an unverified genitive plural through recursive historical assimilation. These completions are derived forms, not quoted full paradigms. Other defensible source variants must not be rejected simply because they are unselected targets.

Cook's other umlaut series, defective comparisons, spatial/temporal comparatives and **-mest** families remain reference/later material. Their omission from productive vocabulary does not imply regular **-ra/-ost** can replace their lexical forms. The ordinary regular and umlaut mechanisms and all four principal irregular families are represented above.

For bounded comparisons, Fulk §35 prints **“Sē wæs betere þonne iċ!”**, attributed there to Beowulf 469. The nominative **iċ** is explicit evidence for a comparison between two subjects with the repeated copula unexpressed. Retain Fulk's printed **betere** in the source note; it is not the course's selected masculine comparative form. New composition may use **A is greater than B**, with both contrasted subjects nominative, as an adaptation of that construction. Do not generalize this into a claim that every phrase after **ðonne** takes nominative.

Assigned adverbs are **wīde/wīdor/wīdost** widely/more widely/most widely, **wel/bęt/bętst** well/better/best, **swīðe** very, and **sōðlice** indeed/truly. Cook §§70, 76–77 and 182 provides formation, degree and ordinary modification; Wright §§555–556 explicitly supplies **wel/bet/betst**. **Wīdor/wīdost** are transparent completions of the checked regular rule. In **bętst**, Wright's adverb spelling is aligned with Cook's printed hooked vowel in §66; this is declared normalization, not inferred vowel length.

Cook's vocabulary confirms the glosses at pp.313, 315, 321–322. **Wīd, swīð, sōð** are supplied derivational bases only, not additional productive adjective paradigms. Cook prints **sōðlice** without a macron over suffix **i**; preserve that baseline. The connective **sōðlice** glossed by Latin *autem* in the Creation reader is separately supplied; learning “truly” does not silently teach that connective use. Adverbs are indeclinable and can qualify verbs, adjectives or adverbs; the selected **swīðe gōd** use is supported by §182.

## Agreeing possessives

The six new adjective lemmas are **min-poss, thin-poss, uncer-poss, incer-poss, ure-poss, eower-poss**. They must remain distinct from the personal-pronoun genitive records. **Mīn/ðīn** use the complete strong long-stem endings. The possessed noun determines agreement; the number of possessors selects **mīn/ūre/uncer** or **ðīn/ēower/incer**. Invariant **his/hiere/hiera** remain the prior Unit 2 system. **Sīn** and emphatic **self** are not assigned.

Wright §464 explicitly classes **ēower/uncer/incer** with **hālig** (§429). That source linkage, with the checked syncope rules, yields:

| Lemma | F.Nsg / N.NApl | M/N.Gsg | M/N.Dsg | M.Asg | F.G/Dsg | Gpl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ēower | ēowru | ēowres | ēowrum | ēowerne | ēowerre | ēowerra |
| uncer | uncru | uncres | uncrum | uncerne | uncerre | uncerra |
| incer | incru | incres | incrum | incerne | incerre | incerra |

The rare **ēowru/uncru/incru** cells select the syncopated **hālgu**-type alternative; retained **ēoweru/unceru/inceru** are valid by the same explicit classification. These are **pattern-derived**, not individually attested in this audit. The complete JSON includes the other cases and numbers. Do not silently remove the medial **e** before consonantal endings or halve **rr** in the selected targets; source-supported simplified variants are recorded separately.

**Ūre** follows **wilde**, except Wright explicitly prescribes feminine Nsg **ūre**, not **\*ūru**. Thus selected M/N.Gsg **ūres**, Dsg **ūrum**, M.Asg **ūrne**, F.G/Dsg **ūrre**, Gpl **ūrra**; neuter N/Apl **ūru** follows the cited pattern, with the **-e** alternative **ūre** admitted. Source simplification **ūrre/ūrra → ūre/ūra** is valid but unselected. All six full NGDA tables, including plural agreement, are present in the inventory.

## Numerals and demonstratives

The bounded productive range is cardinal **1–12** and ordinal **first–seventh**. The high-resolution Cook p.46 image verifies **ān, twēgen/twā/tū, ðrīe/ðrēo, fēower, fīf, siex, seofon, eahta, nigon, tīen, endlefan, twęlf**. Do not add a macron to **endlefan** or substitute an unhooked **e** in selected **twęlf**.

**Ān** follows the full strong paradigm. Its singular cardinal use is productive; plural morphology is recognition/reference only and does not license “one” with plural count nouns. Weak **āna** means “alone” and is an unassigned distinct sense. **Twēgen** and **ðrīe** have complete plural gender/case tables; selected **twēgra/twǣm** admits Cook's **twēga/twām**, and neuter **twā** admits **tū**. Selected dative of three is **ðrīm**. Four through twelve are used uninflected before plural count nouns, following Wright §452. Postposed/substantival numeral inflection, partitive government, decades, hundreds and fractions remain outside the unit.

Selected ordinals are **ǣresta, ōðer, ðridda, fēorða, fīfta, siexta, seofoða**. The full **ōðer** strong table is necessary: its feminine nominative target **ōðru** permits **ōðer/ōðeru** as source-rule variants. It remains strong after an article, including the second night in Cook I.4.12. All other selected ordinals use complete weak endings. **Ǣresta** avoids the unneeded quantity question in the Exercises glossary's alternate “first” entry; no source-error claim is made about that entry.

The existing **se/sēo/ðæt** article forms gain a bounded demonstrative “that/those” use; the original form IDs are reused. Cook notes emphatic masculine **sē**, retained as an alternative. **Ðes/ðēos/ðis** has a complete new paradigm, with selected **ðisses/ðissum/ðisse/ðissa**. Cook p.51 clearly prints **ðes** with short **e**. Instrumentals **ðȳ/ðon** and **ðȳs** remain recognition forms without new instrumental syntax. Relative uses, **ilca/self**, and unrestricted pronoun-like discourse uses are deferred.
