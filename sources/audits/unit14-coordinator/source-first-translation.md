## Independent rendering

Every high honour here in the world is surrounded by dangers: just as worldly dignities grow greater, so the dangers grow fiercer. We can recognize and understand this from these examples. The tree, then, which grows in the forest grove so that it towers above all the other trees and spreads itself out—when a storm suddenly arises, and the strong wind, it is shaken and battered more violently than the rest of the forest. It is likewise with the high cliffs and crags: when they tower far above the rest of the earth, they suddenly begin to fall and crash very violently to the ground. Likewise the high mountains and hills which stand high above the whole world nevertheless pay the penalty for that pre-eminence: they are often threatened and oppressed by fire from heaven and struck by flames. So the high powers here in the world decline and fall and come to ruin; this world's wealth turns to sorrow, and these earthly wonders come to nothing.

## Points requiring exact learner support

- **Ǣghwilc … swā … swā**: the initial universal is not evidence that every indefinite family was previously taught. The proportional comparison must receive local support or explicit new teaching; the later Unit 15 comparison remit remains intact.
- **be ðǣm … bȳsnum**: the relation means recognizing something by/from these examples. It is not automatically licensed by a previously spatial preposition.
- **ðæt … brǣt hit**: the tree spreads itself; the source's result relation and reflexive object must remain clear.
- **gestęnt … and se stranga wind**: both storm and wind belong to the source predicate; retain Cook's note rather than silently repairing the singular verb.
- **se ōðer wudu**: the rest of the forest, not an unrelated second wood. The source explicitly warns against the latter reading.
- **torrum**: Cook marks the contextual crag interpretation as probable. A key must not make a unique literal interpretation more certain than its evidence.
- **feallan … hrēosan**: both infinitives depend on **onginnað**. English may naturally retain or reduce their rhetorical overlap, but the syntactic relation must be explicit.
- **ðā-hwæðre … ðæs ealdordōmes**: the mountains' prominence has a penalty. The lexical meaning of **ealdordōm** is contextual; a mechanical modern office-title gloss would mislead.
- **heofonfȳre … līegum**: the compound's literal analysis does not itself identify every referent; here the context indicates lightning/fire from heaven and flames. Keep lexical and contextual explanation separate.
- **þisse worulde welan**: **ðisse worulde** is genitive dependent on **welan**, as Cook's note says. Do not let the surface agreement suggest a dative phrase.
- **tō lore … tō sorge … tō nāhte**: the three outcome expressions need actual forms and bounded construction support. The final form does not teach the whole negative-pronoun family.
