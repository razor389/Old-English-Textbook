# C01: the quantity of wæstm

The selected Cook editions print **wǣstm**. The course uses **wæstm**, with short æ, as an explicit editorial correction. Exact source transcriptions retain the macron; learner forms and derived inflections use the declared corrected stem. This is a quantity correction, not a change of lexical sense or inflectional class.

Merja Stenroos, “Nu is þeo Leore For-Leten: Conventionality, Complexity and Substitution Sets in Historical English Spelling” (2024), printed p. 118 / PDF p. 10, Table 1, places **wæstm** among lexemes with Old English short /æ/. The coordinator personally inspected the retained [page image](stenroos-2024-p118.png) during this presentation revision. The full article and its identity are registered under `stenroos_2024` in the [source registry](../../registry.json).

The retained Cook witnesses are *Exercises*, printed p. 48 / PDF p. 56, entry “fruit”; *First Book*, Reader I printed p. 125 / PDF p. 141; and its glossary, printed p. 320 / PDF p. 336. These establish that Cook's spelling is consistent across the selected books, so the correction is not described as an isolated printer error. The primary images are [Exercises p. 48](cook-ex-p48.png), [Reader p. 125](cook-fb-p125.png), and [glossary p. 320](cook-fb-p320.png).

[Baker's glossary](https://glossary.oldenglishaerobics.net/oeaginfo.php?objid=xwaestm&objtype=gentry) and [Bosworth–Toller](https://bosworthtoller.com/34463) provide additional lexical support. The correction does not imply that every other spelling in Cook should be regularized. Teaching scope remains governed by the active unit contracts.
