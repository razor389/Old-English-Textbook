## NEW-U10-S01

The Son of Man comes in glory; all the angels come together with him. All nations are gathered before him. He then separates the nations into two groups, compared with a shepherd separating sheep from goats, and places the sheep at his right and goats at his left.

The clause boundaries are:

1. **Witodlice Mannes Bearn cymð on his mægenðrymme**. Mannes Bearn is the subject of the overt finite cymð. **And ealle ęnglas samod mid him tō ðǣm miclan dōme** adds a different nominative plural subject with its coming predicate understood; mid governs him, not ęnglas.
2. **Ðonne sitt hē on ðǣm setle his mægenðrymnesse**. Hē, the Son of Man, sits on the throne.
3. **And bēoð gegaderode ætforan him ealle ðēoda**. The following nominative plural ealle ðēoda is the subject of the plural passive predicate bēoð gegaderode.
4. **And hē tōscǣt hīe on twā**. Hē separates the accusative plural hīe, referring to those nations.
5. **Swā-swā scēaphierde tōscǣt scēap fram gātum**. The supplied comparison is a subordinate clause with its own nominative subject scēaphierde and finite tōscǣt.
6. **Ðonne gelōgað hē ðā scēap on his swīðran hand, and ðā gǣt on his winstran**. The Son of Man places both animal groups; the final coordinated part shares the placing predicate and leaves hand understood.

There are six overt finite predicates. The angels' coming is recovered from the initial predicate, with the agreement required by their own plural subject if expressed.

Swā-swā means “just as” and introduces the comparative clause about a shepherd. It relates the main separation to that comparison and has its own finite clause. Its whole use is supplied by the reading help.

## NEW-U10-S04

Now the Son of Man will come in his glory, and all the angels will come together with him to the great judgment. Then he will sit on the throne of his majesty, and all nations will be gathered before him, and he will separate them into two groups, just as a shepherd separates sheep from goats. Then he will place the sheep at his right hand and the goats at his left.

“Glory” can also render mægenðrymnesse in the throne phrase. The present forms are rendered with future reference appropriate to the announced judgment; the shepherd comparison states a general action. The final hand is understood in the Old English and recovered in English. The printed connective help permits “Now”; no unstated temporal when-clause has been supplied.
