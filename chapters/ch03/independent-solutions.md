## Adverbs

### NEW-U03-V01

Wīde, wīdor, wīdost; sōðlice. None of these adverbs changes for gender, case or number. Wīde is formed in -e and sōðlice in -lice; the regular degree endings are -or/-ost.

### NEW-U03-V02

Ðā męnn geworhton ðæt gōde hof wel.

Gōde is a weak neuter accusative singular adjective modifying hof, after ðæt. Wel modifies the action geworhton and is invariant.

### NEW-U03-V03

Hē geworhte ðæt hof bęt. Hē geworhte ðæt hof bętst.

The invariant comparative/superlative adverbs describe how he built, not a property agreeing with hof. Neither receives a neuter accusative adjective ending.

### NEW-U03-V04

“Truly/indeed, that work is very good.” The shorter sentence is “That work is good.” Sōðlice qualifies the assertion as truly/indeed; swīðe intensifies gōd. Neither is an adjective agreeing with weorc. Reading ðæt as the article “the” instead of pointing “that” is also possible in these isolated sentences.

## Controlled reading

### NEW-U03-L01

The good men built two dwellings. One dwelling was large, and one dwelling was small. The large dwelling was larger than the small dwelling. They gave the large dwelling to the old man. He had three birds. He gave his birds seed and water. The birds lived in the large dwelling. The man said: “My birds are good. Our work is good.”

The men are the builders and givers of the dwelling. The old man receives it, owns the birds and gives them their food/water. In the quotation he speaks of his own birds; “our” includes him and all the builders, as the orientation specifies.

## Fourth-day source reading

### NEW-U03-R01

God then said, “Let there now be lights in the firmament of heaven, and let them separate day and night, and let them be for signs, for seasons, for days and for years. And let them shine in the firmament of heaven and illuminate the earth”; it then came to pass thus. And God made two great lights: the greater light for the illumination of the day, and the lesser light for the illumination of the night; and he made stars. And he set them in heaven, so that they might shine over the earth, and rule over the day and the night, and separate light and darkness; God then saw that it was good. And evening and morning came to pass, the fourth day.

The opening sōðlice is the supplied connective, not an extra assertion that God spoke “truthfully”. The plural wishes, their fulfilment, both assigned illumination functions, the three purpose verbs, God's seeing, and the closing day formula are all retained. The text does not explicitly name the sun or moon.

### NEW-U03-R02

Twā, miclu and lēoht are all neuter accusative plural, the object of geworhte. Headwords: twēgen, micel, lēoht. Miclu is a strong adjective in this phrase without an article/demonstrative: the selected stem micl- has lost the medial e and takes the neuter direct-plural -u. Lēoht is a count noun with an endingless plural, not a singular noun despite its spelling.

### NEW-U03-R03

Māre is the comparative of micel; lǣsse is the comparative of lȳtel. Each is weak neuter accusative singular, agreeing with its individual lēoht in the apposition to the two lights. The greater light is for the illumination of the day; the lesser is for the illumination of the night. These are the supplied purposes, not names of celestial bodies.

### NEW-U03-R04

In ðæt māre lēoht and ðæt lǣsse lēoht, ðæt is the neuter accusative singular determiner of the noun phrase. Before hīe scinen, ðæt introduces the supplied purpose clause “so that they might shine”. Before hit gōd wæs, it introduces the content seen, “that it was good”. The latter two are conjunctions, not inflected determiners agreeing with a following noun.

### NEW-U03-R05

Both geworhte forms are third-person singular preterite indicative of gewyrcean. The first subject is God and its object is twā miclu lēoht, neuter accusative plural. The second subject is hē and its object is steorran, masculine accusative plural placed before the subject and verb.

Bēon is a supplied plural present optative of the copula; scīnen is a supplied plural present optative of scīnan. Neither is a finite form of the familiar weak indicative gewyrcean system, and neither belongs to the indicative production assignment. Scinen later is the distinct supplied past optative spelling.

### NEW-U03-R06

Gōd is strong neuter nominative singular, a predicate agreeing with hit in the supplied content clause. Fēorða is weak masculine nominative singular with dæg in se fēorða dæg. It is an ordinal of the ordinary weak type and also follows se. The source's closing formula and its singular wæs are supplied; the adjective analysis does not require an independently learned rule for that full formula.
