## Source evidence and limits retained

The [expanded grammar audit](../../sources/audits/unit01-source/expanded-grammar.md) records image-checked Cook §§38–53, 84, and 138, with exact printed/PDF locators. Its full nominal inventory distinguishes source-attested cells from forms composed by a checked pattern. These are the bases for the paradigm and vocabulary comparison, not a claim that every composed oblique cell has been independently found in a text.

Particular limits have been preserved correctly:

- **Wyrt** has the checked endingless accusative, supported by FB p.324's §51.1 reference and the reader's accusative wyrt. **Niht** retains Cook's unlengthened selected forms, with evidenced nihte alternatives acknowledged.
- **Ǣfenu** is paradigm-derived; the unit does not claim a newly located independent textual occurrence. Selected **ǣfen** and **lyft** are neuter. Gender alternatives are not declared intrinsically wrong.
- **God** has only the assigned singular divine-name sense. **Ðīestru** has the complete selected plural paradigm without an invented singular. Unused mass/abstract plurals are identified as morphology for recognition, not unannounced productive senses.
- **\*sǣwa** is actually starred in Wright & Wright §388, printed p.187 / PDF207. The learner marks it reconstructed and untested; the contract leaves both milestones null and no graded task needs it. Cook's other selected sea cells retain their separate evidence. The table therefore does not launder reconstruction into attestation.
- [Correction C01](../../sources/CORRECTIONS.md) is an explicit correction to learner wæstm, carried through its forms and relevant answers. Source spelling wǣstm is preserved in the evidence. Cook's hooked **ę**, unlengthened **geseah**, and printed **Geweorðe** remain intact.

The [first-day source audit](../../sources/audits/unit01-source/creation-first-day.md) and fresh images of FB pp.123–124 / PDF139–140 confirm **heofonan** as weak feminine accusative singular, **ðǣre nēowolnesse** as genitive dependent, **brādnesse** as the dative head after ofer, and **wæteru** as accusative plural in the second ofer phrase.
