## Short readings

### CH01-MINI01

“The man is in the dwelling. The seed is in the man's dwelling. The bird is in the tree. The seed is the bird's food.”

The dwelling belongs to the man. In the second sentence, **mannes** is genitive singular, a dependent of **hofe**; **hofe** is dative singular as the head governed by locative **on**. **Ðæs** agrees with **mannes**.

### CH01-MINI02

“The fishes were in the sea. The birds were in the trees. The trees were on the earth.”

Present rewrite:

> Ðā fiscas sindon on ðǣm sǣ. Ðā fuglas sindon on ðǣm trēowum. Ðā trēowu sindon on ðǣre eorðan.

**Trēowu** is nominative plural, the third sentence's subject; trēo is its singular. **Eorðan** is dative singular, governed by locative **on** and accompanied by feminine singular **ðǣre**.

## Creation

### CH01-READ01

Subject **God**, nominative singular. Objects **heofonan** and **eorðan**, accusative singular. **Heofonan** is weak feminine singular here, not plural. “In the beginning God created heaven and earth.”

### CH01-READ02

The earth was empty and void; darkness lay over the surface of the deep. **Wǣron** is plural because its Old English subject **ðīestru** is plural. In the phrase after **ofer**, the head **brādnesse** is dative singular; **ðǣre nēowolnesse** is the dependent genitive singular.

### CH01-READ03

God's spirit was borne or carried over the waters; “moved over the waters” gives the contextual sense. **Wæteru** is accusative plural in this prepositional phrase. The selected dative plural would be **wæterum**, so the printed -u cannot be analyzed as that dative plural.

### CH01-READ04

The quoted **Geweorðe lēoht** expresses a command or wish for light to come into being: “Let there be light” or “Let light come to be.” It does not report that light already exists. The following clause states that light was made/came into being.

### CH01-READ05

**ðā** is temporal “then”; **ðæt** is the complement conjunction “that”; **hit** is neuter singular “it,” referring to light. “Then God saw that it was good.” The embedded clause has its copula at the end; this is not an instruction to put every Old English verb last.

### CH01-READ06

God separated the light from the darkness. Direct object **ðæt lēoht**: accusative singular neuter. Separation phrase **fram ðǣm ðīestrum**: preposition plus dative plural. “Darkness” remains morphologically plural in Old English.

### CH01-READ07

He called the light **Day** and the darkness **Night**. The understood subject of **hēt** is God/he. The close says **one day**, not multiple days and not an explicitly inflected ordinal “first day.”

### CH01-READ08

Independent translation from the printed Old English and local help:

> In the beginning God created heaven and earth. Now the earth was empty and void; and darkness was over the surface of the deep; and God's spirit was borne over the waters. God then said, “Let light come to be”; and light was made. Then God saw that it was good; and he separated the light from the darkness. And he called the light Day, and the darkness Night. Then there came to be evening and morning: one day.

The fluent English singular “darkness was” preserves the sense of the Old English plural. “Was moved/carried,” “came into being,” and “Let there be light” can preserve the corresponding supplied verbal meanings. The final formula is deliberately translated from its **ān dæg** rather than replaced with a familiar ordinal rendering.

## Review

### CH01-REVIEW01

**dagas, hwalas, wæstmas**. The first two have selected singular æ/plural a; **wæstm** retains æ before a consonant group. C01's corrected short vowel is retained throughout **wæstm/wæstmas**; no macron is restored.

### CH01-REVIEW02

Head **hofe**, dative singular after locative **on**; dependent **ðæs mannes**, genitive singular. **Ðæs** agrees with **mannes**, not **hofe**.

### CH01-REVIEW03

**Eorðan** can be G/D/A singular or N/A plural in the selected paradigm. In **on ðǣre eorðan**, it is D singular, locative; in **God gescēop ðā eorðan**, it is A singular, “God created the earth.” The article **ðā** alone is also compatible with plural, but the assigned singular earth sense and the construction select this intended reading.

### CH01-REVIEW04

Old English **ðīestru** is plural and therefore takes plural **wǣron**, whereas idiomatic English “darkness” is singular. In **ðæt lēoht fram ðǣm ðīestrum**, **ðæt lēoht** is accusative singular as the object in the reading, and **ðǣm ðīestrum** is dative plural after **fram**.
