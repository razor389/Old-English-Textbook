## Analysis of all 20 inputs

### NEW-U11-A01

“When the monk came, then the man gave him water.” Subordinate clause: **Ðā se munuc cōm**; main clause: **ðā sealde se mann him wæter**. Cōm is third singular preterite indicative of cuman; sealde is third singular preterite indicative of sęllan. First ðā is temporal “when”, second ðā the main-clause adverb “then”. Him is the dative recipient, naturally the monk.

### NEW-U11-A02

“When you come, then I help you,” describing repeated visits. **Ðonne gē cumað** is the temporal subordinate clause; second ðonne means “then” in the main clause. Cumað is second plural present indicative; helpe is first singular present indicative in this factual account, although its surface is also compatible with a subjunctive elsewhere. Ēow is plural dative after helpan.

### NEW-U11-A03

(a) “While he ate, I carried water.” (b) “While he ate, I carried water.” Both ðā-hwīle-ðe and mid-ðȳ connect simultaneous past activities here. **Ǣt** is third singular preterite indicative of etan; bær is first singular preterite indicative of beran. Neither statement alone asserts that eating caused the carrying. The overlap is compatible with English “was eating/was carrying”, without adding an Old English progressive auxiliary.

### NEW-U11-A04

(a) “Do not go before I come.” (b) “Stay here until I come.” In each, cume is first singular present subjunctive of cuman in an anticipated time clause. Far and wes are singular commands; ne negates far. Future reference comes from the commanded action and the not-yet-realized endpoint, not from a new inflectional future ending. The form cume alone is syncretic; the stated context and construction determine its analysis.

### NEW-U11-A05

“I help you because you are sick.” The action explained is **Ic helpe ðē**; the reason is **for-ðon-ðe ðū eart untrum**. Eart is second singular present indicative of wesan. The clause asserts the sickness as the cause, rather than presenting an intended outcome.

### NEW-U11-A06

(a) “I know that the monk is coming.” The ðæt clause supplies the content known; cymð is third singular present indicative of cuman.

(b) “The monk comes so that he may help me.” The ðæt clause supplies his intended purpose; helpe is third singular present subjunctive of helpan. His coming does not itself prove that the help succeeds.

(c) “The monk is so good that he helps me.” The ðæt clause gives an asserted result linked to swā; hilpð is third singular present indicative of helpan. The identical conjunction does not impose the same clause function or mood in all three sentences.

### NEW-U11-A07

“The man held the animal lest it take the food.” He intends to prevent the animal's taking the food. Nōme is third singular preterite subjunctive of niman in the negative-purpose clause; hēold is third singular preterite indicative of healdan. The sentence does not assert that the animal actually took the food. Nor does “lest” require an additional ne in the selected construction.

### NEW-U11-A08

“Although he is sick, he helps me.” Sīe is third singular present subjunctive of wesan. Yes, the selected concessive subjunctive may admit the sickness as a fact. The clause sets that circumstance against the helping; it need not express doubt or deny the sickness.

### NEW-U11-A09

(a) “If he is here, he helps me.” Is and hilpð are third singular present indicative. Presence remains an open condition.

(b) “If he were here, he would help me.” Wǣre and hulpe are third singular preterite subjunctive, respectively of wesan and helpan. In the specified contrary-present situation, both concern the present, despite their preterite morphology: he is absent now and the help is hypothetical now.

### NEW-U11-A10

(a) “When does the monk come?” Hwonne is the interrogative adverb asking for the time; cymð is third singular present indicative.

(b) “When the monk comes, I help him.” Ðonne introduces a temporal subordinate clause; cymð and first singular helpe are present indicative in this ordinary factual/recurring reading.

(c) “The monk is older than the fisherman.” Ðonne introduces the comparison standard after ieldra. It means “than”, not a time; the two people are nominative in the comparison.

### NEW-U11-A11

(a) “The fisherman came to help the man.” The fisherman is understood as the helper in the tō + inflected-infinitive phrase **tō helpanne ðǣm męnn**.

(b) “The fisherman came so that the monk might help the man.” The monk is the explicit subject of the purpose clause and the intended helper; hulpe is third singular preterite subjunctive. The finite clause can name its own subject, so its actor need not be the fisherman, who is the subject of cōm.

### NEW-U11-A12

Condition: **gif man hine on fȳr dēð**, “if one puts it in a fire”. Main clause: **ðonne flēoð ðǣr næddran onwęg**, “then snakes flee away there”. ### NEW-U11-S06
