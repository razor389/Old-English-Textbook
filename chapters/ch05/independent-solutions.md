## Principal parts

### NEW-U05-P01

**beran**: infinitive citation; **bær**: singular preterite principal part, used for first and third singular; **bǣron**: plural preterite principal part; **boren**: past-participle citation. The middle two are finite preterites; the outer two are nonfinite citations. Bær is not the second-singular form.

### NEW-U05-P02

**Geaf** is the strong preterite of giefan; **sealde** is the weak preterite of sęllan. Giefan uses the strong vowel alternation (gief-/geaf-/gēaf-), while sealde has the weak dental past element. The weak example also has its learned stem change, so “weak” does not mean that the present vowel must remain unchanged.

### NEW-U05-P03

**Ic bær; ðū bǣre; hē bær; wē bǣron.** Bǣre and bǣron use the preterite plural stem bǣr-; the second singular adds -e, and the plural adds -on.

## Clauses

These are independent models, not claims that a unique word order or every spelling is compulsory. Explicit lemma, person, tense, number and government requirements remain binding.

### NEW-U05-C01

**Ðū birst ðone męte, and ic nime ðone fisc.** The two subjects require present2sg and present1sg; both objects are masculine accusative singular.

### NEW-U05-C02

**Wit gēafon ðǣm mannum uncerne męte.** Wit denotes the two speakers and takes the plural verb. Mannum is the dative plural recipient; uncerne agrees with masculine accusative singular męte while identifying two possessors.

### NEW-U05-C03

**Ðā gefēran sǣton on ðǣm hofe, and se mann stōd on ðǣm strande.** The companion subject is plural; the man is singular. Both on-phrases express static location with dative.

### NEW-U05-C04

**Ic ne ǣt ðone męte; ðū ǣte hine.** The first clause has preterite1sg; the second has preterite2sg -e. Hine refers to masculine męte. Ne precedes the first finite verb; no auxiliary corresponding to English “did” is added.

### NEW-U05-C05

**Hīe gesāwon ðā fuglas and nōmon ðā fiscas.** Both preterites and both nominal objects are plural; retain the supplied prefixed seeing verb in this rewrite.

### NEW-U05-C06

**Se mann cwið: “Ic sēo ðā trēowu.”** Cwið agrees with the third-singular man; sēo agrees with the quoted first-person speaker. Trēowu is the taught accusative plural. Gesēo is another taught ordinary seeing verb if the task allows that lexical choice.

### NEW-U05-C07

**Gē geswīcað ēowres weorces.** The verb is present2pl. Geswīcan governs the genitive of the work, and ēowres agrees with neuter genitive singular weorces. A mechanical fram-phrase would miss the stated government target.

### NEW-U05-C08

**Ðæt trēo fielð, and ðā męnn healdað ðæt scip.** The tree requires third singular; the men require plural. Scip is the neuter accusative singular thing held.

**God gescēop ðā heofonas and ðā eorðan.** Heofonas is masculine accusative plural “the heavens”; eorðan is weak feminine accusative singular. The source reading's heofonan is a different feminine singular noun form.

### EX-I-05-02

**God geworhte ðā fæstnesse.** Fæstnesse is feminine accusative singular.

### EX-I-05-04

**God gescēop ðā miclan hwalas.** The article and noun are accusative plural; miclan is the weak adjective after the article. Scēop from the separately taught unprefixed verb is a defensible lexical alternative unless a prefixed target is imposed.

### EX-I-05-05

**Hē geworhte ðā nīetenu ðǣre eorðan.** Nīetenu is neuter accusative plural; the earth phrase is genitive singular. The genitive may precede its head noun without changing the essential meaning.

### EX-I-05-06

**Hē geseah eall ðā ðing.** The supplied accusative plural phrase remains eall ðā ðing; no extra -e is added to eall. Seah is a defensible taught lexical alternative to geseah if no prefixed target is imposed.

### EX-I-05-07

**Hē gefylde his weorc.** Gefylde is the previously taught weak preterite of gefyllan “finish/accomplish a work”; his is the unchanged personal genitive possessor.

### EX-I-05-08

**Hē geblētsað ðone seofoðan dæg.** The verb is third-singular present, not past or plural. The ordinal follows its taught weak inflection; all object elements are masculine accusative singular.

### EX-I-05-09

### EX-I-05-10

**Ic gesēo ðone fugol on ðǣm trēowe.** Gesēo is first singular; fugol is accusative, and trēowe is the taught dative singular in the location phrase. Sēo is also a taught ordinary seeing alternative.

### EX-I-05-11

**Hīe gesāwon ðone fisc on ðǣm wætere.** The seeing verb agrees with plural “they.” The English “fish” does not distinguish singular from plural: **Hīe gesāwon ðā fiscas on ðǣm wætere** is equally defensible for several fish. Sāwon is the unprefixed seeing alternative. A key should not treat the original number ambiguity as a language error.

### EX-I-05-16

## Connected reading

### NEW-U05-T01

One man did not eat and gave his food to the birds. The birds ate and flew. The narrative does not identify whether that man belonged to the original pair or their companions, so the answer should not invent that identity.

### NEW-U05-T02

**Ðǣm gefērum** is the dative plural recipient, the companions. **Ðone męte** is the masculine accusative singular thing given, the food. **Ðā męnn**, nominative plural, controls the plural preterite **gēafon**.

### NEW-U05-T03

**Se mann sitt on ðǣm strande and gesiehð ðā fiscas. Ðā fiscas swimmað on ðǣm wætere.** The two singular verbs become sitt and gesiehð; plural swimming becomes swimmað. Subjects, object and location phrases are unchanged.

### NEW-U05-T04

Two men carried food. Their companions sat in the dwelling. The men gave the companions the food, and they ate. One man did not eat. He gave his food to the birds. The birds ate and flew. The man sat on the shore and saw the fish. The fish swam in the water.

In this model “they” after the giving clause naturally means the companions, but hīe does not morphologically force that antecedent. The final fish are plural in the Old English even though English “fish” is number-ambiguous. The indefinite ān mann does not establish which earlier group contains him.

## Source readings

### NEW-U05-R01

| Source form | Lemma | Strong class | Tense/mood | Person/number |

| gescēop | gescieppan | VI | preterite indicative | third singular |
| cwæð | cweðan | V | preterite indicative | third singular |

| hēt | hātan | VII | preterite indicative | third singular |

All refer to God as subject. Hēt continues the same subject even though that clause does not repeat a subject noun or pronoun.

### NEW-U05-R02

Ordinary **weorðan** means “become.” In supplied **wearð geworht**, wearð combines with the participle geworht in the passive expression “was made.” **Wearð is the finite preterite**; geworht is the supplied nonfinite participle. Learning the ordinary finite system did not independently teach the passive construction.

### NEW-U05-R03

Source **heofonan** is accusative singular of the weak feminine **heofone**, “heaven/the sky.” The exercise's plural “the heavens” can use the taught masculine **heofon**, accusative plural **heofonas**. The two visible endings belong to different noun identities and numbers; heofonan here is not the regular masculine plural.

### NEW-U05-R04

In the beginning God created heaven and earth. Now the earth was empty and void; and darkness was over the surface of the deep, and God's spirit was borne over the waters. God then said, “Let light come to be”; and light was made. God then saw that it was good, and he separated the light from the darkness. And he called the light Day and the darkness Night. Then evening and morning had come to be—one day.

This uses the printed local help for temporal on, connective sōðlice, both ofer constructions, the optative, passive expressions, the content clause, naming, and the closing formula. The final “one day” preserves ān rather than silently changing it to the ordinal “first.”

### NEW-U05-R05

**Cwæð** (cweðan, V) and **hēt** (hātan, VII) are strong preterites. **Geworhte** (gewyrcean) and supplied **tōtwǣmde** (tōtwǣman) are weak preterites with their learned or locally supplied dental formation. All four are third-singular indicative here and have **God** as their subject. The relative-clause wǣron forms have a different, plural waters subject and are not part of this four-form comparison.

### NEW-U05-R06

The waters **under the firmament** are separated from those **above the firmament**. In **fram ðǣm ðe wǣron bufan ðǣre fæstnesse**, ðǣm means “those waters” and is dative plural after fram. The omitted noun is recovered from the water context; the following relative clause identifies these waters as the upper ones.

### NEW-U05-R07

God calls the firmament **Heaven**, expressed by supplied accusative singular **Heofonan** in the naming construction. In the closing formula, **ōðer** means “second” and agrees with masculine nominative singular **dæg**. That local agreement analysis does not make the whole wæs geworden formula an independent production target.

### NEW-U05-R08

God then said again, “Let a firmament now come to be in the midst of the waters, and let it separate the waters from the waters.” And God made the firmament and separated the waters which were under the firmament from those which were above the firmament; it was then done so. And God called the firmament Heaven. And evening and morning had come to be—a second day.

“Also” is a supported contextual alternative for ęft. The subject of both wishes is the firmament; the narrative making and separating have God as subject. The two waters groups and the distinction between the first day's “one day” and this “second day” are retained.

## Cumulative review

### NEW-U05-X01

**Ðū gēafe; ðū sealdest.** Giefan is strong, so second singular uses the plural stem gēaf- plus -e. Sęllan is weak, so second singular uses the weak past stem seald- plus -est. Merely adding -st to the strong third-singular geaf would miss both its selected vowel quantity and the strong second-singular formation.

### NEW-U05-X02

An -ian infinitive alone does not decide conjugational class: **swęrian** has the strong past **swōr/swōron**, while **lufian** has weak dental **lufode**. Their first-singular presents are **ic swęrie** and **ic lufie**. The supplied participles and prefix notation are comparison evidence, not new passive/perfect syntax.

### NEW-U05-X03

**Gē sēoð**: sēon “see,” strong V, present indicative, second-person plural. **Hē sīeð**: sēoðan “boil,” strong II, present indicative, third-person singular. The pronounced/written vowel quantity and spelling differ; the subject pronouns determine the requested person and number. No complement or independent boil construction is introduced by the identification task.
