The noun review checked class and stem evidence independently of the proposed tables: for example, the *fat-* plural of *fæt*, *fēos/fēo* beside *feoh*, heavy-stem zero plurals, and the directly attested *æcernu* in Crawford. The verb review distinguished attested principal parts from derived cells. In particular, preterite-present *beðurfan* does not take an ordinary present pattern; *oncnāwan* has its *cnǣw-* present singular and *cnēow-* past; *dōn* compounds retain their irregular finite stems; *lęcgean* and *sęcgean* require their own stem alternations; and *stelan* has different past singular and plural/subjunctive grades. Valid alternatives are not treated as errors simply because the learner selects one consistent form. The review preserves the evidence for contracted *lēde/lēdon* beside the selected *lęgde/lęgdon*, *sāde* beside *sægde*, and *ofslagen* beside *ofslægen*. It also preserves the following adjudicated questions:

- **Prior *hē funde*.** The earlier allegation of an error was withdrawn on actual evidence. Unit 4 explicitly teaches the form, and Cook §104, printed p.62, identifies it as the usual third singular. Third-singular *fand* and first-singular *funde* remain valid alternatives. The withdrawn diagnosis must not become a new prerequisite failure.
- **44.7 *wē wǣren … cumen*.** Cook §62, p.41, and §179, p.100, permit an uninflected predicate past participle; §188, p.102, licenses *wesan* in the intransitive perfect. Crawford p.190, Genesis 43.10, has inflected *wæron cumene*. The latter is a valid alternative, not evidence that the selected uninflected construction is wrong. Finite *wǣren* carries the plural agreement.
- **Reading *lēafe*, “permission.”** Personally viewed First Book p.302 gives feminine *lēaf* (§51.b), “leave, permission,” and separately neuter *lēaf* (§47), “leaf.” Exercises p.57 labels “permission” §47.
