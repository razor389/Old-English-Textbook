## Principal parts

### NEW-U04-P01

1. **drīfan**: infinitive citation, identifying the present stem.
2. **drāf**: singular preterite principal part; first/third singular indicative.
3. **drifon**: plural preterite principal part; first/second/third plural indicative.
4. **drifen**: past-participle citation.

The middle two are finite indicative forms. The first and fourth are nonfinite citations; they are not extra person forms.

### NEW-U04-P02

| Verb | Class | 2sg preterite | Stem used |
|---|---|---|---|
| rīdan | I | ride | rid-, from ridon, the third principal part |
| bēodan | II | bude | bud-, from budon, the third principal part |
| bindan | III | bunde | bund-, from bundon, the third principal part |

Each adds -e to the plural preterite stem.

### NEW-U04-P03

**Drāf** represents first or third singular preterite indicative. **Drifon** represents first, second or third plural preterite indicative. **Drifen** is the supplied past-participle citation, which has no finite person assignment here.

### NEW-U04-P04

Both **ride** and **hīerdest** are second-person singular preterite indicative, with subject ðū. Strong rīdan uses its plural-preterite stem rid- plus -e, contrasting with singular rād. Weak hīeran uses its dental preterite formation with the second-singular ending, hīerdest. The latter is not formed by applying the strong vowel series.

## Nouns

### NEW-U04-N01

| Case | scip singular | scip plural | wind singular | wind plural |

Scip is a short neuter and takes -u in nominative/accusative plural. Wind follows the masculine -as pattern. The initial sc of scip is before the vowel and does not make its stem syllable heavy.

### NEW-U04-D07

## Source reading

### NEW-U04-R01

In addition to that, northeasterly winds came, and the fierce southwest wind stood against them, and the whole ship broke apart in this terrible tempest. All Apollonius's companions perished, and Apollonius alone reached Pentapolis, the Cyrenian land, by swimming, and there came up onto the shore.

The guiding reading of ‘them’ is the preceding northeast winds; the spelling him alone does not prove its antecedent. The ship's destruction, the companions' deaths, and Apollonius's survival must remain distinct. ‘Perished to death’ gives the supplied tō phrase literally; natural English ‘perished/died’ preserves the result.

### NEW-U04-R02

**Tōbærst**: tōberstan, Class III, third-person singular preterite indicative; subject ðæt scip eall, the whole ship.

**Forwurdon**: forweorðan, Class III, third-person plural preterite indicative; subject Apollonius gefēran ealle, all Apollonius's companions.

The whole prefixed headwords and their meanings are retained.

### NEW-U04-R03

The first **Apollonius** is genitive, limiting gefēran: ‘Apollonius's companions’. The second is nominative, the subject of becōm and the continued subject of ūpēode. Gefēran is nominative plural, so its finite verb is plural forwurdon; the genitive possessor is not that clause's subject.

| Noun | Case and number | Role |
|---|---|---|
| windas | nominative plural | subject of cōmon |
| wind | nominative singular | subject of stōd |

## Cumulative review

### NEW-U04-X01

**Ðū drife. Hīe curon ðæt scip. Se mann bint ðæt dēor.**

The first requires past2sg from the plural stem. The second requires past plural, with the cēosan r-stem. The third requires present3sg, not plural bindað. Each original subject and tense is preserved.

### NEW-U04-X02

**Twēgen gefēran ridon. Hīe fundon twā scipu. Hīe curon ðæt lȳtle scip. Hīe druncon wæter.**

All four sentences have the same two companions as their plural subjects and narrate past actions. The selected ship is small; the two ships found have the neuter plural scipu.

### NEW-U04-X03

**Se ealda gefēra hilpð mīnum fuglum. Mīne fuglas singað wel. Wē fundon his lȳtel scip on ðǣm strande.**

The old companion is the singular helper; the speaker's birds are dative recipients in the first clause and nominative plural singers in the second. Wē denotes the finders. His identifies the male owner, naturally the old companion; it does not identify the finders. His is indeclinable. The chosen strong neuter accusative lȳtel after his follows the previously taught permitted possessive construction; a corresponding weak lȳtle is also defensible under that earlier scope. Strande is dative in the location phrase.

### NEW-U04-X04

**Hē funde**: third singular preterite indicative. **Ðū funde**: second singular preterite indicative. **Hīe fundon**: third plural preterite indicative. Funde versus fundon distinguishes singular from plural here. The pronouns hē and ðū distinguish the two singular persons; the written verb does not. The noted first-person variant funde adds a further possible person only if context supplies it; it does not change these clauses' pronoun-defined analyses.
