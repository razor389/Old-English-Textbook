The exact task is: “I took leave of her (acc.), and announced (om. to) her that I never more would visit her.” The actual direction requires grētan, cȳðan, intended later genēosian with willan, and the reporting subjunctive. The local help supplies negative preterite subjunctive singular **nolde** and the expression **nǣfre mā**.

My solution is:

**Ic grētte hīe, and cȳðde hiere ðæt ic hīe nǣfre mā genēosian nolde.**

This means: “I took leave of her, and announced to her that I intended never to visit her again.” The taking leave and announcing are asserted past actions. The subordinate clause reports the speaker's negative intention about subsequent visits; it does not assert that a visit occurred or establish what eventually happened.

The first **ic** is nominative first singular and remains the understood subject of coordinated **cȳðde**. **Grētte** and **cȳðde** are first singular preterite indicatives, taken from the complete tables in Unit 12 §5. Grētan's assigned leave-taking sense governs accusative **hīe**, explicitly matching Cook's first parenthetical direction. Cȳðan introduces the content clause with **ðæt** and has dative recipient **hiere**. The omission of English “to” therefore removes a preposition, not the recipient.

Within the reported clause, **ic** remains the speaker, since both the reporting and the reported subject are first person. **Hīe** is feminine accusative singular, the visited person. Unit 10 explicitly teaches this accusative frame of prefixed **genēosian**; the actual grammar and lexicon registers confirm it. It is not necessary to import the genitive government of related unprefixed nēosian. Unit 2's pronoun table supplies both selected spellings, hīe and hiere, and distinguishes their cases.

**Genēosian** is the plain infinitive after willan, exactly the productive infinitive use stated in Unit 12 §5. The recognition-only inflected form genēosianne is not required. **Nolde** is the locally supplied preterite subjunctive singular, selected here because the clause reports an intention from a past viewpoint. Its spelling also occurs in the indicative, so spelling alone would not identify the mood. The task and clause function establish the subjunctive analysis. **Nǣfre mā** expresses “never again”; it reinforces the negative already contained in nolde, as §8 explains. The finite verb is placed after its infinitive in the dependent clause, consistent with the unit's reported-clause models.
