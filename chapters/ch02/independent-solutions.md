## Cases, reference and clauses

### NEW-U02-C01

**Hē hīerð hīe:** He hears them / the man hears the birds. Hē is nominative masculine singular, the man. Hīe is accusative plural here, the birds.

**Hīe hīerað hine:** They hear him / the birds hear the man. Hīe is nominative plural, the birds; hine accusative masculine singular, the man. The heard and hearing referents exchange roles, and the verb agrees with the new subject.

### NEW-U02-C02

**Ic sęlle ðǣm męnn sǣd.** Then **Ic sęlle him sǣd.** The man is the dative singular recipient; seed is the accusative thing given. Him replaces only ðǣm męnn.

### NEW-U02-C03

“We two have food. The man gives us two water.” Wit is nominative first-person dual, subject of habbað. Unc is dative first-person dual, recipient of sęlð; the man is that clause's singular subject.

### NEW-U02-C04

**Git nęriað mē. Ic hīere inc.** Git is dual subject; inc dual accusative object. Mē is accusative singular.

### NEW-U02-C05

**Sēo eorðe hæfð wæter. Hēo hæfð gærs.** “The earth has water. It has grass.” Hēo resumes the feminine singular noun as a nominative subject; English can naturally use “it”.

### NEW-U02-C06

**His hof is on ðǣre eorðan. His fuglas libbað on ðǣm hofe.** His is the male possessor's unchanged genitive in both clauses. Eorðan and hofe are the locative dative singulars.

### NEW-U02-C07

**Hēo hæfð hof. Fuglas libbað on hiere hofe.** **Hiere** is the invariant female possessor form; hofe remains locative dative singular.

### NEW-U02-C08

**Hīe habbað hof. Hiera hof hæfð fuglas.** The second subject is singular hof, so hæfð; the plurality of possessors and birds does not control that verb.

### NEW-U02-C09

**Ic geręste mē. Hē geręste hine.** Subjects: ic and hē, nominative singular. Objects: mē and hine, accusative singular, each coreferential with its subject. “I rested myself. He rested himself.”

### NEW-U02-C10

**Git geręstað inc. Hīe geręstað hīe.** The first self-reference is second-person dual; the second third-person plural. Both have plural verbs.

### NEW-U02-C11

**Ic ne hīere ðone fugol.** “I do not hear the bird.”

**Hē ne lufað ðā fuglas.** “He does not love the birds.” Ne immediately precedes the finite verb.

### NEW-U02-C12

**Ðū ne hīerdest hine. Wē ne sęndon ðā fuglas.** Both are preterite; hine is masculine singular accusative, fuglas plural accusative.

### NEW-U02-C13

**Ic sęlle mē męte. Ic sęlle him męte.** Mē is reflexive because the recipient and the subject ic are the same person. Him identifies another male recipient. Both are dative because each names the recipient of giving; męte is the accusative thing given. Reflexivity does not create a separate case form.

## Connected readings

### NEW-U02-N01

The man had seed and water. The men had a dwelling and birds. The birds lived in their dwelling. The man gave the men seed and water. They loved the birds and heard them. They gave them seed and water. They did not send the birds. They performed their work and rested themselves. The man said: “I love the birds.”

My contextual interpretation: the men are the possessors of the dwelling and the subjects of the plural giving/caring/working/resting predicates; the birds are the objects heard and the recipients in the later giving sentence. The phrase “their dwelling” could also refer to the birds' dwelling without a morphological change. The text explicitly permits other defensible references. An answer should not pretend that him or hiera alone names a particular antecedent.

### NEW-U02-N02

| Occurrence | Case/number in this reading | Contextual referent |
|---|---|---|
| on **hiera** hofe, sentence 3 | genitive plural | the men; birds as possessors is also plausible |
| **Hīe** lufodon, sentence 5 | nominative plural | the men |
| hīerdon **hīe**, sentence 5 | accusative plural | the birds |
| **Hīe** sealdon, sentence 6 | nominative plural | the men |
| sealdon **him**, sentence 6 | dative plural | the birds |
| **Hīe** ne sęndon, sentence 7 | nominative plural | the men |
| **Hīe** gefręmedon, sentence 8 | nominative plural | the men |
| **hiera** weorc, sentence 8 | genitive plural | the men |
| geręston **hīe**, sentence 8 | accusative plural, reflexive | the same men as the subject |

Hiera marks a third-person plural possessor but does not select between plural groups. Nominative hīe is plural, and the plural finite verbs confirm it where hīe is subject. An isolated hīe can also be feminine singular accusative; the object uses here are plural through the stated referents. Him alone can be masculine/neuter singular dative or plural dative. The giving/caring narrative establishes its birds-as-recipients interpretation. Geręston hīe is coreferential through the learned reflexive construction; morphology alone does not identify those people as “the men”. Other coherent antecedent choices must be assessed against the whole sequence, not rejected because the spelling lacks an antecedent label.

### NEW-U02-N03

**A to B:** “I have seed. You have water.” Ic = A; ðū = B.

**B to A:** “We two give the birds food. They hear us two.” Wit = B and A; hīe = birds; unc = B and A.

**C to A, B:** “You two love the birds. I hear you two.” Git = A and B; ic = C; inc = A and B.

**A to B, C, D:** “We love them. You hear them, and they hear you.” Wē = A, B, C, D; gē = B, C, D; the first two hīe objects and the final hīe subject = birds; ēow = B, C, D. The final group's membership is explicitly supplied by the learner's scene description.

### NEW-U02-N04

Unc: first-person dual accusative, A and B. In **Hīe hīerað unc**, hīe (birds) is the nominative plural subject and controls hīerað; unc is not the controller.

Inc: second-person dual accusative, A and B. In **Ic hīere inc**, ic (C) is nominative first singular and controls hīere.

Ēow: second-person plural accusative, B, C, D. In **hīe hīerað ēow**, hīe (birds) is the plural subject controlling hīerað. Their shared plurality would not make the object a second subject.

### NEW-U02-N05

**Se mann hæfð sǣd and wæter. Ðā męnn habbað hof and fuglas. Ðā fuglas libbað on hiera hofe. Se mann sęlð ðǣm mannum sǣd and wæter.**

Only the tense forms change: hæfde→hæfð, hæfdon→habbað, lifdon→libbað, sealde→sęlð. The plural dative mannum remains mannum.

### NEW-U02-N06

“They performed their work and rested themselves.” Hīe is the nominative plural subject of both gefręmedon and geręston. Weorc, neuter accusative singular in the stated work sense, is the object of gefręmedon. Hiera is the genitive plural possessor, referring contextually to those men. Final hīe is the accusative plural object of geręston and refers to the same people as the subject.

## Sabbath source reading

### NEW-U02-R01

Accordingly, then, the heavens and the earth and all their array were completed. And God then completed on the seventh day his work which he had made, and he rested himself on the seventh day from all the work which he had performed. And God blessed the seventh day and hallowed it, because on that day he ceased from his work which he created to make.

This is a close rendering of the actual text. “Made/performed” can stand for “had made/had performed”; the relative actions precede the completion/rest in context. Natural English may omit “himself” after “rested”. The last clause intentionally preserves the supplied created-to-make relation, however awkward it sounds. It does not substitute “created and made” or a remembered modern Creation translation. The opening is passive; fullfręmede is not a seventh weak finite verb.

### NEW-U02-R02

| Actual form | Headword | Class | Person/number/tense in context |
|---|---|---|---|
| gefylde | gefyllan | I | 3 singular preterite indicative |
| geworhte | gewyrcean | I | 3 singular preterite indicative |
| geręste | geręstan | I | 3 singular preterite indicative |
| gefręmede | gefręmman | I | 3 singular preterite indicative |
| geblētsode | geblētsian | II | 3 singular preterite indicative |
| gehālgode | gehālgian | II | 3 singular preterite indicative |

Each selected preterite spelling can also be 1sg in another context; God/hē makes each 3sg here. Fullfręmede is a supplied participle; geswāc and gescēop are the supplied strong past forms.

### NEW-U02-R03

**Geręste hine:** hine is masculine accusative singular, referring to God, the subject hē. It is reflexive: “he rested himself”, naturally “he rested”.

**Hine gehālgode:** hine is masculine accusative singular, referring to ðone seofoðan dæg, the seventh day. It is not reflexive: “he hallowed it”. The identical pronoun spelling does not mean that its two antecedents coincide.

### NEW-U02-R04

**Hiera frætwung:** hiera is the plural genitive possessor referring to the heavens and earth; frætwung, “array”, is the possessed feminine nominative singular noun in the subject phrase.

**His weorc:** his is the masculine singular genitive possessor, God; weorc is the possessed neuter accusative singular noun, object of gefylde.

**His weorces:** the same God is the possessor; weorces is neuter genitive singular under the supplied government of geswāc. The head's accusative→genitive change does not turn his into another form. Hiera/his identify the possessor; they do not agree with the possessed head's number, gender, or case.

### NEW-U02-R05

Heofonas: masculine nominative plural. Eorðe: feminine nominative singular. Frætwung: feminine nominative singular. The complete subject is **heofonas and eorðe and eall hiera frætwung**, “the heavens and the earth and all their array”, following wǣron. The coordinated phrase supports the plural verb and supplied plural participle.

### NEW-U02-R06

On the seventh day God completed his work, rested from all that work, blessed the seventh day, hallowed it, and ceased from his work (the reason clause expressly says “on that day”). Making, performing, and creating describe the work in relative clauses; they should not automatically be added as further actions first undertaken on the seventh day.

### NEW-U02-R07

He hallowed the day because on that day he ceased from his work. The final relative clause describes that work as what he created to make; God is the understood maker in the supplied infinitival phrase. The final phrase is not another finite assertion “and made”, nor does it mean that the day itself was created to make something.
