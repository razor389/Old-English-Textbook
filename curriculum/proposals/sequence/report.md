# Proposed grammar-led sequence

22 October 2026. Curriculum analyst's deliverable for `oec-sequence`; the coordinator owns the settled course plan, assignment map, contracts, registers, and Beads updates. This is a design record, not learner prose or an active teaching milestone.

## Recommendation

Adopt eighteen substantial units. Teach usable finite verbs immediately after the opening nominal unit. Keep complete noun systems together, define each verb unit's complete finite scope explicitly, and carry every form into practice before treating it as productive knowledge. A unit may require several study sessions. Units 2, 4, and 6 deliberately provide new composition and analysis practice before the learner has the grammar needed to complete Cook's mixed sentence sets independently.

The active grammar and lexical registers were read and are empty. Every form, sense, and construction begins untaught. Archived work establishes evidence only.

## Unit 1: bounded teaching scope

Retain all forty-six prompts. The combined vocabulary is larger than the archived thirty-three-noun inventory, but the difficulty can be organized by complete patterns rather than by isolated forms. The opening source audit adds **anginn, God, brādnes, gegaderung, lyft, frætwung, weorc, cynn, steorra, līehting**. These spellings and class choices remain the source editor's responsibility until the checked record is delivered; in particular **frætwung**, not an inferred *frætew*, is the reported Cook entry for “array.”

The inherited evidence identifies these necessary systems, to be fully checked in the new analysis:

- Strong masculines in a consonant and in **-e**, including selected **dæg/hwæl** vowel alternations and **fugol/morgen** contraction patterns.
- Strong neuters: monosyllabic short versus heavy stems, disyllabic patterns, **trēo/trēow-**, and lexical exceptions. The **-u/zero** rule must specify its domain; disyllables must not be predicted solely from the initial syllable's weight.
- Strong feminines, including **-nes/-ness-**, **-ung**, and the selected accusative behaviour of nouns such as **tīd**.
- Weak masculine and feminine paradigms, required by **steorra** and **eorðe**. Do not imply that a feminine ending in **-e** and a strong masculine ending in **-e** follow the same declension.
- Individually checked exceptional paradigms for **mann**, **niht**, and **sǣ**. For plural-only **ðīestru**, present its attested/planned plural cases and state that no singular is assigned; do not fabricate singular cells merely to fill a rectangle. The proper-name sense of **God** does not confer a productive plural sense “gods.”
- All article forms. The distinct instrumental forms may be displayed and assigned recognition while their productive syntax waits; keep that distinction explicit.

Unit 1's verbal claim should be exactly **“the complete present and preterite indicative eom/wæs series.”** It is not the complete conjugation of *wesan/bēon*. The visually checked source is FB §138, printed pp.78–79 / PDF94–95, archived images `fb-grammar-pdf94.png` and `fb-grammar-pdf95.png`. The present selected cells are **eom, eart, is**, and plural **sindon**; the preterite cells are **wæs, wǣre, wæs**, and plural **wǣron**. Repeated plural cells apply to all persons. The alternate **bēo** series, negative forms, imperatives, subjunctives, and nonfinite forms are not silently included in that milestone. Any displayed variant needs a precise recognition/production status. Person/number labels suffice for the paradigm; a table does not require independent use of untaught personal pronouns.

Teach subject agreement and noun predicates, basic possessive/relational genitives, direct-object accusatives, and a small explicit set of prepositional government patterns. A useful production selection is locative **on + dative** and separating **fram + dative**, after direct verification. Do not teach a universal “static means dative; motion means accusative” rule: the original reading itself has **ofer wæteru** beside **ofer ðǣre nēowolnesse brādnesse**. Supply those original phrases with their local analyses rather than converting their spellings or assigning a universal rule.

## Unit 1 practice design

The chapter needs more than nominal paradigm recitation and Cook's nominative/genitive phrases. Use a short sequence that makes forms carry meaning:

1. Complete paradigm/diagnostic tasks with number and case explicitly requested; include regular contrasts and one stem alternation in each set. Avoid testing an unattested invented singular of a plural-only noun.
2. Preserve and assign Cook I.1–3 in full, with added editorial directions clearly separated from his original prompts. Require the selected vocabulary only where the purpose is to test it; the key distinguishes target compliance from grammatical alternatives.
3. Give supplied, checked **gescēop** or comparable transitive frames and require the learner to choose and inflect subject/object noun phrases. The verb is present in the task and is not part of independent recall. Alternate the roles of plausible participants, and ask a meaning question after the inflection choice.
4. Use copular mini-passages with explicitly taught **on + dative** locations and **fram + dative** separation where a verified frame supports it. Contrast singular/plural subjects, **is/sindon**, and **wæs/wǣron**. Do not falsely use a copula alone to express an action of separation.
5. Give noun phrases containing a dependent genitive inside a prepositional phrase, with only taught/supplied elements. This prepares the actual darkness clause and demonstrates nested agreement without demanding new verb inflection.
6. Include a short translation/composition set using only the productive inventory and a separate supported comprehension set. The separate key should explain nominative/predicate, object, genitive dependency, and selected government errors, not merely print answer strings.

Each proposed exercise must have an explicit task ID, vocabulary/form/construction dependency list, and model/essential-meaning record in the contract before prose. Do not create a production milestone merely because a form has appeared in a paradigm or reading gloss.

## Unit 1 reading decision

This completes the first day rather than cutting after an isolated nominal clause. Retain it as a **guided reading**, with precise local support for its untaught finite forms and whole constructions: **gescēop**, speech and seeing, naming, separation, the passive expressions, the command/wish, and the complement clause. A headword-only glossary is insufficient. Its question set should require taught nominal/copular analysis, supported narrative comprehension, and a close translation; it must not require recall of supplied verbs or independent analysis of untaught clause types. The source editor's final transcript, not this approximate list, controls exact wording.

This reading's purpose is access to a complete narrative while the first unit establishes nominal and elementary clause control. The independent mini-passages show what the learner can already read without those glosses. Units 2–8 then deliberately remove support by teaching the relevant verb and clause systems. Do not claim that the first unit makes all the reading's grammar productive.
