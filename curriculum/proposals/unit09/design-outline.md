# Unit 9 design proposal

This is a curriculum proposal, not the learning contract or learner prose. The coordinator must reconcile the source and grammar audits and save the contract before authorship.

The chapter is **Complements and reported content**. Retain every exact prompt of Cook Group II, Exercises 9–12: 16 + 15 + 17 + 16 = 64. The source audit controls their text, including the terminal comma in II.9.11 and all parenthetical or bracketed controls. Do not insert an unprinted “that” into II.11.9. Later source assignments resolve earlier deferrals without changing earlier contracts.

## Teaching sequence

1. Distinguish a demonstrative or relative **ðæt** from the conjunction introducing content. Teach finite content clauses after the selected knowing and saying frames. A statement presented as a fact may have the indicative. A report presented as another speaker's content commonly has the subjunctive; no grammatical claim makes that mood obligatory in every attested report. Perception receives its separately audited accusative-actor plus infinitive frame, without extending the content-clause assignment to every prior seeing verb.
2. Keep the participants and times explicit when passing from direct speech to a report. Select present reporting and one bounded past-report setting. Avoid teaching an automatic modern-English backshift rule or the entire Unit 12 tense/sequence system.
3. Teach dependent wh-questions and **hwæðer** “whether” with the selected matrix verbs. A question word keeps its own internal clause function; it is not assigned the matrix verb's object case. The whether-conjunction sense is distinct from Unit 8's inflected “which of two?” adjective.
4. Extend infinitival complementation by explicit lexical frames: **hātan** commanding an accusative actor to perform a plain infinitive, and perception with an accusative actor plus plain infinitive. Contrast those with Unit 6's same-subject modal complement and inflected purpose infinitive. Retain the infinitive's own objects and case government. Optional biddan/lǣtan systems are unnecessary here.
5. Teach the separately evidenced object/predicate construction and distinguish it from Unit 7's naming construction. Practice an adjective agreeing with the object, including a form that visibly distinguishes accusative from nominative.
6. Introduce the lexical and morphological systems required by the complete Cook sets, with explicit bounded frames for usefulness, benefit, refusal, preference, partitives, vocative adjectives, temporal date and the selected compound number. Full new nominal classes and verbal systems must be displayed before their tasks. Earlier forms do not prove new senses or government.
7. Give short original tasks targeting the central complement distinctions, then all four complete Cook sets. Follow with a connected controlled reading and the exact connected four-turn “Choice of Occupations” dialogue, Cook p. 131 lines 7–15, with the source editor's support and complete notes. End with a small cumulative application.

## Practice design

Cook's 64 prompts already provide broad translation and trade vocabulary practice. The proposal adds 33 purposeful tasks. Include identification of clause functions, contextual mood choices, direct-to-reported conversion, independent composition of embedded questions, actor/object analysis in infinitival complements, object-predicate agreement, a bounded unreal wish, new inflectional exceptions, reading comprehension and complete translation. The new u-stem, neuter -e and agent -end noun classes receive complete tables and meaningful use, alongside explicitly derived displays for nouns of prior classes.

Every literal learner input and expected output requires token-level form/sense identities; construction dependencies are explicit. Public task files contain literal inputs and sorted requirement sets. Ordered answer tokens, translations, alternatives and diagnoses belong only in the private models file. Recognition-only or supplied material must remain printed wherever a task needs it; it is not independently tested.

## Boundaries

The actual registers contain 328 lemmas, 4,876 forms, 371 senses and 322 constructions. Unit 9 may explicitly promote particular prior supplied records, preserving their original IDs, surfaces, analyses and supplied histories. **ðæt** content clauses, trade vocabulary, **mid** accompaniment and other larger Unit 8 source constructions do not become productive merely through repetition.

First/second personal genitives, reference instrumental cells, plural cardinal ān, defective verb citations, case attraction and reconstructed **\*sǣwa** retain their limits unless a specific new assignment says otherwise. Promote ēower only in the assigned “which of you?” partitive frame. Teach Cook's appositional **hīe sume** for II.12.10 separately from the permissible genitive alternative. The unreal nonpast subjunctive was recognition-only in Unit 7, so the independent wish in II.10.15 receives bounded new teaching.

Keep advanced causal/adverbial systems for Unit 11 and extensive sequence/voice for Unit 12. A single assigned Cook causal clause can receive a bounded construction or an exact scaffold without certifying every causal conjunction/mood. A contextual indicative or a documented variant can be valid Old English while not meeting a stated subjunctive exercise target; the key must say which issue applies.
