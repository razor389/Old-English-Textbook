# Unit 10 design proposal

This is the curriculum input for **The case system in extended sentences**, not a learning contract or learner chapter. The coordinator reconciles source and grammar evidence and saves the contract before lesson prose. Only Unit 10 is being developed.

Retain all **55** exact prompts of Cook Group III, Exercises 13–15: 16 + 17 + 22. Their identifiers, English, grammatical references and parenthetical controls come from `sources/audits/unit10-source/exercises-prompts.json`, independently checked against the two printed pages. Present each complete set together. No difficult prompt is omitted or deferred by this design.

## Teaching sequence

1. Begin from the familiar distinction between a noun phrase's own case and the case of its dependent genitive. Contrast a relational genitive with an object governed directly by a verb. Consolidate the earlier `geswīcan` and `gīeman` frames; introduce the specifically evidenced denial frame required by III.15.4. A recipient/person and a thing may be governed differently in the same clause.
2. Introduce the complete exceptional noun systems required by the source, with their stem alternations and selected variants. Other nouns receive the complete familiar model and their own diagnostic row. Do not bury the one form the learner cannot predict in a vocabulary gloss. Full new adjective systems likewise have an explicit cell display route, with substantive-person uses distinguished from ordinary attribution.
3. Teach bounded genitives of quantity: a selected part or amount and a genitive whole. Contrast an agreeing numeral or adjective with a substantival quantity expression. The independently productive scope must be justified by the grammar audit; it does not include higher numerals, free-choice indefinites, negative quantity or the wider Unit 15 system.
4. Organize dative uses by construction: recipient, beneficiary, the governed person with the selected verbs/adjectives, and experiencer in a bounded impersonal frame. Reuse earlier `helpan`, `fręmman` and `behēfe` only within their actual scopes. Teach the new frame explicitly; do not infer it from an English object or from a familiar infinitive.
5. Contrast accompaniment with means. Teach the selected `mid` plus dative means construction and the instrument expressed without a preposition. Deliberately promote selected masculine/neuter instrumental determiner/adjective forms from recognition, while keeping the merger with dative forms visible. A distinct morphology cell, a syntactic instrumental relation and a dative-governed prepositional phrase are separate facts.
6. Teach the remaining source lexical frames and essential bounded constructions before the complete Cook sets: temporal question wording, perception with an agreeing object description, naming the people described by an adjective, motion to a destination, and the assigned verb systems. Explain English future reference through the selected Old English present or supported construction, not an automatic equivalence of English *will* with volitional `willan`. Larger clause systems in III.15.15 and the exact reading receive local support.
7. Place purposeful original analysis, form-selection and translation tasks before the three complete Cook sets. Follow them with a short connected new account and the meaningful contiguous opening tableau of *The Day of Judgment*: the coming, enthronement, gathering and separation, ending before the king's speech. Retain the entire exact selected text and its source notes, with local support for untaught clause relations and source forms.

## Practice requirements

Cook supplies much useful translation but does not itself cover the planned case system. Original work must therefore independently test each new productive frame, including a governed genitive, quantity versus attribution, dative experiencer, means versus accompaniment, and a distinct instrumental form. New exceptional declensions and important verbal alternations need a substantive form task as well as contextual use. Do not add repetitive vocabulary quotas or a second set of translations that merely copies Cook.

The proposed structure is six morphology tasks, twelve short analysis/contrast tasks, eight controlled English-to-Old-English tasks, all 55 Cook prompts, and eight reading tasks. The exact original count may change only to close an identified dependency or coverage gap. Fourteen concise translated examples will illustrate the principal distinctions. Public learner inputs remain separate from private models and ordered expected outputs.

## Cumulative boundaries

All nine actual contracts and the actual cumulative registers were loaded; their identities and teaching scopes are retained in `prior-audit.json`. The starting registers have 400 lemmas, 5,770 forms, 455 senses and 365 features. No Unit 10 milestone exists yet. Earlier supplied or recognition-only records are promoted only by explicit identity and scope reconciliation.

Earlier instrumental forms and `mid` means are not already productive. Familiar `ðyncan`, `fēdan`, `be-foran`, destination `tō`, `dēorwierðe`, `cwic` and the source's temporal connectives do not acquire productive status merely because their strings occur in earlier readings. First/second-person genitives, reference-only `gehwā`, singular `manig`, higher numerals and reconstructed `*sǣwa` keep their limits unless a specified new contract entry changes one.

Do not expand the chapter into a full adverbial-clause system, tense/sequence system, future auxiliary system or indefinite/negative quantifier system. Local support remains printed with every task that depends on it. Independent tasks use only already productive or explicitly newly productive records; recognition tasks may use displayed reference forms without testing recall.
