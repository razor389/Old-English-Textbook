# Unit 7 design: subjunctive, participles, and passive

Pre-contract proposal, 23 October 2026. The coordinator owns the final contract. No learner chapter or answer key has been drafted.

## Prior teaching inspected

All six active contracts and both actual registers were inspected against the immutable `build/unit07-prior-baseline.json` at `47b92a4`: 275 lemmas, 3,487 forms, 305 senses, and 241 grammatical features. Earlier tables and supplied passages are not treated as prior teaching.

The ordinary finite systems, selected commands and infinitives, adjective inflection, personal pronouns and nominal cases are available. Earlier participles remain citations or supplied source forms. The passive use of *weorðan*, naming with two accusatives, function/purpose *tō*, spatial *ofer/bufan/under/tōmiddes*, and *nū/swā/ðus* require explicit new teaching. *Eall* is recognition-only. *Swīðe* “very” is already productive. Ordinary *beran* “carry” does not itself license bearing seed or fruit.

## Sequence

1. Distinguish assertion, second-person command, and wish/exhortation. Introduce subjunctive as Cook's “optative”; do not define it simply as uncertainty.
2. Give the complete present and preterite ending systems. One singular form serves all three persons, and one plural form all three persons (including dual pronoun subjects). Preserve every logical cell in the contract while printing compact four-cell lexical rows.
3. Work through the selected lexical systems in conceptual groups: weak I, weak II/III, strong I–III, strong IV–VII including contractions and j-presents, then preterite-presents and anomalous verbs. Name the productive roster. Generic formation rules do not silently promote every previously cited lemma.
4. Contrast direct second-person commands with first- and third-person exhortations, and both with capability, permission, obligation, and volition expressed by the previously learned modal verbs. Identical surface forms must be interpreted in their clause context.
5. Teach basic *gif* clauses. An open condition can be indicative; a contingent condition can be subjunctive. *Gif* is not an automatic subjunctive trigger. A bounded recognition example shows that preterite subjunctive can refer to an unreal nonpast situation; the complete past morphology is productive, but no general remote-condition translation formula is assigned.
6. Teach present and past participle formation, keeping the selected lexical prefix and principal-part stem. Optional `(ge)` citation notation is not a word. Present participles describe an ongoing activity; past participles can be adjectival or part of a passive. No general perfect/progressive auxiliary system is inferred.
7. Display complete relevant strong and weak participial adjective models, with all three singular genders and all plural cases. Distinguish this agreement from the source's verbal passive usage and its inflectional variation. Keep source spellings and analyses explicit.
8. Introduce passive *wesan/bēon* and *weorðan* with the selected past participles, in assertion, command, and wish. Teach the ordinary tendency toward state/result versus event without making it an absolute equation. Agent phrases are not needed for this unit's independent production.
9. Add the tightly bounded Cook vocabulary: complete *geweald* noun declension, productive *eall* and *ǣlc* strong forms, the five new weak lexical systems, and the required prepositional/adverbial frames. Show the exact meaning and case government before the mixed Cook work.
10. Practice forms in use, clause interpretation, voice changes, and controlled composition; retain all 34 exact Cook prompts and IDs.
11. Read the new connected account, then the complete fifth-day Creation paragraph, with exact local support and all original notes. Analyse newly taught forms inside the original text without silently teaching its relative clauses, result formula, or other supplied constructions.
12. End with cumulative diagnostics combining earlier object/recipient government, modal infinitives, agreement, and the new mood/passive distinctions.

## Display principles

- Four columns for each selected verb: present singular, present plural, preterite singular, preterite plural. The heading expressly says “all persons”; every logical person/number cell remains recoverable.
- Keep strong plural-preterite stems visible, especially contrasts such as *band/bundon → bunde/bunden*, and contractions such as *sēo/sēon*. Do not import the indicative second/third-singular mutated stem into the present subjunctive.
- The alternate *bēon* present is labelled separately from the *sīe* present; the shared preterite is shown without inventing a second past system.
- New verbs get full indicative and subjunctive systems. Any independently required command or infinitive receives its complete relevant pair.
- Participles are actual selected words, with formation notes and a separately defined syntactic scope. Modal defective nonfinites remain absent.
- Full *eall*, *ǣlc*, and participial models use nominative, genitive, dative, and accusative throughout. Earlier instrumental reference boundaries remain intact.
- The compact tables should be grouped under short explanations and followed by application. This is one unit covering several study sessions, not a single memorization sitting.

## Exercise and review design

All 34 assigned Cook prompts are retained. Approximately 46 additional tasks provide meaningful practice; their number is subordinate to their purpose. Mechanical formation is a minority of the original work. The remaining tasks require clause interpretation, explanation of agreement, active/passive changes, controlled composition, coherent reading, and cumulative repair.

The source reading is the complete fifth-day Creation paragraph, Cook p.126, editorial lines 5–14 / PDF142. Its plain *blētsode* is not silently identified with the earlier *geblētsian*. *Sǣ* in *ðǣre sǣ wæteru* is a genitive dependent on “waters,” not a locative preposition phrase. Original note numbers 4–21 remain preserved. Exact transcription, learner text, and change notes remain separate in the source editor's files.

Private model answers are separate from the task and composition proposals. The independent reviewer should solve from the learner text and contract first, then receive the answer key and private author material. A valid alternative may miss an expressly stated morphological target; the key should distinguish those judgments.

## Exclusions retained

No Unit 8 work; no independent relative or interrogative clause production; no general indirect speech, purpose/result clause, mood attraction, perfect, progressive, agent-phrase, causative-*lǣtan*, or *hātte* system. *Nabban*, *bregdan*, and *fēolan* receive no automatic new production milestone. First/second personal genitives, instrumental syntax, plural cardinal *ān*, and reconstructed `*sǣwa` retain their earlier limits.
