**Correct or qualify these supplemental meanings and locators:**

- **munan: intend.** Cook §134 p78/PDF94 says this explicitly. The proposal imported “remember” from **gemunan**, separately printed on p293/PDF309.
- **nēosian: seek; look for (+ genitive).** Cook p307/PDF323 and the actual ch10 discussion distinguish it from **genēosian: visit (+ accusative)**.
- **hǣl / ābēodan:** present the greeting formula **hǣl ābēodan**, “bid someone hail / give someone greeting”; **him** is its dative recipient. Cook’s note p176/PDF192 supplies the actual phrase; vocabulary independently establishes hǣl as neuter and ābēodan as strong II. Do not merge hǣl with feminine hǣlu or expose unrelated senses merely because they occur in the dictionary.
- **fēran:** the actual discussion is ch16 key591, Cook XII, XIII.33.3, `EX-XII-XIII-33-03`; the proposal’s Exercise34 locator is wrong. Cook p287/PDF303 gives “go, journey.”
- **fæc:** the vocabulary entry is printed p285/PDF301, not p286. Its separate reading references remain valid.
- **āhwæðer:** “either; one of two,” without unqualified “any.” **ǣghwæðer:** “each of two; both,” with Wright §471 p239/PDF259 as the exact full-form source. That page also explicitly supplies **āhwā: anyone**. Cook §89 lists contracted ǣgðer, not the full ǣghwæðer.
- **sīn:** Wright §464 p234/PDF254 explicitly supports “his; her; its”; Cook §83 supplies the reflexive relation. “Own” may clarify that relation but should not become an obligatory extra word in every translation.
- **wilde:** Wright §433 p213/PDF233 directly gives “wild”; p214 relates nīwe to the model. The p49 citation concerns nīwe’s historical spelling.
- **gelǣdan:** narrow the observed citation to “bring; carry.” **gelǣran** is named in the book only to explain gelǣred; inspected Cook pages supply gelǣred and lǣran, so do not add unobserved finite forms.
