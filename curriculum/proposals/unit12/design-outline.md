# Unit 12 design proposal

Unit 12 develops **tense, sequence, and voice** from the eleven reviewed units. The actual contracts and cumulative registers at `74da7bb`, not the archived experiment, establish prior teaching. This is a public design proposal; the coordinator reconciles it with the independent source and grammar audits and checkpoints the learning contract before either master is drafted.

Retain all **38 original Cook prompts**, Group VI, Exercises 19–21: seventeen, nine and twelve items. Preserve their exact English, parenthetical and bracketed controls, number indications and section references. Keep editorial directions and any local help separate. Exercises 19–21 stay complete; no item is silently omitted or reassigned.

## Teaching sequence

1. Begin with the two morphological tenses and the temporal relations a context can express through them. Reuse successive past narration and the already taught future reference of a present indicative. Contrast the forms actually written with the English tense appropriate to a contextual translation. A simple preterite can represent an event earlier than another past event; neither English *had* nor *will* automatically requires an auxiliary in Old English.
2. Teach the selected **habban + accusative object + past participle** construction with its active subject–object relation. Contrast ordinary possession with the completed-action/result construction. Give all six present indicative and all six preterite indicative auxiliary positions with the same explicitly identified object and participle. These are construction positions, not new lexical form records or extra inflectional paradigm cells. Explain the earlier event from a past viewpoint without turning a developing Old English construction into the entire modern perfect system. Selected bare participles are productive; documented agreeing alternatives are explained without silently assigning every possible adjective cell.
3. Revisit active and agentless passive clauses through their participants. The passive subject is the entity affected, and the finite auxiliary agrees with it. Contrast **wesan/bēon** and **weorðan** as context-sensitive state/result and event tendencies, not an infallible tense or aspect code. The new active habban construction does not make its accusative object a subject.
4. Add bounded recognition of **wesan + an intransitive past participle** and **wesan + a present participle**. An intransitive departure is not a passive merely because *be* and a participle occur together. A present-participle periphrasis need not require a modern English progressive. New recognition bases and prior supplied source examples retain explicit limits; recognition analysis does not test their independent production.
5. Develop tense relations in reports: presence simultaneous with a present or past saying, a completed action anterior to a past report, and an intended later action. Give the reporting stance and time relation in tasks. Distinguish a report's subjunctive from a claim of falsity. The anterior report uses the already taught preterite subjunctive **hæfde** with the new participial construction; its syncretism with indicative hæfde is explicit, and the twelve indicative construction positions do not claim to exhaust all moods. Past **willan** can report an intention without asserting its fulfillment; avoid a mechanical English backshift rule.
6. Present the complete new lexical systems required by Cook with their ordinary argument frames. New **fōt**, **brycg** and **hęre** tables must show every assigned case/number and the relevant stem changes. Other nominal or adjective systems require literal full displays or complete explicit earlier-model routes. New verbs need complete defined indicative, command or base-participle systems, with no syntax inferred from a citation alone. Higher numeral, negative, quantitative and source-only expressions receive either explicit bounded teaching or visible local support.
7. Move from diagnostic form work and interpretation contrasts to controlled composition, then all three complete Cook sets. The original work must carry the new temporal and voice distinctions because many Cook prompts chiefly exercise familiar finite clauses with additional vocabulary.
8. Read a short connected new account independently, then the exact Picts and Scots episode with its local helps and original apparatus. The source selection begins with the raids, includes the appeal, Roman relief and withdrawal, and ends with the renewed attack. It gives a complete event chain and an actual **wæs onweg gewiten** to interpret in context.

## Practice and reading

The proposed public inventory has **76 tasks**: all 38 Cook prompts and 38 original tasks. The originals comprise six morphology tasks, one compound-construction table, eleven analysis tasks, eight controlled compositions, four tasks on the new reading and eight source-reading tasks. Twelve translated examples establish the contrasts before they are tested.

The 53-word new account, *A house and a return*, has a completed building, arrival, announcement, reported intention, departure and return with food. It uses actual productive earlier vocabulary and the newly taught habban construction. Its tasks test connected translation, chronology, speaker/reference tracking and active/passive structure. Its full translation and all expected Old English outputs remain private until the independent solver freezes their work.

The exact source is Cook *First Book* pp145–146 / one-based PDF161–162, from **Ðā ongunnon twā ðēoda** through **and hīe mid ealle foriermdon.**, ending before **And hīe ðā eft sęndon**. The current image-checked transcription contains 155 words in two paragraphs and all 21 attached notes. A separate English context supplies the preceding Roman withdrawal; the numerically dense p144 narrative is outside this assignment. Preserve source spellings such as **hęre, ongēaton, ādrifon** and **Rōmanisca**, and distinguish original source notes from new teaching help. The historical ethnological discussion in the apparatus is retained as 1895 commentary, not taught as a present historical conclusion.

Source questions reconstruct the five main stages, track the changing plural pronouns, identify the three functions of **ðā** and the anticipatory **ðæt**, analyse the departure periphrasis, compare active/passive participant roles using the original note, interpret the reported instruction prospectively from the instruction, and explain the final crop comparison. Supplied frames remain visible during analysis and full translation. No Latin knowledge is assumed merely because an original note cites Latin.

## Prior boundaries and review

The actual baseline contains 560 lemmas, 7,481 forms, 663 senses and 507 constructions. Unit 7's passive and selected participial systems, Unit 9's reports and Unit 11's adverbial clauses provide real prerequisites. They do not establish every participle of a known verb, an unrestricted perfect/progressive system, general tense sequence, or a new lexical case frame. In particular **geworht** has a supplied history that requires an explicit new production milestone. Earlier **wæs gebiddende** and **wæs blinnende** remain supplied exact expressions unless a separately bounded new assignment says otherwise.

Keep prior sense and form IDs, analyses, milestone history and surfaces intact when promoting a record. Personal genitives, cardinal plurals, unassigned negative/indefinite systems, unsupported modal cells, higher numeral families, reference-only nominal plural senses and the reconstructed **sǣwa** retain their limits. The complete morphology displayed for a word does not confer every lexical or syntactic use.

The selected **gemētt** cell is present indicative third singular. Explain the preterite **gemētte** through its checked weak paradigm; do not copy the lexical-audit contrast note about an unassigned gemētan past participle into learner prose. No gemētan participle system or additional supplied citation is introduced by this unit.

The source editor owns transcription and lexical/nominal evidence, the grammar analyst owns verbal/construction evidence, and design owns the public sequence, practice inventory and eventual private key. Every actual public input and required private output must be bound to precise form/sense/construction records. Full paradigm cells must map to visible learner text. A form ID or a general evidence pointer alone does not prove that a dependency has been taught or supplied.
