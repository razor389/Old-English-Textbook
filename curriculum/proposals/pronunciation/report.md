# Pronunciation guide: curriculum analysis

Issue: `oec-pron-scope`. Independent curriculum inspection, 29 October 2026. The coordinator owns source reconciliation, the guide contract, shared indexes and completion.

The guide can stand before Unit 1. No full pronunciation system is already taught. All eighteen active contracts and both actual registers were parsed; relevant learning records and learner-master wording were inspected. The [analysis](analysis.json) retains input identities, exact feature boundaries, 21 supplied lexical candidates with source locators, reading candidates and five proposed practice groups. This analysis reuses checked lexical evidence; it does not claim fresh phonetic source-image inspection.

## Existing teaching and boundaries

The current totals remain **1,265 lemmas, 13,295 forms, 1,687 senses, 1,214 grammar features, and 644 distinct assigned Cook items**. No course or register file was changed.

Unit 1's `orthography` and `syllable-weight` features are recognition only. Its evidence record `FB2-4` identifies Cook §§2–4, printed 5–6 / PDF 21–22, and expressly excludes a complete pronunciation system. Later records for geminates, consonant contractions, the *giefan* palatal series and syncope concern bounded morphology. They supply useful spellings but do not authorize a general pronunciation rule.

Three alignment points matter:

- Unit 1 explicitly places stress on **-ginn** in **anginn**. A general first-syllable rule needs named prefix exceptions.
- Unit 4 discusses the two written letters **sc** in **scip** when explaining syllable weight. The guide should distinguish those two letters from their usual single sound; no Unit 4 revision is necessary.
- Preserve Cook's letters, hooked **ę**, macrons and undotted **c/g**. Any stress highlighting, syllable divider or added mark must be identified as a teaching aid. A long vowel, a heavy syllable and a doubled consonant are different things.

## Proposed scope and placement

Use a standalone guide linked before Unit 1, with its own contract and separate practice key. Keep all eighteen unit/register milestones intact. The guide teaches sound recognition and pronunciation; all vocabulary meanings, inflected-form meanings and sentence translations are supplied. It tests neither vocabulary recall nor grammar production.

The coordinator's selected working convention is **Cook spelling, Fulk's voicing and rounded diphthongs, and the traditional short vowels Fulk permits where helpful**. State this as a practical convention. Do not imply that every reconstruction detail in Cook and Fulk is identical.

Cover letters and quantity; simple vowels; the selected **ea/eo/ie** values; consonants, including contextual **c/g/sc/cg/ng/h**; **f/s/th** voicing; consonant duration; syllables and stress. Put fine historical distinctions, rare variants and technical terminology in brief endnotes. Approximately six to eight learner pages and a short key should suffice if the tables remain readable.

Use the existing Cambria table style. A useful three-column arrangement is spelling, plain instruction or American English cue, and a glossed Old English example. IPA is optional once when an unfamiliar sound is introduced. Full word transcriptions and IPA-memory exercises would work against the user's stated purpose.

## American English cues

Give ordinary words where they help, but label approximations accurately. A bare **caught/bought** cue is insufficient for Americans whose corresponding vowels merge with **cot**. The authors' primary research summary demonstrates both contrasting and merged American systems. Add mouth/lip instructions to make the cue usable. [Esposito, Arctander and Kinney, “Merger in Minnesota”](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/158th/esposito.htm)

For **bait/boat**, explain how to avoid importing the modern glide into a selected simple vowel. Distinguish vowel quality from duration: simply prolonging **bit** or **foot** is not a complete instruction for every long-vowel target. Fulk's source editor confirms that §19 permits traditional lax short vowels, supporting the coordinator's choice without requiring a theoretical detour.

For sounds without an ordinary American equivalent, give a concrete mouth action. A foreign-language word alone is not enough. Let the source reconciliation settle **r**; do not impose an unsupported compulsory trill. Aspiration of stops can be omitted from required beginner outcomes unless the source evidence makes it necessary. Fulk's IPA demonstration should not automatically become an Old English historical prescription.

## Practice and review

Five short groups can test the actual guide:

1. Distinguish printed vowel length and doubled consonants, then read supplied examples.
2. Choose the taught sound or mouth instruction for selected **c/g/sc/h/y** examples and give a short reason.
3. Apply the bounded **f/s/th** voicing rule to checked examples or pairs.
4. Mark stress and preserve syllables in previously displayed words, including a named prefix exception.
5. Read the supplied Cook sentence and identify two specified sound features.

The separate key should give the target sound in ordinary language, the reason, permitted alternatives under the chosen convention, and useful error diagnoses. It can check understanding of the rules; it cannot certify an unheard oral performance. The independent reviewer should solve these written decisions before consulting the draft key.

The curriculum task is ready for closure after the coordinator accepts this scope. Source-value reconciliation and the exact final word/task selection remain the contract stage, before prose.
