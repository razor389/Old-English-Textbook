## Accepted boundaries

- Teach the full personal-pronoun nominative, genitive, dative and accusative inventory, including both duals. Nominative, dative and accusative cells are productive. First- and second-person genitive cells are morphological recognition only until their ordinary use is taught. Do not suggest that **mīn**, **ðīn**, **uncer**, **incer**, **ūre**, or **ēower** can be prefixed unchanged to any noun: the related attributive possessives inflect with the strong adjectives in Unit 3.
- Teach invariant third-person genitives as possessors. Their reference follows the possessor; they do not agree with the possessed noun. Preserve the checked selected spellings and state the status of any displayed alternatives.
- Teach ordinary reflexive use of personal pronouns. Cook's **geręste hine** supplies an exact lexical and syntactic basis for self-directed resting. The adjective **self** and its inflection remain outside this unit.
- Teach complete affirmative present and preterite indicative paradigms, including second/third-singular stem changes and the necessary dental-preterite subpatterns. All four Class III verbs are explicit. Infinitives and participles shown as principal parts are recognition citations; their syntax, participial agreement, subjunctives and imperatives are not thereby taught.
- Present complete paradigms economically: four distinct present columns (1 singular, 2 singular, 3 singular, all plural persons), three preterite columns (1/3 singular, 2 singular, all plural persons), and an explicit statement that dual subjects take plural verb forms. Checked stem derivations can connect prefixed source lemmas to a fully displayed pattern. The contract still identifies every person/number cell.
- Extend Unit 1's supplied-frame subject/object/recipient work to independently inflected weak verbs. Teach simple **ne** negation. Contracted negative forms must have their own explicit scope; they do not follow merely from knowing affirmative **habban** or the copula.
- Keep the sonorant diagnostic personal and semantically ordinary: **ęfnan**, rather than turning the source's impersonal **hyngran** into an unsupported personal verb.

## Proposed learning order

1. Refresh noun/article case and the already taught copula through pronoun substitution. Introduce full pronoun tables, referent tracking, dual versus plural agreement, and the productive/recognition boundary of the genitive row.
2. Establish the complete weak indicative ending systems and the difference between a finite form and a nonfinite citation. Present Class I long-stem and dental patterns together, then the short/geminated and short **-ian** patterns; give exceptions beside their governing pattern. Present Class II and all four Class III verbs in full.
3. Use those forms in active clauses: personal-pronoun subjects and objects, giving with recipient and thing, ordinary third-person possession, and reflexive resting. Contrast present and preterite through changed event meaning, not only replacement of endings.
4. Add simple negation and mixed controlled translation. A connected composed account and short conversation reuse the actual Unit 1 nouns and the audited verbs. The conversation gives first/second persons and the dual a reason to occur; it need not introduce questions, commands, adjectives or infinitive complements.
5. Read the complete closing Sabbath paragraph, Cook FB p.128 / PDF144, with local support for its actual remaining constructions. Its six weak finite verbs provide a deliberate reduction in verb support after Unit 1; the other source grammar stays explicitly supported.
6. Finish with cumulative tasks combining person, number, tense, case, reference and meaning. The separate key must distinguish a grammatical alternative from compliance with a requested form or target.
