## Main grammar

Teach **ðæt** content clauses in selected knowing and saying frames. Distinguish factual indicative from the contextual subjunctive of a report; subjunctive does not assert falsehood. Contrast reporting now and reporting in the past with simultaneous content. Explain person reference and the bounded tense contrast without an exceptionless English backshift rule. Seeing plus a finite content clause is outside the new productive frame; the perception construction taught here uses an infinitive.

Teach dependent wh-questions and **hwæðer** “whether” after knowing or telling. Case follows the interrogative's role inside its own clause. Distinguish this conjunction from the earlier inflected “which of two.” Give useful clause-order patterns without converting them into a rigid universal formula. Explain that factual indirect questions can have indicative mood.

Teach **hātan + accusative actor + plain infinitive** for commands, **gesēon + accusative actor + plain infinitive** for perceived actions, and **gewyrcean + accusative object + agreeing accusative adjective**. Distinguish the earlier naming sense of hātan and the earlier same-subject modal infinitive. The infinitive's actor and its object may both be accusative; interpret their different functions from the frame and context.

Promote the bounded present unreal wish with preterite subjunctive **wǣre** or **hæfde**, already recognized in Unit 7, to production. Distinguish an unreal present wish from an actual past statement. Teach the dative experiencer and infinitive subject in **ūs is lēofre wician … ðonne …**, including parallel mid phrases; English “had rather” here does not assert past time.
