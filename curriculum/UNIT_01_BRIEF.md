# First unit brief

Status: candidate scope for source and curriculum audit; no learning contract or learner prose yet.

Build the first substantial unit around nouns, the article, and basic sentence structure. Cook's Group I, Exercises 1–3 are the initial source set: 16 + 12 + 18 = 46 original prompts. Exercises 1–2 have complete inherited audits; Exercise 3 has only an opening structural inspection. Verify it before assignment.

## Required design work

- Inventory every noun class and irregular pattern needed by the selected vocabulary. Introduce complete singular/plural paradigms for those classes, with checked endings, stem changes, and the neuter -u/zero rule. Determine which minor classes can sensibly follow in later units; record any resulting redistribution of source items.
- Present the full article paradigm and the basic case uses required by the unit. Explain agreement, genitive relationships, and the selected prepositional constructions.
- Give accusative and dative forms actual practice. Cook's first three exercises mainly test nominatives and genitives, so supply suitable checked new tasks.
- Plan copular and other early verbal groundwork sufficient for connected reading. Define the full paradigm(s) introduced and the syntactic uses taught. Do not assume the old course's supplied wæs, bēon, or participles are learned.
- Let vocabulary size follow the source and teaching needs. Keep required vocabulary before production exercises; distinguish reading support.
- Audit a contiguous Creation extract, including verbs, adjective agreement, case government, discourse particles, and clause structure. Prefer a meaningful connected span with transparent support over isolated clauses. If adaptation is needed, preserve and document the source wording and changes.

Use the active source registry, inherited audit index, and archived evidence. Carry forward correction C01 where applicable. Record genuinely new evidence for the additional paradigm cells and uses; previously checked nominatives do not establish all oblique forms.
