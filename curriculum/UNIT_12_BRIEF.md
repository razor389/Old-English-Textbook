## Tense, sequence, and voice

Begin with Old English's two inflectional tenses. English translations may require present, simple past, perfect, pluperfect or future expressions according to the situation. A simple preterite can denote an event earlier than another past viewpoint. The present can have future reference, as already taught. Do not turn English auxiliary words into an automatic translation algorithm.

Teach **habban + accusative object + selected transitive past participle** for completed action or present/past result. Print all twelve person/number positions of the present and preterite **indicative construction**, separately from inflectional paradigms. Bind each position to actual auxiliary, object and participle forms; whole clauses are not new lexical forms. Show finite auxiliary agreement with the subject. Use selected bare participle bases in production, while explaining that source usage also permits participle agreement with the object. The directly inspected Bosworth–Toller *habban* main and supplement entries support both uninflected and inflected forms. Correct agreeing alternatives are not errors merely because the model uses a bare base. Do not infer all causative uses, all intransitive *have*-perfects or full participial adjective declensions from this table.

Recognize **wesan + gewiten** and **wesan + sittende** within their assigned lexical scope. The former can mark an intransitive result or anterior event; the latter can emphasize an ongoing state/activity but does not require a mechanical English progressive. Recognition of these constructions does not require their independent composition. Keep these distinct from the earlier passive. Compare active clauses with agentless passives and distinguish a narrated event from a resulting state when context supports the distinction. Removing an active agent loses information; the task should say so. No new productive **fram** agent construction is assigned.

Extend earlier reported statements to simultaneous, anterior and intended-later time. Teach reference relative to the reporting situation, rather than compulsory backshift. A report with **hæfde … geworht** uses the explicitly identified prior preterite subjunctive form where that is the contracted analysis; the construction table remains labelled indicative. **Wolde + infinitive** may express intention viewed from the past and is not a general future-tense suffix.
