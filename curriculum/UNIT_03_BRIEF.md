# Unit 3 brief: adjectives, comparison and number

The third unit follows the reviewed Units 1–2. Only their actual form, sense and construction milestones are prerequisites. Earlier supplied adjectives, numerals and reading constructions are not prior teaching. This unit completes the ordinary adjective system, introduces agreeing possessives, comparison, bounded adverb formation and degree, cardinal numbers one through twelve, ordinals first through seventh, and the pointing uses of the demonstratives. It may occupy several study sessions. Finish it before drafting Unit 4.

## Teaching contract

Present the complete strong and weak adjective declensions across gender, singular/plural and nominative, genitive, dative and accusative. Display complete representative patterns with an explicit application table for the other vocabulary. Include short/long stems, **glæd/glad-**, syncope and retention, **-e** stems, consonant doubling/simplification, **gearu/gearw-**, and **hēah** contraction. Give the diagnostic forms and important exceptions together. Separate distinct instrumental forms as recognition/reference; instrumental syntax is not productive. **Eall** is reference recognition only here.

Teach attributive agreement, ordinary positive/superlative predicate agreement, weak ordinary adjectives after articles/demonstratives, and the always-weak comparative. Do not make the presence of a possessive a universal weak-adjective trigger: Cook says the weak form sometimes occurs there. Avoid testing an unintroduced adjective construction merely because its inflected form is available.

Give complete agreeing forms of **mīn, ðīn, uncer, incer, ūre, ēower**, with the possessed noun controlling gender, case and number. Owner number selects the possessive. Keep their records distinct from Unit 2's recognition-only personal genitives. Explain syncope before vowel endings and retention before consonantal endings, the special feminine nominative singular **ūre**, and supported alternative forms. Third-person **his, hiere, hiera** remain invariant.

Teach regular, umlaut and irregular degrees through **lēof, eald, gōd, yfel, micel, lȳtel**. Present a complete comparative declension and explain its application to all six series, including selected genitive plurals **lēofra, ieldra, bęterra, wiersena, mārra, lǣssena**. Superlatives use the already complete strong/weak system. Bound comparisons with **ðonne** to parallel copular subjects, with a nominative standard and an optionally unexpressed repeated copula. Selected adverbs have their own senses and forms; **wel/bęt/bętst** must not be confused with the agreeing adjective.

Teach full **ān** singular cardinal use; its plural forms are recognition/reference, not a count-one construction. Give all cases and genders of **twēgen** and **ðrīe**. Teach four through twelve in ordinary invariant position before plural count nouns and practise every selected number. Give ordinals first through seventh with complete weak inflection and the strong exception **ōðer**. Higher numbers, independent/substantival numeral inflection and partitive genitives are outside this contract. Give complete **ðes** and **se** demonstrative forms, retaining Cook's short-e **ðes**. Relative and general third-person uses are not implied.

Give a complete new **ðing** noun paradigm. Promote the counted-luminary sense of **lēoht** explicitly, with its complete heavy-neuter paradigm and four plural forms; its earlier mass sense and recognition-only plural cells do not establish this use. Treat source **heofonan** as the weak feminine form in its actual contexts, not as the prior strong masculine paradigm.
