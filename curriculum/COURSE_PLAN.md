# Course plan

Planning seed, 22 October 2026. This document proposes a course structure; it is not a completed syllabus or a learning contract. No unit has been drafted or taught in this repository.

## Decisions established with the user

The course assumes no Old English experience. Readers know standard grammatical terminology; an explanatory appendix can be added later. Its aims are practical reading and controlled composition, with the concise rules, paradigms, and exercises of a substantial grammar course. Lambdin's Gothic course is a model for teaching organization and economy, not a source of prose to reproduce.

Grammar determines the units. Present whole declensions and full paradigms together, explaining their endings and stem alternations. A unit may combine several Cook exercises. The previous rule of one numbered Cook exercise per lesson has been superseded.

Larger vocabulary lists are acceptable when the grammar, exercises, and reading justify them. Thirty words is neither a quota nor a ceiling. Readings should become substantial, connected passages with deliberate grammatical and lexical preparation. A short clause surrounded by supplied forms should not be the default unit reading.

Continue to distinguish source text, adaptations, and new teaching material. Evidence-backed corrections to Cook remain permissible with a concise note and a preserved original. Source citations belong in notes, without interrupting exposition. The existing LuaLaTeX work provides a starting point for eventual complete unit and separate key PDFs.

The fresh course starts with **no taught knowledge**. Archived lessons, vocabulary lists, coverage records, and keys are evidence and prior experiments. They confer no recognition or production milestones here, including for words displayed in their tables or supplied in their readings.

## Recommended first unit: nouns and noun phrases

The leading candidate combines **Group I, Exercises 1–3: 46 original items**. Exercises 1–2 cover predominantly singular and plural naming; Exercise 3 introduces genitive relationships. This is a recommendation pending the new audit, not a fixed unit boundary. See [Cook map](COOK_MAP.md) for source locations and verification limits.

Teach complete singular/plural case paradigms for the noun classes required by the unit, together with the full article, their case uses, and the relevant stem changes. Include strong and weak patterns and distinguish exceptional nouns. Giving each introduced declension a complete account does not require placing every Old English noun class in the first unit. The exact class inventory must follow the audit.

Exercises 1–3 principally practise nominatives and genitives. Add meaningful accusative and dative work: objects, selected prepositional phrases, and contrasts showing what the cases do. Introduce the verbs and prepositional government needed for these tasks before asking for independent production. Paradigm tables alone do not provide this practice.

Include basic clause construction from the beginning. A useful planning option is to introduce complete present and preterite indicative paradigms of the copula early, with subject agreement and predicate constructions. The final scope must be chosen together with the reading. Any further verbal forms supplied in a reading need explicit local support and must retain null teaching milestones until deliberately taught.

## Provisional broader progression

These are teaching blocks, not numbered units or exact Cook allocations. Some may require more than one unit; basic syntax and reading continue throughout.

| Teaching block | Proposed organization |
|---|---|
| Script and sounds | Letters, vowel quantity, pronunciation conventions, and the stem-weight distinctions needed for inflection. Keep the account usable alongside the first paradigms. |
| Nouns, articles, and basic clauses | Full relevant declensions, number and case, genitives, the copula, selected prepositions, and elementary sentence structure. Exercises 1–3 are the first candidate source set. |
| Adjectives, comparison, and numerals | Full strong and weak adjective paradigms with their conditions of use; agreement, predicate adjectives, comparison, and required numerals. Exercise 4 is a candidate anchor but has additional verbal demands. |
| Pronouns, verbs, and clause construction | Introduce complete relevant pronoun paradigms and organize verbs by coherent conjugational patterns. Teach present/past contrasts, strong and weak formations, principal parts, and frequent irregular verbs with the syntax needed to use them. Exercises 5–8 supply candidate material, not an established allocation. |
| Commands, wishes, and dependent clauses | Mood, imperatives, participles and passive constructions, relative clauses, complement clauses, and their characteristic word order. Place individual topics earlier when an audited reading requires them. |
| Consolidation through extended prose | Reuse inflection and syntax in longer connected readings and composition; introduce further classes and constructions in coherent units rather than as unexplained exceptions attached to individual prompts. |

Do not infer a full-book sequence from this outline. Later Cook exercises and reader selections have not been mapped comprehensively.

## Reading design

Choose a connected reading while establishing each unit's contract, then audit its vocabulary, inflected forms, and syntax. Decide what the unit teaches, what genuinely limited support can supply, and what requires a different passage or a declared adaptation. A vocabulary headword does not explain an unfamiliar inflection or construction. Do not label a reading graded merely because it is short or heavily glossed.

Full noun paradigms alone do not make the Creation narrative an elementary reading. The [verified opening](../reference/previous-course/sources/transcriptions/ch01/reader-I-opening.md) already requires a strong preterite (**gescēop**), copular past forms, objects, prepositional government, predicate adjectives, and discourse particles. The [darkness clause](../reference/previous-course/sources/transcriptions/ch02/reader-candidates.md) adds a genitive dependent within a prepositional phrase. Continuing the opening introduces passive constructions and a command or wish. Cook's introductory note, printed p.123 / PDF139, also presupposes personal pronouns, demonstratives, ordinals, **wesan**, **weorðan**, prepositions, and several particles.

A longer opening extract therefore needs an explicit verbal and syntactic plan. Alternatively, use a clearly labelled controlled adaptation while preparing the original. Neither the exact first reading nor a fixed word count is settled. The complete Creation selection, pp.123–128, has not been audited as one graded passage.

## Work before drafting Unit 1

1. Reconcile Exercises 1–3 and possible connected readings into a new form-and-construction inventory. Reuse verified source evidence, but audit the expanded paradigms and Exercise 3 from the selected images.
2. Set the unit's class inventory, verbal groundwork, case practice, vocabulary, and reading boundary together. Separate full displayed paradigms, assigned learning, supplied support, and anything unresolved.
3. Record a new learning contract and source crosswalk before learner prose. Preserve every original item identifier and account for any movement, adaptation, or deferral; do not silently lose material during regrouping.
4. Draft the full unit and explanatory key, then obtain an independent solve-through and linguistic/source review. Award this course's first milestones only from its own finalized teaching record.

The [archived dependency audit](../reference/previous-course/sources/analysis/ch02/dependency-report.md) and [source registry](../reference/previous-course/sources/registry.json) provide reusable evidence. Their old chapter boundaries, assignments, and mastery assumptions do not govern the new course.
