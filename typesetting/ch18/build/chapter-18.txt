UNIT 18

Independent prose consolidation

A connected narrative changes speakers, refers back across clauses, and
presents some actions as wishes, instructions or reports. Identify each
clause’s participants and time relation before joining it to the whole.
The final numbered Cook exercises form a sustained Joseph composition
sequence. The original Old English reading continues the banquet scene
with the princess’s gifts and the farewells. These are separate texts. The two
translated examples and lettered tasks are new teaching material.
Forms and help. A dictionary form identifies a word; it may still need
inflection. The complete tables teach their named systems and stated
meanings. Diagnostic stems are references to those systems, not
assignments of unlisted moods. An expression labelled Supplied remains
visible during the task: use its unfamiliar forms with the explanation given.
Its independent recall is not required. In verb tables, 1, 2 and 3 denote
person.

1. Clauses, reference and reporting

A noun phrase takes its case from its own clause. A relative expression can
have a different role from its antecedent. The particle ðe has no visible case
ending; identify its verb and complements to establish its role.
Se mann ðe se cyning geseah, sang.
The man whom the king saw sang.
The man is subject of sang, while the relative represents the object of
geseah; the king is the relative clause subject.
Se cyning hēt ðone mann ðǣm cnihte ðā bōc sęllan.
The king ordered the man to give the servant the book.
The accusative man performs the infinitive; the servant is its dative
recipient and the book its accusative object.
A third­person pronoun follows its referent’s grammatical gender, including
that of an inanimate noun. Agreement can exclude a referent without
selecting a unique person. There is no general nearest­noun rule.
In direct speech, ic identifies the current speaker. A report revises that
reference to fit its reporting clause. A preterite subjunctive under a past

2. Nouns and adjectives

Choose case from the phrase’s function: nominative subject, accusative
direct object, genitive possessor or licensed complement, dative recipient or
licensed complement. A preposition or particular verb can determine the
case.

Strong masculine nouns normally have genitive singular ­es, dative ­e,
nominative/accusative plural ­as, genitive plural ­a and dative plural ­um.
Strong neuters share ­es, ­e, ­a and ­um; nominative and accusative agree,
with plural ­u or no ending according to the stem. The selected feminine
oblique singular ends in ­e. Stem changes and weak assa are given
individually.

A singular­only table gives all four cases of the stated mass or abstract
meaning, using a familiar class. It does not claim that no plural is possible in
other senses.

3. Verbal systems

Each heading states the complete system added or reviewed. Indicative
tables distinguish present and preterite. Subjunctive tables give all persons
of the named tense; identical forms do not remove person distinctions.
Commands distinguish singular and plural addressees. Infinitive and
participle pairs add no unlisted finite forms.

A modal takes a plain infinitive. The inflected infinitive requires its stated
construction, commonly with tō . A base past participle may occur in the
specified passive or result predicate without a new adjective declension.
Meaning and complementation remain separate from the form.

175.18–20: seeing and falling in love
