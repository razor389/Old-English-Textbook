# First pilot brief

Historical brief. The subsequent user decisions supersede its provisional subset option: the completed pilot contains all of Exercise 1, and future lessons map one-to-one to complete numbered exercises. Use the current compact style in `EDITORIAL_POLICY.md`; goals and prerequisites remain in coverage rather than requiring discursive learner sections.

## Deliverable

One compact, complete chapter containing:
1. learning goals and prerequisites;
2. the necessary grammar;
3. new vocabulary;
4. worked examples and preparation;
5. a manageable selection of Cook's exercises;
6. a graded reading/translation section from the chosen *First Book*;
7. cumulative or chapter review;
8. a separate explanatory key;
9. an item-by-item coverage record and review report.

Use `templates/student-chapter.md` and `templates/answer-key.md`. Prepare `chapters/ch01/coverage.json` as the learning contract before drafting, using `templates/coverage.json` and the illustrative record shapes in `templates/coverage.example.json`. The example contains placeholders, not course content. Record every audited source item as assigned, deferred, or explicitly excluded; adaptations remain assigned items with a declared origin category.

## Scope

Start by auditing Exercise 1, including its exception in item 9. A small subset may be more teachable than all items at once. Record every unassigned original item for later use.

Select the reading only after identifying what the learner can understand. Prefer a small supported excerpt from reader I. Supply an explanation for any deliberately retained out-of-scope form, and make clear which features are only being recognized with help. If using extracted phrases, label them as such; if adapting sentences, label and document the changes.

Do not make the intact opening paragraph mandatory merely because it is the first passage in Cook. Conversely, keep the reading section meaningful: give a clear comprehension or translation task, not just an unrelated list of nouns.

The author may modestly expand the pilot's grammar if that creates a coherent reading, but must reassess workload and avoid silently turning it into an entire opening unit.

## Pilot evaluation questions

Can a learner:
- identify what they are meant to learn?
- find each required new word before the independent exercises?
- perform the exercises using only the taught material and declared support?
- distinguish word recognition from knowledge of a complete paradigm?
- understand how the reading relates to the lesson?
- learn from the key when an answer differs?

Can a reviewer trace every source item and every linguistic alteration?

## Do not infer completion

The starter contains no approved vocabulary or grammar milestones. Retrieve and verify the actual lexical information needed. Report the pilot as complete only after drafting, independent solving, revision, and a final coverage pass.
