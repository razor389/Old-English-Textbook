# Chapter 2: Plural nouns

<!-- Chapter: ch02. Independently reviewed. Source crosswalk: coverage.json. -->

## 1. Nominative plural

The nominative plural article is **ðā** for all three genders. A noun retains its gender in the plural.

<!-- example: ch02-example-01 -->

**se fisc → ðā fiscas**; **sēo wyrt → ðā wyrta**; **ðæt dēor → ðā dēor**.

Plural formation depends on declension, not gender alone. The following patterns occur in this lesson:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Masculine **-as** | **fisc → fiscas** |
| Feminine **-a** | **wyrt → wyrta** |
| Neuter **-u** | **nīeten → nīetenu** |
| Neuter, no added ending | **dēor → dēor** |

Stem changes accompany some endings: **dæg → dagas**, **hwæl → hwalas**; **fugol → fuglas**, **morgen → morgnas**, **tācen → tācnu**. **trēo** has **w** before the ending: **trēowu**. Nouns in **-nes** have **-nessa**. Learn **niht**, **męnn**, and **sǣs** individually.

**Neuter -u and zero.** The neuters in §2 belong to the strong declension. In monosyllabic strong neuters, a light stem—a short vowel or short diphthong followed by one consonant—normally takes **-u**. A heavy stem—a long vowel or long diphthong, or a short vowel or diphthong followed by two or more consonants (including a doubled consonant)—normally has no ending. Thus **gēar, dēor, sǣd** remain unchanged.

<!-- example: ch02-example-02 -->

Supplied illustrations, not vocabulary for recall: **hof → hofu**, dwelling(s); **word → word**, word(s).

Two-syllable nouns vary; a heavy first syllable followed by a light second syllable often gives **-u**: **nīetenu, ǣfenu**. Some lose the second vowel before **-u**: **tācen → tācnu**. Retain the forms in §2. **trēo → trēowu** follows a separate pattern despite its long diphthong.

The nominative plural is used for plural subjects and for the isolated phrases below. Coordinate definite phrases as before: article + noun, **and**, article + noun.

## 5. Reading and translation

A clause from God's blessing of the living creatures in the Creation narrative.

The following forms are supplied, not assigned for independent recall.

| Form | Meaning and analysis |
|---|---|
| **bēon** | be; present plural optative (subjunctive), here expressing a command or wish: “let … be.” |
| **gemanigfielde** | multiplied; increased in number. Past participle, masculine nominative plural. |
| **ofer** | over; across. Preposition. |
| **eorðan** | earth; oblique singular of **eorðe** after **ofer**. |

**bēon gemanigfielde**, “let … be multiplied”; **ofer eorðan**, “over/across the earth.”

<!-- item: ch02-text-01 -->

> and ðā fuglas bēon gemanigfielde ofer eorðan.

Using the supplied forms, answer in English:

1. What is to increase, and where? <!-- item: ch02-reading-01 -->
2. Identify the subject's article and noun; give gender, number, and case. <!-- item: ch02-reading-02 -->
3. Translate the clause. <!-- item: ch02-reading-03 -->
