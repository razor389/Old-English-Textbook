## Reading and declared normalization

Exact source:

> and ðā fuglas bēon²¹ gemanigfielde ofer eorðan.”

Learner display:

> and ðā fuglas bēon gemanigfielde ofer eorðan.

The extract is the final coordinated clause in God's blessing beginning **Weaxað**. The opening **and** is at the end of printed line 12; **ðā fuglas** begins line 13. These positions were checked against the printed marginal line numbers.

Declared changes: remove cue **21**, reflow the printed line break, and omit the closing quotation mark whose opening is outside the extract. Preserve every word, the lowercase initial **and**, all vowel marks, and the final period. The learner origin category is **declared normalization** for these display changes; there is no lexical or grammatical adaptation. **gemanigfielde** has no added vowel mark.

Four forms are supplied locally: **bēon**, **gemanigfielde**, **ofer**, **eorðan**. Their construction-level support is also supplied: **bēon gemanigfielde**, “let … be multiplied,” and **ofer eorðan**, “over/across the earth.” All four form milestones and both construction milestones remain null for recognition and production. The previously taught sense of **eorðe** does not establish knowledge of **eorðan**.

**bēon** is a finite present plural optative here: FB §138, p.78 / PDF94; the source's cue 21 points to §193(a), p.103 / PDF119, command or emphatic wish. It is not the identically spelled infinitive. The preceding **gemanigfielde** has note 19, “Past part. in nom. plur.”; the repeated form agrees with masculine plural **fuglas**. The glossary gives **ge-manig-fieldan (113), multiply**, p.293 / PDF309. The model translation was independently made from this wording: “and let the birds be multiplied over the earth.” English “let the birds multiply” preserves the sense.

**eorðan** is a weak singular oblique form, FB §53, p.37 / PDF53. Accusative and dative are syncretic, and [*ofer* has spatial uses with both](https://bosworthtoller.com/24000). No unique case is asserted or tested. Genitive has the same noun spelling but is not proposed as the complement licensed by **ofer** here. This analytical limit creates no unresolved learner dependency because the whole phrase is supplied.

The reading tests comprehension, the nominative plural subject **ðā fuglas**, and supported translation. It does not test independent conjugation, participle agreement, mood selection, or prepositional government.

## User-requested clarification: neuter -u and zero

Before handoff, the user asked whether the neuter ending could be predicted. The contract was amended before the prose revision: §1 now states the regular monosyllabic strong-neuter rule with a local definition of light/heavy stem; review3 asks why **sǣd** has no ending. Cook §4, p.6/PDF22, distinguishes long vowels/diphthongs from short ones and describes consonant-group weight; §47, p.32/PDF48, directly gives **hof/hofu** and **word/word**. These two fully glossed examples are supplied illustrations, not additions to assigned vocabulary, and their four forms have null recognition/production milestones. The rule itself has a recognition milestone; no unlisted noun production is required.

The prose distinguishes this rule from variable disyllabic forms and **trēo/trēowu** (FB §47.1, 3, 6, 7). [Rodríguez Ledesma (2022), §5](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1360674321000435) corroborates the traditional conditioning and substantial analogical variation, including **tācnu**. The historical tendency in longer stems is not a deterministic rule; the lesson retains its selected forms. This expands the exposition, not a correction of Cook or a dialect change. The updated chapter has 73 requirements, 2 examples, and 21 keyed tasks.
