## Define the knowledge contract

Before drafting each requested lesson, inventory the whole corresponding exercise and candidate reading against all earlier reviewed coverage. Record production and recognition outcomes, actual prerequisites, new word senses and forms, supplied support, and every original item ID.

The exercise determines the necessary grammar and vocabulary. Roughly thirty new words is a workload reference, not a cap that justifies omitting items. Keep the reader selection manageable by choosing a shorter extract or supplying explicit support. Do not silently treat a supplied form as learned. Record any unresolved source problem; do not call an incomplete exercise complete.

## Presentation and cumulative records

Use compact rules, paradigms, vocabulary, brief examples, and exercises, without repeated coaching. Retain the short orthographic orientation in Chapter 1 and assume standard grammar terminology. Keys may share explanations and variant notes across a compact answer table.

Update `grammar.json` and `../lexicon/entries.json` only after review. Mark exposure through a gloss separately. When revising an earlier lesson, use its feature and lemma IDs to locate affected later items.

## Later planning

Complete one requested lesson at a time. Preserve the full source crosswalk, including deferred reader candidates and declared adaptations. Audit the next complete numbered exercise before drafting it; do not write later chapter prose in advance.
