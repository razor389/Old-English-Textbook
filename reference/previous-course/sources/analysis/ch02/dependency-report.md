## Reader selection

Select the final clause of the blessing in Reader I, printed p.126/PDF142, printed lines12–13:

> and ðā fuglas bēon gemanigfielde ofer eorðan.

The source has cue21 on **bēon** and a closing quotation mark from the surrounding speech. Remove those two apparatus/punctuation elements and reflow the line; preserve every linguistic form, the lowercase initial **and**, and the final stop. This is a complete clause excerpt, not the complete original sentence. See the source editor's [candidate record](../../transcriptions/ch02/reader-candidates.md).

The production lesson provides **ðā fuglas**; **and** is already known. Supply all other help locally:

| Supplied form | Analysis and meaning |
|---|---|
| bēon | finite present plural optative/subjunctive, “let … be” |
| gemanigfielde | nominative plural past participle, “multiplied; increased in number” |
| ofer | preposition, “over; across” |
| eorðan | oblique singular of eorðe, governed by ofer |

Supply **bēon gemanigfielde** as “let … be multiplied” and **ofer eorðan** as “over/across the earth.” These forms and constructions retain null recognition and production milestones. The reading may test comprehension, the nominative plural subject, and translation with the support; it must not demand independent recall of optative/passive morphology or prepositional government.

The analyst independently inspected §138 p.78/PDF94, showing plural optative **bēon**; §193.a p.103/PDF119, explaining its use for a command or emphatic wish; p.126 n.19, identifying the preceding identical **gemanigfielde** as a nominative plural past participle; and the vocabulary entry **ge-manig-fieldan (113), multiply**, p.293/PDF309. The selected clause's **bēon** is finite, despite sharing its spelling with the infinitive.

The case of **eorðan** is syncretic. The weak noun paradigm and [Bosworth–Toller, ofer](https://bosworthtoller.com/24000) support both accusative and dative analyses for this spelling in spatial uses; the clause supplies no overt agreement ending that selects one. Record accusative/dative as the contextual possibilities, supply the phrase, and do not test a unique case. This is a transparent limitation of the parse, not an unresolved student requirement.

Independent close translation: “and let the birds be multiplied over the earth.” Fluent alternative: “and let the birds multiply across the earth.” The essential meaning is a wish or command that the birds increase in number. An indicative statement that they already have multiplied loses the force of the clause; an intransitive English **multiply** preserves its meaning despite the Old English passive.

The briefly considered darkness clause at p.123 actually includes **brādnesse** and a dependent genitive **nēowolnesse**, and has no **ðā** before **ðīestru**. It would require more support without exercising a newly taught noun. The source editor records this candidate, the two-lights candidate, and their deferrals.
