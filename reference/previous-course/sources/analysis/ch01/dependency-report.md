# Chapter 1 dependency analysis

Verified 2026-10-21 by the curriculum/linguistic analyst. This file records proposals and source evidence; it is not a learner lesson or an approval of the eventual chapter.

## Recommended contract

The user accepts approximately thirty new vocabulary items and assumes standard grammar terminology but no Old English. Assign all sixteen items of Cook, Group I, Exercise 1, with an explicit editorial direction to produce **nominative definite noun phrases**. This is a clarification of the standalone English prompts, not a claim that Cook printed case instructions.

The thirty distinct noun targets fit a small grammatical scope: lexical gender, the three nominative singular article forms, and coordination with **and**. Item 9 introduces the one required plural phrase **ðā ðīestru**. Teach the actual plural form before recall practice, and record its production milestone individually. It does not establish the ability to form other noun plurals. Do not omit item 9 merely because of a generic vocabulary limit.

The opening independent work may be organized into two study sessions without creating a new course chapter. All required words must still appear before the corresponding tasks. The empty cumulative registers establish no prior Old English knowledge.

## Source-dependent decisions

- **ǣfen** is neuter in Cook, and that gender is defensible. Exercises vocabulary p.46 / PDF54 points to First Book §47.7, p.33 / PDF49, where it appears under neuters. Bosworth–Toller’s supplement lists masculine and neuter and supplies an explicitly neuter example; modern scholarship also recognizes the variation. Retain **ðæt ǣfen** as the Cook-based model, and acknowledge **se ǣfen** as a grammatical alternative. This is not a demonstrated error requiring correction. Evidence: [BT supplement](https://bosworthtoller.com/37608), [BT main entry](https://bosworthtoller.com/391), [Vezzosi 2023, p.164](https://doi.org/10.36253/lea-1824-484x-14824).
- **trēo**, not an unannounced replacement **trēow**, is the selected headword in Exercises p.65 / PDF73. First Book §47.3, p.33 / PDF49, explicitly permits final **-w** in the nominative singular as an alternative. The target and alternative must not be confused with an invented correction.
- **gærs**, not silently substituted **græs**, is Cook’s grass headword, Exercises p.49 / PDF57.
- **heofon** is masculine; **heofone** is a separate weak feminine option. First Book §53.3, p.37 / PDF53 expressly distinguishes them. Selecting the masculine form does not authorize feminine article agreement with that same form.
- The article paradigm prints **se** (with emphatic **sē** separately noted), **sēo**, **ðæt**, and plural **ðā**, First Book §84, p.50 / PDF66. Preserve these source spellings, including eth.
- Cook’s noun-class references carry gender information. They must be followed to the actual grammar pages; a gloss alone does not verify gender.

The twelve noun entries for items 1–6, their exact scanned headwords, and their form-level proposals are in `curriculum-proposals.json`. Items 7–16 are assigned to the separate lexical checker/source editor; the coordinator must reconcile their evidence before drafting the complete lesson.

## Reading boundary and support

Use the unchanged linguistic text of the clause:

> Sēo eorðe sōðlice wæs īdel and ǣmtig;

Locator: First Book, Reader I, printed p.123 / PDF139, second sentence, opening clause through the first semicolon. Preserve the semicolon and label this as an extracted clause. Omission of footnote cues 4, 5, and 6, and reflow of the line break, are declared editorial treatment. Do not label it a complete original sentence.

The learner already has **sēo eorðe** and **and**. Supply these four actual forms, not just dictionary lemmas:

| Supplied form | Actual analysis | Help needed |
|---|---|---|
| sōðlice | adverb/connective here | “now” or “for its part”; Cook’s note 5 gives Latin *autem*. The general vocabulary also gives “indeed, truly.” |
| wæs | third-person singular preterite indicative of wesan | “was,” the linking verb with eorðe as subject |
| īdel | feminine nominative singular predicate adjective | “empty, void” |
| ǣmtig | feminine nominative singular predicate adjective | “empty, void” |

A close, independently prepared translation is **“Now the earth was empty and void.”** The two adjectives overlap in meaning; do not create a false sharp distinction between them. A good key accepts defensible synonyms and distinguishes the broader literal “indeed/truly” from the contextual connective reading suggested by the note. The familiar “without form” is a freer rendering, not the sole English meaning that a learner must reproduce.

Teach recognition of the subject and past-time state; leave the four supplied forms, predicate-adjective inflection, and conjugation as **supplied support**. A reading gloss must not become a production milestone. Ask for comprehension and selective parsing before full translation.

Evidence checked visually:

- Reader p.123 / PDF139, note 5 and note 6.
- First Book §138, pp.78–79 / PDF94–95: the verb and its singular preterite **wæs**.
- First Book §149 and §150, p.88 / PDF104: nominative subject and case agreement of a predicate adjective.
- First Book §57.3, p.39 / PDF55: relevant disyllabic adjective forms.
- First Book vocabulary **īdel**, p.301 / PDF317; **ǣmtig**, p.274 / PDF290; **sōðlice**, p.313 / PDF329.
- First Book vocabulary **and**, p.275 / PDF291.

The entire opening paragraph would additionally demand object cases, a prepositional phrase, genitives, plural verb agreement, passive verb phrases, and mood distinctions. Reading it independently is not an outcome of this lesson. The coordinator should record deferral for any such candidate source spans actually audited.

## Orthographic orientation

Keep orientation narrow and accurate. First Book §§2–3, p.5 / PDF21 verifies **æ**, the interchangeable manuscript th letters **ð/þ**, Cook’s preference for **ð**, and the historical distinction represented by **ę**. First Book §4, p.6 / PDF22 explains vowel length and the mark on the first element of a long diphthong.

A learner may be asked to recognize/copy these marks. This evidence does not justify invented pronunciation cues, replacing all **ð** with **þ**, replacing **ę** with plain **e**, or interpreting a macron as stress. No pronunciation task should require more than has actually been taught.
