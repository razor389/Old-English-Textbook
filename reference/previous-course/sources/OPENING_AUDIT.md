## Editorial analysis: what those facts mean for the project

These are teaching judgments, not headings or instructions supplied by Cook. The handling recommendations below reflect the agreed one-complete-exercise-per-lesson structure of 22 October 2026; early suggestions to split exercises are superseded.

| Material | Planning issue | Suggested handling |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise 1 | A first noun-phrase lesson needs an item-level number and vocabulary audit. | Completed in Chapter 1: all sixteen items, with thirty nouns and the required forms. |
| Exercise 2 | A plural lesson must account for the actual forms each word needs. | Introduce only the patterns used; do not imply one universal plural rule. |
| Exercise 3 | Genitives add both form and phrase-order requirements. | Teach the relevant construction and model it before composition. |
| Exercise 4 | Several different learning targets are mixed together. | Teach the required agreement, comparison, ordinals, and command work within the complete Exercise 4 lesson; audit before drafting. |
| Exercise 5 | Clause production depends on more than memorizing a verb list. | Audit subjects, objects, tense, pronouns, and prepositional phrases together. |
| Reader I | The original introductory selection assumes substantial grammar. | Choose and support a small excerpt matched to the current chapter. |

## First decisions

1. Keep original IDs and assign each complete numbered exercise to one lesson in source order.
2. Audit requirements before drafting explanations.
3. Track recognition and production separately.
4. Add the lexical information needed to use words, not merely English glosses.
5. Preserve Cook's source text separately from adaptations.
6. Use the complete Exercise 1 pilot to assess presentation and workload; later vocabulary and grammar scope follow the complete source exercise.
