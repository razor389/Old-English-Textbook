# Pronunciation practice: key

These answers follow the guide's chosen reading convention. The English words identify particular sounds; they are not replacement spellings for whole Old English words. The permitted American **r** and practical short-vowel approximations are acceptable. Different scholarly reconstructions do not make all sound choices interchangeable within these exercises.

## A. Length contrasts

<!-- answer: PRON-P01a -->

**A1. ac, “but”; āc, “oak”.** **Ā** in **āc** is long. Both vowels have the *father* quality; hold the second longer. Both final consonants are **k**. Do not replace **ā** with the English letter name **a**.

<!-- answer: PRON-P01b -->

**A2. is, “is”; īs, “ice”.** **Ī** in **īs** is long. Use *bit* for short **i**, held *see* for long **ī**. The selected practical vowels differ in quality as well as length. Both words end in unvoiced **s**, as in *sip*; modern English *is* does not determine Old English voicing.

<!-- answer: PRON-P01c -->

**A3. god, “god”; gōd, “good”.** **Ō** in **gōd** is long. The vowel of **god** is short and rounded, as described in the guide; **gōd** has the steady initial vowel of *go*, held without the glide. Retain hard **g** in both. Do not copy the vowels of the modern English glosses.

<!-- answer: PRON-P01d -->

**A4. full, “full”; fūl, “vile”.** **Ū** in **fūl** is long, with the *food* quality; **full** has short *foot*-type **u**. There is prolonged **ll** in **full**, whereas **fūl** has single **l**. Lengthening the vowel while ignoring the consonant difference does not give the complete contrast.

## B. Consonants and unfamiliar sounds

<!-- answer: PRON-P02a -->

**B1. cuman, “come”.** **C** is **k**, as in *kit*. It is a stopped sound, without the **ch** beginning heard in *chin*. The English gloss illustrates the initial consonant, not the whole Old English word.

<!-- answer: PRON-P02b -->

**B2. cyrice, “church”.** Both **c** letters have the **ch** sound of *chin*. Keep three syllables and the light final **e**. Short **y** remains the rounded-front vowel taught in the guide; the modern English gloss is not a complete pronunciation model.

<!-- answer: PRON-P02c -->

**B3. cēlan, “cool”.** **C** is **k**. This is a supplied exception to the front-vowel tendency. The following **ē** does not authorize changing it to **ch**.

<!-- answer: PRON-P02d -->

**B4. giefan, “give”.** Initial **g** is consonant **y**, as in *yes*. The following **ie** is the taught single-syllable glide beginning with the *see* vowel. Neither hard **g** nor the sound of English **j** is the requested initial sound.

<!-- answer: PRON-P02e -->

**B5. fugol, “bird”.** **G** is continuous and voiced: bring the back of the tongue near the hard-**g** position, leave a gap, and sustain the airflow with throat vibration. A complete closure would give a hard stop; omitting the sound would lose a consonant.

<!-- answer: PRON-P02f -->

**B6. scip, “ship”.** **Sc** is the single **sh** sound of *ship*. Do not pronounce separate **s** and **k** here. That separate pair belongs to the supplied exception **frosc**, “frog”.

<!-- answer: PRON-P02g -->

**B7. niht, “night”.** Use the front, unvoiced continuous **h** sound: raise the middle of the tongue toward the roof of the mouth and let breath pass through the gap. Both **h** and **t** are audible. The modern gloss does not license a silent **h** or a long vowel.

<!-- answer: PRON-P02h -->

**B8. wyrt, “herb; plant”.** **Y** is a short rounded-front vowel. Keep the tongue position of *bit* while rounding the lips as for **oo** in *food*. Do not pull the tongue back into an ordinary **oo** sound or make the consonant **y** of *yes*.

## C. Voice

<!-- answer: PRON-P03a -->

**C1. cnīf, “knife”.** Final **f** is unvoiced, as in *fan*. The preceding vowel does not voice a word-final **f** under this guide's rule. The initial **c** is also sounded, as **k**.

<!-- answer: PRON-P03b -->

**C2. cnīfas, “knives”.** **F** is voiced, as in *van*, between **ī** and **a**. This is the same written letter as in **cnīf**, now in a different sound environment. The final **s** remains unvoiced.

<!-- answer: PRON-P03c -->

**C3. rīsan, “rise”.** **S** is voiced, as in *zip*, because it stands between voiced vowels. Its spelling does not require the unvoiced sound of *sip* in this position.

<!-- answer: PRON-P03d -->

**C4. hūs, “house”.** Final **s** is unvoiced, as in *sip*. It remains unvoiced despite the preceding vowel. A voiced **z** is not the selected word-final reading.

<!-- answer: PRON-P03e -->

**C5. ōðer, “other”.** **Ð** is voiced, as in *this*, between vowels. The sound follows its environment here, not a distinction between the letters **ð** and **þ**.

<!-- answer: PRON-P03f -->

**C6. hǣlðu, “health”.** **Ð** is unvoiced, as in *thin*. Although **l** and **u** carry voice, this word is an explicitly supplied exception. Applying the ordinary surrounding-sounds rule without its stated limits would give the wrong target.

## D. Syllables and stress

<!-- answer: PRON-P04a -->

**D1. habban, “have”.** Two syllables; stress the first. **Bb** is prolonged: hold the lip closure before releasing into the second syllable. There is no extra vowel or third syllable between the two written consonants.

<!-- answer: PRON-P04b -->

**D2. fugol, “bird”.** Two syllables; stress the first. There is no doubled consonant. Keep the second vowel audible and the medial **g** continuous and voiced; dropping the unstressed **o** is not the requested careful reading.

<!-- answer: PRON-P04c -->

**D3. anginne, “the beginning”.** Three syllables; stress the middle one, containing **ginn**. Prolong **nn** without adding a vowel. The **g** is hard. The ordinary first-syllable tendency does not override this explicitly supplied stress pattern.

<!-- answer: PRON-P04d -->

**D4. gescēop, “created”.** Two syllables; stress the second, **scēop**. There is no doubled consonant. The prefix **ge-** is unstressed; long **ēo** remains one vowel glide within the stressed syllable. Two vowel letters do not create two syllables there.

## E. Cook's opening sentence

The supplied meaning is “In the beginning God created heaven and earth.” No independent translation is tested.

<!-- answer: PRON-P05a -->

**E1. gescēop, “created”.** **G** has the sound of **y** in *yes*; **sc** has **sh**. The prefix contains a light **e**; the stressed second syllable contains long **ēo**, moving from a held, steady *day*-type vowel toward **oo**. Keep the prefix vowel: **g** and **sc** are not adjacent sounds with no vowel between them.

<!-- answer: PRON-P05b -->

**E2. heofonan, “heaven”.** **F** is voiced, as in *van*, between the **eo** glide and the following **o**, both voiced vowels. Keep three syllables, with stress on the first. The rule depends on surrounding sounds, not on the modern English spelling of the gloss.

<!-- answer: PRON-P05c -->

**E3. eorðan, “earth”.** **Ð** is voiced, as in *this*, between voiced **r** and **a**. The rule is “between voiced sounds”, not only “between vowels”. Keep the initial **eo** glide and the second syllable; the single-syllable English gloss is not a pronunciation model.
