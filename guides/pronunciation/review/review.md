## Initial required repairs (resolved)

### PRON-R01: final doubled consonants

The learner’s §6 and key A4 require prolonged final **ll** in **full** and treat that duration as part of the contrast with **fūl**. The rule is extended too far. Cook §11, printed9–10 / PDF25–26, explicitly excepts the end of a syllable from his instruction to treat doubled consonants differently from modern English, then illustrates the medial case. Fulk §16, printed11 / PDF23, writes **ful(l)**; that orthographic variant does not license grading a mandatory final-duration contrast. The coordinator has independently re-viewed the Cook pages and confirmed the problem.

Required repair: make A4 a vowel-quality/length contrast without demanding a longer final **l**. Qualify the general duration statement so a reader does not automatically prolong final **nn** in **mann/anginn** either. Use **habban** and inflected **anginne** for the already supplied medial long-consonant practice. Repair both exposition and A4’s error diagnosis. Preserve the spelling and the distinction between written doubled consonants, phonetic duration, and syllable weight.

This finding concerns the selected practical teaching rule. It does not claim to resolve every scholarly question about historical final geminates.

### PRON-R02: anginne sound and main stress

The learner supplies hard **g** and middle-syllable main stress in **anginne**; key D3 repeats both. The current evidence inventory gives the actual Cook form and the prior course entry, but no noun-specific pronunciation witness establishing those claims.

Fulk’s actual page images give palatal **onġinnan** at printed19 / PDF31, **on-ġinnan** beside unmarked **ongonn/angunnan** at printed298 / PDF310, and **ā-ġinnan** at printed254 / PDF266. These are relevant counterevidence to an unrestricted hard-g generalization, but they do not by themselves prove the corresponding noun’s sound. The reviewer has retained and personally inspected all three page images in [evidence](evidence/).

Cook §23, printed18–19 / PDF34–35, does not supply **anginn** as a root-stressed noun. Its named noun-prefix exceptions are **be-**, **ge-**, and sometimes **for-**. The Cook auditor independently confirmed that the vocabulary’s **an-ginn** division contains no explicit phonetic or stress marking. The earlier Unit1 phrase “stressed stem -ginn” is a prior teaching statement, not independent pronunciation evidence; it might refer to secondary stress and cannot establish the guide’s claimed main stress.

A primary research lead is Melinda Mix’s 2007 University of Montana thesis, *Verb Contraction in the West Saxon Dialect of Old English: An Optimality Theory Account*. Its [official repository record](https://scholarworks.umt.edu/etd/1168/) confirms the author, title, date, and thesis status. Indexed text for printed6 gives the noun with first-element main stress, secondary stress on **-ginn**, and an **n + y** sequence. At this initial review stage the actual thesis page image is still being retrieved; the indexed passage is a finding aid, not accepted facsimile evidence.

Required repair: settle both the sound and primary stress from an inspected noun-specific witness, then amend the supplied reading help, stress exposition, D3 key and relevant contract/example records together. Do not infer the noun solely from the prefixed verb, and do not conceal the issue by deleting help while still requiring oral reading of the word. If the first-element stress is verified, distinguish it from secondary stress in any reconciliation of the earlier course statement.

### PRON-R03: position of the bridge sound

The **cg** table calls the affricate “the sound in the middle of *bridge*.” It is the final sound of that English word. Change the cue to “the final sound of *bridge*.” The intended sound elsewhere is clear; this is a small cue-accuracy repair, not a new consonant analysis.
