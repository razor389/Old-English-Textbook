# Grammatical-topic index proposal: pronunciation and Units 1–9

This proposal indexes actual learner explanations and complete paradigms. It is ready for reconciliation with the late-half proposal; it does not assign final page numbers.

It contains **279 entries/subentries under 77 main headings**, with 346 supported targets, 40 see aliases and 25 see-also records. All ten assigned learner components are covered.

## Evidence and anchoring

`index-early.json` gives each target’s component ID, exact source TeX path, unique whole heading line, source heading line number, master path/line, and a short actual supporting quote. Selected stable grammar-feature and teaching IDs connect the target to the cumulative register. The JSON also records all ten active contracts and the source identities.

66 unique unheaded explanations have exact TeX paragraph or table-marker alternatives. These matter especially for Unit 9’s dative of usefulness, mixed-gender predicate agreement and direct address after several adjective tables, and Unit 8’s relative-person agreement and case attraction. Keep the unique containing heading as contextual evidence; use the precise alternative where the explanation is far from that heading. No accepted text needs to change.

## Coverage choices

- All ten assigned learner components are represented. Core nominal, verbal, syntactic and pronunciation systems are indexed with reusable subtypes and distinctions.
- Every primary target is an existing unique whole TeX section/subsection heading verified against the source file. Supporting quotes are actual master substrings (ellipsis denotes truncation only).
- Some important unheaded discussions occur after long tables. Their exact unique TeX paragraph alternatives are reported for the coordinator; no extra heading or page number has been invented.
- Complete lexical paradigms are grouped under their grammatical class or irregular system. A full catalogue of verb or noun spellings belongs in the glossary, not this index.
- Reference and recognition locations are useful for lookup, but their scope notes preserve the contracts; index inclusion does not promote instrumental syntax, modal missing forms, case attraction, or a cited participle into production.
- Early duplicate targets are intentional only where they offer distinct user lookup routes, e.g. Accusative/object predicates and Infinitives/accusative actors. Coordinator may combine them with late-half entries.

## Omissions and reconciliation

- Exclude incidental exercise mentions, source vocabulary glosses and repeated answerable prompts. Index explanations or complete paradigms, including recognition references when explicitly useful.
- Unit9 adds many nouns, adjectives and verbs in familiar classes. Index its three genuinely new noun systems, extended constructions and agreement distinctions; do not append every lexical paradigm to the index.
- Earlier supplied-only content is not used as the chief index authority when its actual lesson occurs later in Units1–9: relative clauses, passives, wishes and content clauses point to their explanatory units.
- Do not turn Unit7’s supplied unreal condition into a general unreal-conditional lesson; later-half proposal should supply later systematic condition and tense coverage.
- The complete indefinite/free-choice and negative-pronoun systems belong to Unit15, not the limited Unit8 preliminary paradigms. Merge late-half entries under Quantifiers, Indefinite pronouns and Negation.
- The full instrumental and extended case syntax belong to later units; retain early reference-form anchors only as complementary morphology targets.
- The sound-history appendix is assigned to the late-half proposal. Its breaking, umlaut and ablaut accounts should be the main historical entries; present stem changes here are practical inflectional targets.
- Later word formation should own productive prefix/suffix classifications; the early prefix entry records lexical identity and prevention of automatic formation only.
- Add later full tense, perfect, progressive, passive, report, clause-order and discourse treatments to the existing headings rather than splitting duplicate synonyms.
- No final-book pagination is assumed. The coordinator must resolve actual pages after integration and rerun uniqueness against the preserved source TeX identities.
