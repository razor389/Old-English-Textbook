Most also provide the exact unique explanatory paragraph as a more precise alternative. The heading remains context; paragraph alternatives are particularly useful for Unit 10’s case distinctions and Unit 14’s suffix/prefix material. These are proposals for invisible anchors, not changes to learner prose.

## Limits and integration

- No individual index entries for ordinary vocabulary, every repeated lexical paradigm, Cook item IDs, exercise headings, task-control instructions, source apparatus or supplied dictionary headwords.
- Do not treat displayed forms as evidence of unrestricted syntax: the entries retain recognition labels for the intransitive perfect/progressive-like patterns, supplied scope for cross-clause fronting and the local limits of impersonal systems.
- Do not add an unrestricted past-counterfactual system, modern-English progressive tense, automatic future auxiliary, mechanical backshift or universal verb-final rule: the learner text explicitly limits these.
- Units16–18 repeat much earlier grammar; only useful additional explanation or distinctive later cases receive new targets. Keep the earlier teaching target primary after reconciliation.
- The late proposal distinguishes word-order fronting from sound-change fronting. Preserve that distinction in aliases.
- The kinship/root/participial-noun targets in Unit13 deliberately point to actual table headings rather than the earlier broad noun section.
- Figurative language is indexed only at the prose instruction about grammatical structure and context, not at the exercise titles or as a new exhaustive theory of metaphor.
- Readings retain additional locally supplied constructions, but most do not warrant separate general grammatical index entries. The supplied fronting analysis is included because Unit13 explicitly discusses its limit in the main lesson.
- Broad aliases such as Indirect speech, Perfect constructions and Suffixes should be reconciled with the earlier-half vocabulary of headings before final generation.
